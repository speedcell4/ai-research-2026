# Recursive Self-Improvement, Self-Evolving Agents, and Automated AI Research (April–October 2026)

*Survey note prepared 2026-10-03. Scope: arXiv submissions 2026-04-01 → 2026-09-30, plus ICLR/ICML/NeurIPS-2026-adjacent work. Evidence base: ~253 unique abstracts harvested from the arXiv API (my last API call was 2026-10-03; I stopped calling export.arxiv.org after the parent's shared-IP rate-limit notice), full-text HTML of six papers, abs pages for 29 candidates, and web search for venue/lab context.*

---

## 1. Synthesis: what is actually happening

**The loop is real, but it almost entirely lives at the harness/scaffold layer — not in the weights.** The dominant 2026 pattern is a frozen backbone model wrapped in an *evolvable harness* (prompts, control flow, memory, context management, tool use), which an outer optimizer rewrites, keeping rewrites that improve a held-out grade. AIDE² is the cleanest closed-loop demonstration: over an autonomous 8-day run it made 99 rewrite proposals, accepted 7, raised its incumbent grade from 0.703 to 0.778, and the resulting agent matched a two-year, human-engineered production research agent on four external benchmarks — including an out-of-distribution physics/weather-forecasting task. Mendel Gödel Machine and Gödel Forest show the same shape in coding-agent scaffolds (Polyglot 50.8% → 93.2%) and in *data-centric* RSI that actually fine-tunes parameters (average +10.70 pts across six RSIBench-Data tasks). This is genuinely a self-improvement loop with measured iteration-over-iteration gains — not a single lucky rewrite.

**But compounding/accelerating improvement is not demonstrated; the field's own best measurement says it is still bounded.** AIDE²'s "ignition test" — does the self-improved agent improve the self-improvement loop faster than the human-engineered one? — was *inconclusive* (0.780 vs 0.782 across three seeds, with the authors explicitly refusing to claim a win). Only 7 of 99 proposals were accepted, i.e. the loop's hit rate is low. AI4AI-Bench, which isolates the algorithmic-design level precisely because that is where compounding would have to come from (a better objective/update rule changes the compute→capability exchange rate), finds that across 290 cells the mean score is 0.166 and the best *configuration* reaches 0.288 on a scale where 0.1 = the repository's shipped algorithm and 1.0 = task optimum; 124/290 submissions leave the repo *worse* than baseline. The RSI survey of 1,250 papers concludes open-ended RSI "remains bounded by grounding requirements, collapse dynamics, and compute constraints on every measured axis." The Economics of RSI's back-of-envelope calibration reaches the same verdict: current feedback loops are not strong enough for self-sustaining acceleration, though they are strengthening.

**The binding bottleneck is evaluation/verification, not proposal generation.** Three independent strands converge here. (i) Reference-free self-reward/self-play loops are structurally hackable: on GSM8K with Qwen3 policies, a hidden-anchor audit shows the judge's pass rate rising 0.716 → 0.938 while true accuracy stays flat at ~0.20; the errors transfer across judge families and scales, and a strict three-judge ensemble still accepts 55% of them. Requiring the judge to commit to its own answer first before seeing the candidate collapses the false-positive rate from 0.719 to 0.012. (ii) Reusing a finite benchmark for repeated accept/reject decisions inflates promotions: REUSE measures up to 20.7% false promotions in current RSI-style promotion schemes and drives that to 0% with a certified decision-only-feedback rule, *without* losing true population gain (7.02–7.19 pts). (iii) The survey's "verification hierarchy" observation — formal verifiers strongest, intrinsic self-assessment weakest, and demonstrated self-improvement strength tracks the hierarchy — framing the human-in-the-loop research *direction-setting* bottleneck and the *verification* bottleneck as the same bottleneck.

**Other named bottlenecks: strategy lock-in, collapse, and scope.** Analyzing 1,338 frontier post-training trajectories, *What is Missing from AI Post-Training AI* finds only 2.1% of transitions between adjacent training runs change the strategy at all; agents revise execution but not the plan, and even a mid-run human instruction beats the agent's own continuation by up to 17.44 points. *ReSAIL* documents outright collapse in iterative agent self-distillation across deployment cycles and mitigates it (+22.5% final-cycle success on ALFWorld/TextCraft). The survey argues collapse is the *default* dynamic of self-generated-data loops.

**Automated AI research at scale is now a deployed reality with a modest quality ceiling.** FARS ran 417 hours, produced 166 papers across 67 topics (~$1,120/paper), and was evaluated by 282 structured reviews: mean overall rating 3.17/10, and only 11.4% of reviewed papers reached the ICLR-equivalent acceptance threshold at the paper level (and 14 of those 16 had a single reviewer). The AI Integrity Audit flagged problems in 27.9% of reviewed papers, concentrated in low-rated ones. Independent shadow evaluations — where frontier agents take on the central open question of an *unpublished* paper and the original authors grade it — had both papers unambiguously rejected despite agents completing all the engineering.

**What is hype.** "Recursive self-improvement" is being applied to prompt/context tweaks, one-shot self-refinement, and single-generation gains; roadmap/position papers (*The Last AI Built by Humans*; the "genuine RSI" agenda) are aspirational and provide no loop-closure measurement. Self-reported cross-system SOTA (e.g. an evolved small-model scaffold "surpassing GPT-5") is usually measured on a 60-task subset, against a leaderboard score from a different protocol, and with no weight updates — treat those numbers as directional. Also note that many strong results compare against a *harness* (Claude Code/Sonnet-5) rather than the same base model the loop started from.

**Who is publishing.** Weco AI (AIDE²), Analemma (FARS), NVIDIA Labs (SoL-Pi), Google Research (RRSI), and a heavy academic/startup mix: HKUST + evolvent-ai (Gödel Forest), LMU Munich (Mendel GM), Cambridge + collaborators (Red Queen Gödel Machine; cf. Cambridge's "Red Queen hypothesis" press), Tsinghua (What is Missing; MetaRSI), Shanghai AI Lab (iCoder, RSI-Master), HKU (HELIX), CUHK (AutoDataBench), SJTU, HKUST(GZ). In this particular harvest the *frontier labs* do not directly dominate the arXiv RSI literature; they appear mainly as the essay-framing (Anthropic's RSI continuum) and as the models/harnesses being optimized. Caveat: my harvest is keyword-driven and cannot rule out lab papers using different vocabulary.

## 2. Ranked list (15 papers)

### 1. **Recursive self-improvement of AI research agents (AIDE²)**
- **Authors:** Dhruv Srikanth, Bingchen Zhao, Dixing Xu, Yuxiang Wu, Zhengyao Jiang (Weco AI) — Srikanth et al.
- **arXiv:** 2609.26457 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26457
- **Venue:** Preprint (no venue listed on arXiv); project by Weco AI. *Read in full (all main-text sections).*
- **Contribution:** Formulates RSI at the harness layer as bi-level optimization (inner loop optimizes code against a task metric; outer loop rewrites the inner-loop agent), with public task signal separated from private held-out grading so the inner agent cannot optimize the selection criterion. In a single autonomous 8-day run on a 100-node trajectory it accepted 7 successive rewrites, lifting the incumbent private grade from 0.703 to 0.778 and adding a UCB1 strategy-bandit search policy plus bounded, failure-memory context management (per-call prompt size reduced up to ~50×).
- **Evidence/why it matters:** This is the strongest existing demonstration that an agent can improve its *own* research efficiency and that the gains transfer: the final agent matches/exceeds a two-year human-engineered production baseline on ALE-Bench, MLE-Bench, FML-Bench, and out-of-distribution WeatherBench 2, while reward hacking on held-out kernel tasks fell from 55% to 32% without being optimized for. Crucially, its "ignition test" for accelerating RSI was inconclusive — the most important honest negative result in the area.

### 2. **FARS: A Fully Automated Research System Deployed at Scale**
- **Authors:** Qiong Tang, Xiangkun Hu, Xiangyang Liu, Yiran Chen, Yunfan Shao et al. (Analemma) — Tang et al.
- **arXiv:** 2606.31651 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31651
- **Venue:** Preprint (industry deployment). *Read in full (main sections).*
- **Contribution:** A four-stage (Ideation → Planning → Experiment → Writing) multi-agent AI-for-AI system with a shared auditable workspace and machine-readable "experiment contracts." In its first continuous deployment it ran 417 hours, consumed 21.6B tokens (~$186k), and produced 166 complete papers across 67 fine-grained AI/ML topics (72 seed, 94 emergent), preserving all intermediate artifacts.
- **Evidence/why it matters:** Evaluation is unusually credible for this genre — 282 structured human reviews of 140 papers, with artifact-grounded integrity audits. The results are sobering: mean rating 3.17/10, only 11.4% of papers reach the acceptance threshold at paper level, integrity issues flagged in 27.9% of papers, and experimental insufficiency in 92.9% of reviews. It is the best available empirical answer to "what happens when you actually deploy automated research at scale."

### 3. **AI4AI-Bench: Benchmarking LLM Agents in Algorithmic Design for Recursive Self-Improvement**
- **Authors:** Yizhe Chi, Wenyi Li, Deyao Hong, Xiaoqiu Wang, Mingju Gao et al. — Chi et al.
- **arXiv:** 2608.20318 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20318
- **Venue:** Preprint. *Read in full (main sections).*
- **Contribution:** Ten frozen research repositories spanning ten training-algorithm families (SFT, agentic RL, on-policy distillation, reward modeling, DPO, diffusion RL, unlearning, graph diffusion, weight averaging, pruning). An agent gets 4 hours on one B300 to rewrite the training algorithm, then the code is retrained from scratch for up to 12 hours and scored by a hidden evaluator; every submission is also *classified by what it changed* (execution vs. learning rule).
- **Evidence/why it matters:** This is the field's sharpest skeptical measurement. Mean score 0.166, strongest system 0.250, best single configuration 0.288 (0.1 = shipped algorithm, 1.0 = optimum); 124/290 attempts are *worse* than baseline; only 122/263 changed the learning procedure at all, and those averaged 0.226 vs 0.126. Since RSI compounding must ultimately come from algorithmic design, this is direct evidence that automated algorithmic self-improvement is still far from closing the loop.

### 4. **Gödel Forest: Balancing Search Depth and Breadth for Data-Centric Recursive Self-Improvement**
- **Authors:** Ziqi Zhao, Fanqing Meng, Haocheng Lu, Lingxiao Du, Qiguang Chen, Mengkang Hu, Xiao-Ming Wu (HKUST / evolvent-ai) — Zhao et al.
- **arXiv:** 2609.36675 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36675
- **Venue:** Preprint. *Read in full (intro, method, results).*
- **Contribution:** Moves RSI from harness editing to *data-centric* RSI: each archived agent is a data-engineering program that synthesizes training data, fine-tunes a target model, and is evaluated downstream; a forest of persistent search trees explores the data space while an asynchronous shared memory (global leaderboard + distilled procedural "lessons") propagates failures and breakthroughs across trees.
- **Evidence/why it matters:** On RSIBench-Data (SWE-bench Verified/Multilingual/Pro, Terminal-Bench 2.0, GPQA Diamond, AIME 2026) it gains +10.70 pts on average with *lower* wall-clock on 5/6 tasks; an ablation shows shared-memory co-evolution contributes +7.00% over independent parallel search. Caveat to weigh: the official evaluation shares task identities with the selection evaluator (post-selection verification, not held-out generalization), and the +19 pt SWE-bench Verified headline compares an evolved Qwen3.5-35B against a Claude Code/Sonnet-5 baseline.

### 5. **Mendel Gödel Machine: Recursive Self-Improving Coding Agents via Comparative Evolution**
- **Authors:** Changzhi Liu, Yilun Liu, Sikuan Yan, Volker Tresp, Yunpu Ma (LMU Munich) — Liu et al.
- **arXiv:** 2608.07645 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07645
- **Venue:** Preprint. *Read in full (intro, method, results, limitations).*
- **Contribution:** Extends archive-based empirical Gödel machines (Darwin Gödel Machine / HGM lineage) with *comparative* self-modification operators: reaction-norm mutation (edit using an agent's trajectories across multiple tasks) and cross-lineage hybridization (edit using a reference agent's trajectory on the same task), both reusing evidence already in the archive at no extra evaluation cost.
- **Evidence/why it matters:** Under a matched budget of 200 evaluations + 24 expansions with a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B backbone: SWE-bench Verified-60 68.3 → 78.3 vs. HGM's 73.3; Polyglot-60 50.8 → 93.2 vs. HGM's 77.9, with the evolved scaffold transferring to DeepSeek-V4-Pro (96.9% Polyglot) and to SWE-bench Pro/Multilingual. The "surpasses GPT-5 with ~117× fewer parameters" claim is a leaderboard comparison on a 60-task subset and should be treated as directional; the limitations section is unusually candid about archive dependence and single-seed risk.

### 6. **The Red Queen Gödel Machine: Co-Evolving Agents and Their Evaluators**
- **Authors:** Alex Iacob, Andrej Jovanović, William F. Shen, Daniel Burkhardt, Meghdad Kurmanji, Nurbek Tastan, Lorenzo Sani et al. (Cambridge and collaborators) — Iacob et al.
- **arXiv:** 2606.26294 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26294
- **Venue:** Preliminary preprint / work in progress (stated on arXiv).
- **Contribution:** Makes the evaluator part of the improvement loop via "controlled utility evolution": search runs in epochs with a fixed within-epoch criterion, while the utility is updated at epoch boundaries, preserving per-epoch self-improvement guarantees under a non-stationary objective. Applied to coding (with agent-as-judge review), scientific paper writing/reviewing, and Olympiad proof writing/grading.
- **Evidence/why it matters:** Adds a code-review judge signal to beat prior SOTA on coding at 1.35–1.72× fewer tokens; co-evolved writers reach 1.78–1.86× higher judge-panel acceptance; an adversarial objective discovers reviewers equally strict on AI and human papers after finding the strongest baseline over-accepts AI-generated papers at up to 1.91× the human rate. It is the clearest formal treatment of the "who evaluates the evaluator" failure mode, though it is self-described work in progress.

### 7. **Recursive Self-Improvement in AI: From Bounded Self-Refinement to Autonomous Research Loops (survey)**
- **Authors:** Mingguang Chen, Licheng Wang, Bo Qu — Chen et al.
- **arXiv:** 2607.07663 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07663
- **Venue:** Preprint (44 pp. survey). *Read in full (abstract, intro, self-evaluation sections, discussion).*
- **Contribution:** Systematizes 1,250 arXiv papers (2024–2026) on two axes — what the system improves (deployment behavior, policy, evaluator, or the research process) × degree of loop closure (human-in-the-loop → fully closed) — with a dedicated category for self-evaluation. Introduces an explicit "verification hierarchy" (formal verifiers strongest → intrinsic self-assessment weakest) and catalogs failure modes (self-confirming loops, model collapse, diversity collapse).
- **Evidence/why it matters:** Its two headline claims are the field's best-organizing hypotheses: demonstrated self-improvement strength tracks position in the verification hierarchy, and the "research direction-setting" bottleneck is the same bottleneck as verification. It also quantifies the boom (74% of the corpus posted in 2026; ~500 papers in 2026 Q2), making it the natural map for anyone entering the area.

### 8. **Which Self-Improvements Should We Trust? Reliable Self-Improvement When Agents Reuse Their Benchmarks (REUSE)**
- **Authors:** Xiaojing Sun, Yuhan Zeng, Zihua She, Xiao Wang — Sun et al.
- **arXiv:** 2609.33180 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33180
- **Venue:** Preprint. *Read extensively (abstract, method, theory, results, discussion).*
- **Contribution:** Identifies why reusing a finite evaluation set inside an adaptive self-improvement loop produces false promotions — within-round selection, repeated testing, and *adaptive dependence* on prior promotion decisions — and proposes REUSE, a certified evaluation/promotion framework that returns only promotion decisions and allocates an error budget over all possible promotion histories. Theorem: for a user-specified α, with probability ≥ 1−α every promoted modification is a genuine population improvement.
- **Evidence/why it matters:** In live self-improvement experiments, competing frameworks (empirical best-of-K, SICA-style, PACE-style) commit false promotions at rates up to 20.7%, while REUSE commits 0% and matches the best true population improvement (7.02 pp at n=2,000; 7.19 pp at n=10,000). It supplies the missing statistical foundation for accepting self-modifications — exactly the "trust the grade" problem AIDE² flags as its central weakness. Scope caveat: demonstrated on a classification task with decision-only feedback.

### 9. **More Convincing, Not More Correct: Self-Play Reward Hacking of Reference-Free LLM Judges**
- **Author:** Chenyu Zhou — Zhou.
- **arXiv:** 2607.05904 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05904
- **Venue:** Preprint.
- **Contribution:** Shows that conditioning a judge on a candidate makes it score plausibility, not correctness, creating a false-positive basin that self-play exploits. Introduces a hidden-anchor audit (a held-out exact-match check the judge never sees) and a de-anchoring fix: force the judge to commit to its own answer *before* seeing the candidate.
- **Evidence/why it matters:** On GSM8K with Qwen3 policies, self-play drives judge pass rate 0.716 → 0.938 while true accuracy stays ~0.20; the hacked errors transfer across Qwen/Llama/Gemma judge families and (even at 3.5× scale), a three-judge ensemble still accepts 55%, and no plausibility-scoring defense closes the basin. Commit-first cuts the false-positive rate from 0.719 to 0.012 and, used as the training reward, keeps it at zero. This is the single most decision-relevant mechanism result for anyone building a self-rewarding or RLAIF loop.

### 10. **Can AI agents conduct open-ended AI research? Early evidence from two case studies (shadow evaluations)**
- **Authors:** Peter Kirgis, Sayash Kapoor, Andrew Schwartz, Stephan Rabanser, David Africa et al. — Kirgis, Kapoor et al.
- **arXiv:** 2607.27191 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27191
- **Venue:** Preprint.
- **Contribution:** Proposes "shadow evaluations" as a third way to measure AI R&D automation: an agent takes on the central, open-ended research question of a high-quality *unpublished* paper, and the paper's original authors grade the output. Two unpublished NeurIPS 2026 submissions were used, with frontier agents given six days and thousands of dollars of compute.
- **Evidence/why it matters:** A high-quality negative result: the agents completed all the engineering without human help but could not make substantial progress on the research questions, and both papers were unambiguously rejected by the authors. Five recurring failure modes are catalogued (misjudging the bar for publishable research, uncreative responses to shortcomings, ineffective backtracking, poor resource awareness, instruction drift), and a second model/scaffold reproduced them — evidence that current automated research is engineering-strong and judgment-weak.

### 11. **What is Missing from AI Post-Training AI: An Empirical Analysis**
- **Authors:** Joy Jia Yin Lim, Xin Huang, Hao Peng, Yaxi Lu, Xin Cong, Zhong Zhang, Maosong Sun, Yankai Lin (Tsinghua) — Lim et al.
- **arXiv:** 2608.19072 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19072
- **Venue:** Preprint.
- **Contribution:** Separates execution-level capability (iterating within a chosen training strategy) from strategy-level capability (revising the strategy as evidence accumulates), and analyzes 1,338 publicly released post-training trajectories produced by frontier agents.
- **Evidence/why it matters:** The strategy is locked in before the agent writes any code, and only 2.1% of transitions between adjacent training runs ever change it. Experience scaffolds improve execution (+12.6 GSM8K, +40.8 HumanEval) but not strategy; extra reasoning compute refines rather than revises; human pre-run review changes *which* strategy is locked in, not *whether* lock-in occurs; and a single mid-run instruction beats the agent's own continuation by up to 17.44 points under the same budget. This isolates the missing capability that keeps humans in the RSI loop.

### 12. **RSI-Master: Structuring Experiments to Guide Autonomous Model Improvement**
- **Authors:** Yaxin Du, Xiyuan Yang, Zhifan Zhou, Yujie Ge, Cheng Wang et al. (SJTU and collaborators) — Du et al.
- **arXiv:** 2609.35561 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35561
- **Venue:** Preprint.
- **Contribution:** Autonomous post-training across iterations via an "Experiment OS" that regularizes step-wise actions against hacking and maintains traceable records, plus Reviewer-Guided Research Orchestration that organizes Workers and Reviewers into a dynamically growing research DAG to avoid strategy lock-in.
- **Evidence/why it matters:** On PostTrainBench with Qwen3-4B-Base it averages 54.49 vs 46.53 for the strongest agent baseline at a reported 0.0% hacking rate; scaling to 35B it surpasses the human-developed Instruct model on LiveCodeBench-v6 (41.21 vs 37.36) and SciCode, and reaches a nonzero score on HorizonMath (unsolved research problems where most frontier models score near zero). The explicit coupling of anti-hacking regularization to the experiment loop is a concrete answer to the verification problem.

### 13. **iCoder-27B: Recursive AI-Led Development of Frontier Industrial Coding Model**
- **Authors:** Cheng Yang, Jiayang Lyu, Shangyuan Liu, Guibin Zhang, Jiong Lin, Xinlei Yu, Junchi Yan, Shuicheng Yan, Weinan E, Linfeng Zhang, Qibing Ren — Yang et al.
- **arXiv:** 2609.29626 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29626
- **Venue:** Preprint.
- **Contribution:** Pushes AI-led model development from "small models, bounded tasks" toward a release-ready frontier model. Human input is compressed into a high-density, low-frequency interface (reusable "research skills" encoding objectives, scaffolds, permissions, procedures); the agent then evolves data and coordinates SFT, on-policy self-distillation, and RLVR to produce a 27B model for RTL design and GPU-kernel optimization.
- **Evidence/why it matters:** Across seven benchmarks the resulting model leads RTLLM (outperforming GPT-5.5 and Claude-Opus-4.8), ranks second on CVDP and KernelBench L2 (+16 pts over GPT-5.5), and ties the best TritonBench result, with case studies showing competitive iterative optimization at substantially fewer tokens. It is the most concrete "AI builds a large, deployable AI artifact" claim in this window — worth noting that humans still set the objectives and permission boundaries, so the *autonomy* is in execution, not direction.

### 14. **From RLVR to RLSVR: Task Transformation Induces Self-Verifiable Rewards for Open-Ended LLM Self-Improvement (SpyRL)**
- **Authors:** Qinsi Wang, Jing Shi, Huazheng Wang, Kun Wan, Yiran Wu, Bo Liu, Qingyun Wu, Hai Helen Li, Yiran Chen, Handong Zhao, Wentian Zhao — Wang et al.
- **arXiv:** 2607.23802 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23802
- **Venue:** **COLM 2026** (stated on arXiv).
- **Contribution:** Extends RLVR beyond math/code by transforming an open-ended task into a self-verifiable proxy environment whose rules generate reward automatically. The instantiation (SpyRL) is a self-play social-deduction game ("Who Is the Spy?"): agents get asymmetric information, attempt the same target task, and vote to identify a spy; spy identity is predetermined so voting outcomes are fully verifiable while identification correlates with output quality.
- **Evidence/why it matters:** It outperforms existing self-improvement methods on non-verifiable tasks (summarization, creative writing) and improves verifiable reasoning too, making task transformation — rather than better judges — a candidate route up the verification hierarchy. One of the few papers in this area with a confirmed 2026 venue.

### 15. **ReSAIL: Mitigating Collapse in Iterative Agent Self-Distillation**
- **Authors:** Shengjie Jin, Hengbo Xu, Zelong Sun, YuJie Guo, Zhiwu Lu — Jin et al.
- **arXiv:** 2609.39306 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39306
- **Venue:** Preprint.
- **Contribution:** Documents that iterative privileged-information self-distillation (treating the student as the next teacher) causes *deployment* performance to collapse across cycles, and proposes ReSAIL: select interaction steps where privileged information most changes the teacher's predictions, balance distillation losses across trajectories, and regularize the student's privileged-conditioned outputs toward the frozen teacher to preserve next-cycle supervision.
- **Evidence/why it matters:** On ALFWorld and TextCraft, ReSAIL sustains gains across three cycles with +22.5% average absolute final-cycle success over self-distillation baselines, and sensitivity-guided selection also improves multimodal GUI action prediction on AITZ. It is the clearest direct measurement-plus-fix for one of the two collapse dynamics the survey calls default.

## 3. Honorable mentions (strong or important, outside the top 15)

- **HELIX: Model-Harness Co-evolution for RSI** — Tianyu Fan, Chao Huang (HKU) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13951. Typed, source-traceable harness substrate; one evolution round on code repair yields +4.0% fixed-harness coverage and 438 verified SFT/critic/filter/preference records from a 200-slot sibling slice. The clearest "harness evolves → data trains model → model motivates next harness" implementation.
- **SoL-Pi: Recursively Scaling Auto-Research Loops for Efficient Agent Harness** — Haozhe Liu, Tian Ye, …, Enze Xie, Song Han (NVIDIA Labs) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20519. Four harness mechanisms survive selection; 44.7–49.0% less token traffic and ~1/3 lower API cost at comparable EdgeBench performance. Production-oriented RSI.
- **RRSI: Regularized RSI of Agent Harnesses** — Peng Xia, Rujun Han, …, Tomas Pfister et al. (Google Research) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24972. Explicitly targets out-of-distribution overfitting in harness evolution; +14.1 pts in-distribution and +4.7 pts across five OOD benchmarks at 30% fewer policy tokens. Code at google-research/rrsi.
- **The Economics of Recursive Self-Improvement** — Tom Cunningham, Lukas Althoff, Basil Halperin, Brian Jabarian, Andrew Koh, Arjun Ramani, Phil Trammell, Parker Whitfill, Cheryl Wu — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15802. Formal feedback-loop models; distinguishes narrow vs. broad capability; calibration suggests loops are *not yet* strong enough for self-sustaining acceleration but are strengthening. Best quantitative skeptic's frame.
- **A Theoretical Framework for Self-Play Theorem Proving Algorithms** — Thomas Chen, Zhiyuan Li — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01861. Proves that a prover–conjecturer system with a reversible-random-walk conjecturer grows the proved-theorem set exponentially if the theorem graph is well-connected, and proposes a diffusion-based diversity objective to fix the observed drift toward artificially complex conjectures. Rare formal guarantee for self-play self-improvement.
- **Verifier-Backed Hard Problem Generation (VHG)** — Yuhang Lai, Jiazhan Feng, Yee Whye Teh, Ning Miao — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06660. Three-party (setter–solver–verifier) self-play to prevent problem-generation reward hacking; beats naive self-play baselines on integrals and general math.
- **AutoDataBench: Can Agents Write the Data That Feeds the Self-Improvement Loop?** — Haotian Luo, Haoyu Wang, Zeyu Qin, Huanjin Yao et al. (CUHK) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35025. Judged sample-by-sample against data-pipeline acceptance criteria; no agent exceeds 20/100 in 45 minutes. A useful negative pressure test for the "data production scales with compute" premise.
- **Redwood: A Frontier AI Accelerator Designed, Verified, and Deployed from Scratch in 2 Weeks by AI** — Architect Labs — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26418. End-to-end AI-designed accelerator (all blocks to 95% coverage; 3.4× perf/W vs. a measured Jetson Orin Nano baseline); "Qwen running on Redwood also helped design next-generation Redwood" is a concrete hardware-level RSI data point, though the claim is architectural/industrial and hard to audit externally.
- **Evolutionary Safety of Recursive Self-Improving AI: Taxonomy, Risk Discovery, and Evaluation** — Chang Gong, Jingping Bi, Di Yao, Xinjian Liang, Chao Xiang, Ruijie Guo — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31186. Names intent drift, error accumulation, experience contamination, safety-property erosion, evaluator drift, and risk propagation, with a five-layer taxonomy and governance principles. Best safety-side companion to the survey.
- **Learning Meta-Skills for Agent Harness Design in Test-Time AI4AI** — Cheng Qian, Kunlun Zhu, Beibin Li, Zhenhailong Wang, Heng Ji — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38143. A frozen Builder learns transferable "when/what to support" meta-skills; +8.95 pts over no-skill construction, +12.02 over direct delivery, with both roles played by the same model.
- **MetaRSI / RSI2** — Zihan Tan, Leixin Sun, …, Qiushi Sun, Shudong Liu et al. — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06396. Composes Data-RSI, Harness-RSI, and Model-RSI in one loop kernel with a meta-level schedule optimizer; the most ambitious typed decomposition, but validated only on code and closed-form science.
- **The Last AI Built by Humans: Toward Genuine Recursive Self-Improvement** — Yi Duan, Ying Liu, Zirui Tang et al. — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11873. A roadmap (execution autonomy → strategy autonomy → experience acquisition → environment adaptation → recursive meta-improvement) with a "Headroom-Closed Index." Useful agenda, but position paper — no closed-loop measurement.
- **SAEScientist-Bench** — Yuqiao Tan, Shizhu He, Jun Zhao, Kang Liu — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09113. ## 5. Bottom line

RSI in the April–October 2026 literature is **a real but narrow engineering loop**: frozen models with evolved harnesses/data strategies show repeated, occasionally transferable, iteration-over-iteration gains (AIDE², Mendel GM, Gödel Forest, RSI-Master). It is **not** a demonstrated compounding or accelerating phenomenon — the field's own benchmarks (AI4AI-Bench), economics (Economics of RSI), surveys (1,250-paper survey), and AIDE²'s own ignition test all fail to show self-sustaining acceleration. The decisive constraints are (i) verification/judge reliability, (ii) collapse and diversity depletion, (iii) strategy lock-in/direction-setting, and (iv) compute cost of evaluating each self-modification. The most valuable contributions of this period are therefore the ones that measure or repair those constraints — REUSE, the hidden-anchor reward-hacking result, the strategy-lock-in study, FARS's artifact-grounded integrity audit, and the shadow evaluations — rather than the ones that add another percentage point on a benchmark.
