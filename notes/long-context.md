# Long Context & Long-Horizon Memory — Survey of Apr–Oct 2026

*Scope: long-context modeling, context extension (RoPE scaling et al.), efficient attention, KV-cache compression/eviction, long-context retrieval & RAG at scale, agent memory systems, context engineering, long-context evaluation, and the long-context-vs-RAG-vs-memory debate.*

*Method note: discovery used a 6-query arXiv harvest (long context, context extension, RoPE, KV cache, context compression, agent memory; relevance-sorted, Apr 1–Sep 30 2026) yielding **520 unique papers** and **959** total arXiv hits for the phrase "long context" in the window; full texts were pulled from arxiv.org HTML. No relevance/citation counts were available (Semantic Scholar rate-limited; OpenAlex indexing lag), so ranking is mine and is explained per entry. All URLs are arXiv abstract pages unless noted.*

---

## 1. Synthesis: what is actually happening

**Long context has not cooled — it has changed shape.** Volume is still enormous (959 arXiv papers containing "long context" in six months; 520 unique papers surfaced by just six relevance queries), and it is now a *training*, *systems*, and *memory* problem rather than a "push the window bigger" contest. The advertised frontier has moved past 1M tokens (Claude Fable 5 is cited at 1M in one paper; one 2026 model card cites 2M), and single-needle retrieval at 1M tokens is essentially solved: in an independent 1M-token evaluation, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5 each scored 100% on 1M-token needle retrieval ([2605.02173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02173)). But the same study shows **multi-hop reasoning over 1M tokens still degrades**, and a large body of 2026 work (context rot, "proximity trap", lost-in-the-middle descendants) shows the *effective* context is far smaller than the nominal one. The new frontier is therefore: (a) **long-horizon agent memory and context management** (compaction/summarization), (b) **training recipes that make long context actually usable**, (c) **theory of why long context fails**, and (d) **evaluation that is not a needle-in-a-haystack**.

**Theme counts inside my 520-paper set (title matches unless noted):** memory 130; agent 128; KV cache 78; compaction/compression 86; RoPE/position 49; video/vision/multimodal 51; RAG/retrieval 41; benchmark/eval 27; RL 11. In abstracts: agent/long-term memory 109; context engineering/compaction 16; context extension 19. This is the clearest signal of where the field's energy went: **memory systems and context compaction now rival, and arguably exceed, positional-encoding work in volume.**

What is *real*:
- **Architecture and training choices, not just RoPE tricks, dominate long-context extensibility.** Controlled pretraining ablations (172k H100-hours, 26 matched 7B models) show that individually minor choices — QK-norm presence, GQA, sliding-window attention, 4K vs 8K pretraining — compound to up to a **47-point** swing in downstream long-context scores, invisible in short-context loss ([2608.10296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10296)).
- **Theory caught up and is now actionable.** RoPE is provably unable to simultaneously distinguish positions and tokens as context grows ([2605.15514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15514)); a companion theory turns that into per-head, per-task diagnostics and training-free rescaling that recovers up to +20/+25 points ([2609.39929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39929)).
- **Context compaction is now a formal object and a first-class research area.** Its minimum budget equals the one-way communication complexity of the induced query problem, and selection is provably weaker than generation by Θ(log n) in some cases ([2608.01326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01326)). In practice it is being *trained* into agents: RL over compaction gained +5.5–6.7 points on SWE-bench Verified / Terminal-Bench 2.0 and was adopted in the GLM-5.2 RL pipeline ([2607.05378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05378)).
- **Long context and RAG are converging, not competing.** The 2026 answer is routing/hybridization: entropy-based routing between retrieved chunks and full-context escalation ([2609.35831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35831)), and scaling laws that quantify how pretraining memory trades off against retrieval ([2604.00715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00715)). No serious paper argues long context replaces retrieval.
- **The ICLR 2026 "Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation" (LiC) line is productive and contested.** Follow-ups disagree on mechanism (intent/pragmatics vs. context weighting vs. model unreliability) but agree the failure is real and largely a **reliability** collapse, not an aptitude collapse.

What is *hype or thin*:
- **"Million-token" is mostly capacity, not capability.** Advertised windows are met by trivial single-needle tasks; multi-hop, aggregation, and reasoning at 1M remain weak, and most "1M-context" papers evaluate on a handful of synthetic tasks.
- **Agent-memory papers are a systems flood with weak evaluation.** Many are frameworks/prototypes; benchmarks are only now adding validity checks. DolphinBench documents outright defects in incumbents (6.4% wrong answers in an audit of LoCoMo; ground-truth errors in LongMemEval) and notes most benchmarks don't certify that a test is solvable *with* memory and unsolvable *without* it ([2609.24971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24971)).
- **KV-cache compression wins are often small and error bars overlap.** The leading theory paper's own LongBench-v2 "long" experiment is underpowered: 37.04% ± 4.65 (full KV) vs 34.26–37.04% for compressed variants ([2607.01520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01520)). Compression is real engineering, but headline accuracy claims deserve skepticism.
- **Context compaction has a safety cost that was not on the radar.** Compaction silently deletes in-context governance constraints, raising violation rates from 0% to 30% (up to 59% for some models) ([2606.22528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22528)). This is the most consequential critique of the "compact and continue" paradigm.

**Answers to the key questions.** (1) Still hot, but the frontier is agent memory / compaction / training-and-architecture, with 1M+ now table stakes and long *video* + multimodal the expanding edge (51/520 papers touch video/vision/multimodal). (2) Leading methods: per-head RoPE (AdaRoPE), hybrid/linear architectures (Gated DeltaNet, HyLo upcycling), trained compaction (CompactionRL), test-time training, and hybrid routing; leading benchmarks: RULER/HELMET/LongBench-v2 plus new difficulty-controlled (PredicateLongBench), action-based (DolphinBench), safety (LongPIBench) and multimodal (SynthDocBench) suites. Critiques: short-context metrics don't predict long-context ability; NIAH is saturated; single-needle ≠ reasoning. (3) Long context, memory, and RAG are complementary, with routing/scaling-law work replacing the earlier binary debate. (4) Yes — models degrade on long-horizon multi-turn tasks; the LiC paper won an ICLR 2026 Outstanding Paper and has directly spawned mechanistic debate and mitigation work.

## 2. Ranked papers (15)

### 1. Cracks in the Foundation: Seemingly Minor Architectural Choices Impact Long Context Extension
**Amanda Bertsch, Luca Soldaini, Matthew R. Gormley, Graham Neubig, Hannaneh Hajishirzi, Kyle Lo, Dirk Groeneveld** — arXiv [2608.10296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10296) — **COLM 2026** (per authors).
This is the decade's most useful controlled negative result for long context. Holding data, tokenizer, extension recipe and optimization fixed, the authors train **26 matched 7B models (OlmPool, ~172,000 H100-hours)** that vary only four decisions used somewhere in the Olmo/Llama/Qwen families: QK-norm, GQA, sliding-window attention, and pretraining context length. Any one choice is minor; combining three or more drops HELMET-32K by up to **26.5–47 points**, and a simple count of "long-context-inhibiting features" is the single best predictor (in-sample R²=0.67, LOO R²=0.61). Crucially the damage is invisible in short-context loss, perplexity, or in-loop benchmarks (R²≤0.39), and 50B-token extensions do not wash it out. It releases all checkpoints, and shows Llama-3 is a particularly extensible architecture for architectural (not data) reasons.
**Why it matters:** practitioners committing to architecture before the long-context phase can now see that "minor" choices are decisive; existing RoPE-scaling recipes validated on Llama may not transfer.

### 2. RoPE at the End of Its Rope? Theory, Diagnosis, and Mitigation of Long-Context Failures
**Yuyang Wu, Yufeng Du, Hao Peng** — arXiv [2609.39929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39929) — preprint (Sep 2026).
A finite-window theory of RoPE that permits unequal query/key coefficient magnitudes across frequencies — closer to trained models than prior distributional assumptions. It formalizes two failure modes, **semantic reversal** (position shift flips which key wins) and **positional insensitivity** (adjacent positions become indistinguishable), shows both are governed by the high-frequency *norm share*, and proves a context-length bound beyond which no fixed attention-score margin can avoid both. It then introduces **RoPE Profiler**, which reuses cached Q/K activations (zero extra forward passes) to score every head, and uses the diagnosis to apply targeted training-free high-frequency rescaling. Across **49 long-context task settings** and two models, reasoning tasks cluster as semantic-reversal-limited while retrieval tasks are positionally limited (cross-model Spearman ρ=0.658), and targeted interventions improve accuracy on ~63–65% of settings, by up to **+20 points (Qwen3-8B)** and **+25 points (Llama-3.1-8B)**.
**Why it matters:** it converts RoPE-is-limited pessimism into a practical diagnostic + intervention pipeline; the honest caveat is that the headline gains come from an exploratory per-task intervention search ("best observed"), so the diagnostic ranking is stronger evidence than the peak numbers.

### 3. Context Compaction Theory
**Hayder Tirmazi, Sam Markelon, Allison Bishop, Michael Mitzenmacher** — arXiv [2608.01326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01326) — preprint.
The first formal treatment of the operation every long-horizon agent now runs. It models compaction as two games — **Context Selection** (keep a subset) and **Context Generation** (emit an arbitrary bounded message) — and proves that the generation game is *equivalent to one-way communication complexity*: the minimum compaction budget to answer a query family within error ε equals the one-way communication complexity of the induced problem. Selection is a restricted protocol class, and there exist query families where generation needs Θ(log n) less budget. A case study feeds 15,000 items to a production Anthropic compaction endpoint and finds it answers set-membership queries at close to **random-guess error**, while a Bloom filter of the same size is near-optimal — direct evidence that surviving summaries lose information they in principle need not.
**Why it matters:** it gives agent designers a calculable lower bound on what compaction must retain (e.g. Ω(Nm) bits for dependency/CVE set-disjointness) and reframes context engineering as an information-theoretic design problem.

### 4. CompactionRL: Reinforcement Learning with Context Compaction for Long-Horizon Agents
**Yujiang Li, Zhenyu Hou, Yi Jing, Jie Tang, Yuxiao Dong** — arXiv [2607.05378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05378) — preprint.
Instead of treating compaction as an inference-time heuristic, this work folds summarization into the RL rollout: when the budget is exhausted the agent resumes from a learned summary, and the summarizer is optimized jointly with task execution under a shared task reward. Two technical fixes make this trainable — token-level loss normalization for the severe execution/summary length imbalance, and **cross-segment generalized advantage estimation** so the terminal reward is not artificially shortened at compaction boundaries (single-rollout PPO avoids the group-wait problem). On agentic coding it lifts GLM-4.5-Air by **+6.6 (SWE-bench Verified, 66.4% Pass@1) and +4.9 (Terminal-Bench 2.0, 26.2%)** over inference-time compaction, and GLM-4.7-Flash by **+5.5 / +6.7**, with consistent ablations on PPO, token-level loss and cross-segment GAE.
**Why it matters:** the strongest demonstrated long-horizon-memory gain in the window and evidence the technique has moved into frontier training (deployed in the GLM-5.2 RL pipeline). The headline is capability, not a new benchmark.

### 5. Is Agent Memory a Database? Rethinking Data Foundations for Long-Term AI Agent Memory
**Abdelghny Orogat, Essam Mansour** — arXiv [2605.26252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26252) — preprint (vision/position, with prototype).
This paper argues that long-term agent memory is a **new data-management workload**, not a storage problem, and formalizes **Governed Evolving Memory (GEM)**: a state M_t=(D_t,S_t,P_t) evolved by four state-level operators (ingestion, revision, forgetting, retrieval) under six correctness conditions. It audits five database paradigms (relational, KV/document, RDF/property graph, temporal, vector) and five agent-memory families (tiered/MemGPT, fact-extraction/Mem0, graph/Zep, consolidation/MIRIX, RL-driven) and shows none supports all four needed capabilities; three structural observations argue no record-level system can meet the conditions because retrieval must itself mutate state. A property-graph prototype (MemState) shows feasibility.
**Why it matters:** it is the clearest framing paper for a subfield that otherwise consists of ad-hoc systems, and it names concrete failure modes (unregulated growth, missing semantic revision, capacity-driven forgetting, read-only retrieval). Evidence is conceptual + prototype, not benchmark wins — treat it as a research agenda, not a result.

### 6. DolphinBench: Mapping the Pareto Frontier of Agent Memory
**Soumil Rathi, Deshraj Yadav, Taranjeet Singh (Mem0)** — arXiv [2609.24971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24971) — preprint.
A memory benchmark that fixes three things at once: it evaluates **action/task completion** rather than question-answering (a QA question leaks that retrieval is needed and which fact matters), it **requires accuracy, dollar cost and median latency** in every submission, and it **verifies each test with an agent given the relevant history (must pass) and without it (must fail)**. It has 3 knowledge-work personas, ~500k tokens of user history each, and 600 tests. Best configuration reaches **70.67%** task success; cost and latency rankings differ from accuracy rankings (e.g. Mem0 improves accuracy and cuts latency while raising cost).
**Why it matters:** it is a direct critique of the memory-benchmark ecosystem (citing a 6.4% error rate in LoCoMo and ground-truth errors in LongMemEval) and the only benchmark in the set that treats memory as a cost/latency-constrained engineering problem. Caveat: authored by a memory vendor (Mem0) and evaluated on synthetic histories, so numbers should be read as relative, not absolute.

### 7. The risk of KV cache compression
**Lukas Haverbeck, Carmen Amo Alonso, Andres Felipe Posada-Moreno, Sebastian Trimpe, Marco Pavone** — arXiv [2607.01520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01520) — preprint.
Recasts KV-cache compression as **sparse approximation of a probability measure** (strictly generalizing token eviction to sparse reweighting) and characterizes its **minimax risk** in terms of a data-dependent complexity measure — the response profile of the cache under the future-query distribution. It gives matching upper and lower bounds, a sharp separation between query-aware and query-agnostic compressors ("spectral barrier"), and design principles that map to causal prefill + autoregressive decoding. Instantiated in a concrete algorithm evaluated on the "long" subset of **LongBench-v2 with Qwen3-32B** at ~95% compression.
**Why it matters:** it is the reference theory for a very crowded empirical area and tells designers which caches can be compressed at all. The empirical claim is deliberately weak (accuracy 34.26–37.04% with overlapping ±4.6 standard errors vs a 37.04% full-KV baseline) — cite the theory, not the benchmark result.

### 8. Governance Decay: How Context Compaction Silently Erases Safety Constraints in Long-Horizon LLM Agents
**Shiyang Chen** — arXiv [2606.22528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22528) — preprint.
Shows that context compaction is a **safety-critical** surface: in-context governance constraints (org policies, workspace memory, standing instructions) that agents reliably obey are treated as low-salience and dropped by summarizers, after which the same model performs the prohibited action. With **ConstraintRot** (deterministic tool-call grading) over 7 model families and 1,323 episodes, compaction raises violation from **0% to 30% on average (up to 59%)**; when the constraint survives the summary violation stays 0%, when dropped it reaches 38%, and decay is **8.3× larger for soft organizational policies** than hard safety norms. A **Compaction-Eviction Attack** lets an adversary who controls only ingested data bias the summarizer to delete a policy (0%→65% on one model), and **Constraint Pinning** (≈47 tokens, <0.5% overhead) restores 0% violation in their tests.
**Why it matters:** it identifies context management as a first-class governance surface, independent of the model/tool layer. Single-author and needs replication, but the mechanism is corroborated by concurrent work cited in the paper, and the dose-response evidence is strong.

### 9. RoPE Distinguishes Neither Positions Nor Tokens in Long Contexts, Provably
**Yufeng Du, Phillip Harris, Minyang Tian, Eliu A. Huerta, Srikanth Ronanki, Subendhu Rongali, Aram Galstyan, Hao Peng** — arXiv [2605.15514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15514) — submitted to NeurIPS 2026 (per authors).
A content-independent analysis showing that, as context length grows, RoPE-based attention loses two properties central to its usefulness: **locality bias** (near positions are no more favored than far ones) and **token-relevance consistency** (a key that outranks another in one position may not in another), with failure probability approaching 0.5 — no better than chance. It further proves attention scores can be invariant when a token is moved or replaced (position/token aliasing). Increasing the RoPE base trades one failure for the other and cannot preserve both.
**Why it matters:** it is the strongest "RoPE has hard limits" theorem of the window and the theoretical counterpart to 2609.39929's diagnostic program; it frames why per-head/task-specific frequency allocation (AdaRoPE) is a principled direction rather than a hack.

### 10. Retrieval and Multi-Hop Reasoning in 1M-Token Context Windows: Evaluating LLMs on Classical Chinese Text
**Eric H. C. Chow** — arXiv [2605.02173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02173) — preprint.
An independent frontier-model evaluation at the advertised 1M-token scale, with two tests: (1) single-needle retrieval at 1M with real vs. training-prior-contradicting ("altered") needles at three depths, and (2) three-hop chain traversal at 256K, 512K and 1M tokens. Single-needle retrieval at 1M is essentially solved — Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5 each reach **100%** — but multi-hop performance reveals distinct deca[ys].
**Why it matters:** it is the clearest public falsification of "1M context = solved": capacity is there, compositional reasoning over it is not. It also uses altered needles to separate genuine in-context retrieval from memorization, a methodological improvement over standard NIAH.

### 11. Where Does Long-Context Supervision Actually Go? Effective-Context Exposure Balancing (EXACT)
**Jinchang Zhu, Jindong Li, Chengyu Zou, Rong Fu, Chao Wang, Haowei He, Menglin Yang** — arXiv [2605.10544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10544) — preprint.
Argues long-context adaptation is mis-modeled as window scaling: under packed training with document masking, each target token's *effective* context is usually short, so supervision is wasted on short-context predictions. **EXACT** reweights targets by inverse frequency within the long effective-context tail. Across seven Qwen/LLaMA continual-pretraining configurations it improves **all 28** trained/extrapolated NoLiMa and RULER comparisons (e.g. NoLiMa +10.09 / RULER +10.69 on Qwen2.5-0.5B; RULER +17.91 on LLaMA-3.2-3B), with a distance-resolved probe showing gains concentrated when evidence is thousands of tokens away.
**Why it matters:** it is a cheap, broadly applicable training-time fix that beats architecture-dependent recipes, and it explains why short-context validation is uninformative (echoing 2608.10296).

### 12. Beyond the Context Window: An Adaptive Entropy-Based Routing Framework for Hybrid Retrieval and Long-Context Language Models (EDAR)
**Isaac Olufadewa, Miracle Adesina, Ezekiel Oladejo, Owen Adeniyi, Fadare Fadekemi, Olamide Oso, Uthman Babatunde, Matthew Olawoyin** — arXiv [2609.35831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35831) — preprint.
Frames the 2026 consensus directly: pure long-context is expensive and under-attends the middle; pure RAG is fast but bounded by retrieval quality and brittle to partially relevant or contradictory chunks. **EDAR** decides *at inference time* whether to answer from retrieved chunks or escalate to full long-context, using the predictive entropy of the first few generated tokens of the RAG answer, with the threshold tuned on held-out data for a cost/accuracy trade-off.
**Why it matters:** it is a clean operationalization of "long context complements RAG" and one of the few papers that explicitly targets the routing decision. Small-scale (11 pages, 2 figures) — treat as a strong design pattern rather than a definitive result.

### 13. To Memorize or to Retrieve: Scaling the Interaction Between Pretraining and Retrieval
**Karan Singh, Michael Yu, Varun Gangal, Zhuofu Tao, Sachin Kumar, Emmy Liu, Steven Y. Feng** — arXiv [2604.00715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00715) — preprint.
Studies the trade-off at the heart of the debate: how much should a model *memorize* during pretraining versus *retrieve* at inference? It derives scaling-law-style relations for the interaction between pretraining compute/data and retrieval, with the title's framing ("RAG-considerate pretraining") and a log-form retrieval law fitted across benchmarks.
**Why it matters:** it supplies the quantitative missing piece for "long context vs RAG": the right point depends on the pretraining/retrieval budget, not on ideology. I read the abstract and metadata, not the full text, so I flag the evidence as not independently verified.

### 14. Intent Mismatch Causes LLMs to Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation
**Geng Liu, Fei Zhu, Rong Feng, Changyi Ma, Shiqi Wang, Gaofeng Meng** — arXiv [2602.07338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07338) — preprint, Feb 2026 (just before the window; included as the key follow-up to the ICLR 2026 Outstanding Paper "LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation").
Directly challenges the original LiC explanation: it argues the ~30–60% multi-turn drop is **not** a capacity deficit but a pragmatic intent-alignment gap, decomposed as P(response) = Σ P(execution | intent)·P(intent | context). The paper claims the relative degradation stays roughly constant (~60% in their replication, Figure 2) as model scale grows — implying scaling and training alone cannot fix it — and proposes a **Mediator–Assistant** architecture in which an experience-driven Mediator rewrites vague user turns into explicit instructions before the Assistant executes.
**Why it matters:** it is the mechanistic counterpoint to the ICLR 2026 paper and shifts the remedy from "train models to be more reliable" to "fix intent transmission." The theoretical arguments are strong; the empirical recovery evidence is weaker than the causal claim.

### 15. SeDT: Sentence-Transformer Decision-Transformer Conditioning for Multi-Turn Conversation Reliability
**Ramakrishna Vamsi Setti, Jagadeesh Rachapudi, Sachin Chaudhary, Praful Hambarde, Amit Shukla** — arXiv [2605.26788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26788) — preprint.
A third diagnosis of the same failure: a flat conversation history gives every prior turn equal implicit weight, so the model cannot distinguish critical constraints from filler. SeDT imports **return-to-go** conditioning from offline RL — each conversational shard gets a cumulative relevance score from semantic, lexical and positional signals, and the annotated full history is presented at the final turn, with no training and no weights changed. On the Lost-in-Conversation benchmark across 3 LLMs × 3 tasks it beats the sharded baseline in **9/9** combinations (up to **+37.7** mean performance) and reduces unreliability in 7/9.
**Why it matters:** it shows the LiC gap is at least partly a *context-weighting* problem fixable at inference. Caveat: independent-researcher authors, models are 2024-era (GPT-4o-mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Llama 3.3-70B), so absolute gains may shrink on frontier models.

## 4. Also notable (not in the ranked 15)

- **AdaRoPE: Not All Attention Heads Should Rotate and Scale Equally** — Shaowen Wang et al. — [2607.19363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19363) — **ICML 2026**. Per-head learnable RoPE frequencies and attention scaling; consistent gains over partial-RoPE/NoPE and better context extension. The leading "fix RoPE" architecture paper.
- **RoPE at the End of Its Rope** is the diagnostic counterpart (§2.1 above).
- **SpectralShift: Context Window Extension of Gated DeltaNet via Spectral Reparameterization** — Zian Liu et al. — [2609.14320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14320). Extends linear-attention (Gated DeltaNet) models by reshaping the decay spectrum; relevant because linear/hybrid layers are replacing softmax attention at the long-context frontier.
- **Long-Context Aware Upcycling (HyLo)** — Parsa Ashrafi Fashi et al. — [2604.24715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24715). Converts pretrained Transformers into hybrid archives (MLA + Mamba2/Gated DeltaNet), extends usable context up to 32×, cuts KV memory >90%, enables 2M-token prefill.
- **RecaLLM: Addressing the Lost-in-Thought Phenomenon** — Kyle Whitecross, Negin Rahimi — [2604.09494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09494). Shows in-context retrieval degrades *after a short reasoning span* (reasoning and retrieval interfere), and post-trains models to interleave retrieval with reasoning.
- **PredicateLongBench** — Siddhartha Jain, Ameya Velingker — [2607.08284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08284). Controls task difficulty along explicit axes (e.g. longest contiguous subsequence satisfying predicates) instead of reporting one saturated average.
- **LongPIBench** — Yupei Liu et al. — [2608.28411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28411) — Findings of EMNLP 2026. Shows prompt-injection defenses are substantially weaker in long contexts than in short ones.
- **SynthDocBench** — Abhigya Verma et al. — [2607.10400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10400) — **COLM 2026**. Controlled benchmark for long-context visual document understanding.
- **The Sirens' Song / Proximity Trap + ProxBench** — Xiaoyu Yang et al. — [2609.26718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26718). Argues distant-evidence failure is caused less by distance than by cumulative competition from proximal irrelevant background; proposes LYRA relevance alignment.
- **Training Long-Context VLMs with Generalization Beyond 128K** — Zhaowei Wang et al. — [2605.13831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13831). Practical data-mixture findings for multimodal long context (long-document VQA beats OCR transcription; balanced length distributions beat target-length-focused).
- **LongNovel** — [2608.18082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18082) — EMNLP 2026. Multi-scale benchmark for hallucination detection in long-novel summarization.
- **MedLoCoMo** — [2607.22566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22566). Multi-session medical dialogue benchmark; a rare domain long-context eval.
- **From Recall to Forgetting** — [2604.20006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20006) — ACL 2026. Benchmarks long-term memory for personalized agents.
- **Mem2ActBench** — [ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.370/). Benchmark for long-term memory utilization in task-oriented autonomous agents (abstract-level; ACL page).
- **Long-Context Aware Upcycling**, **Self-Guided Test-Time Training for Long-Context LLMs** — [2607.09415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09415). TTT that samples spans by question relevance rather than randomly, because random-span TTT can *hurt*.
- **MGAL: A Multilingual Granularity-Aware Long-Context Benchmark** — [2608.20853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20853).
- **KVMem** — [2609.04852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04852). Virtualizing million-token agent workspaces on a consumer GPU (systems side of agent memory).

## 5. Caveats and open questions

- **Coverage gap.** My harvest started from six relevance-sorted queries and completed only the first six before arXiv rate-limiting; a planned "long-context benchmark", "context engineering", "million-token", "long video" and "multi-turn" sweep returned empty and was abandoned at the parent's request. Benchmarks in particular are under-sampled relative to their importance — the ranked list's benchmark entries lean on LongPIBench/PredicateLongBench/SynthDocBench/DolphinBench, but there are likely more ACL/EMNLP/COLM 2026 long-context benchmarks not in this set.
- **No citation or acceptance-count signal.** OpenAlex indexing lag and Semantic Scholar 429s meant I could not rank by citations or by number of accepted papers. Venue tags come from author-reported arXiv comments and can be wrong or aspirational (e.g. one paper self-labels "ICML 2025" on a 2026 submission); only COLM 2026 and ICML 2026 labels from well-known groups were treated as reliable.
- **The "hot vs cooled" verdict is qualitative + within-window.** I did not run the prior-6-months comparison (that was the parent's clean-window trend scan). Within the window, the composition is unambiguous: memory/compaction/agent-memory papers now outnumber pure context-extension papers, and theory has caught up to practice.
- **The five most likely-to-be-overstated claims** to re-check before citing: (i) RoPE-end's +20/+25-point gains (best-of-search intervention); (ii) the KV-risk paper's LongBench-v2 accuracy (wide error bars); (iii) Governance Decay's magnitudes (single author, not yet replicated); (iv) the 1M-token "solved" claim (single-needle only, one evaluator); (v) DolphinBench's absolute scores (vendor benchmark, synthetic histories).
