## 1. Synthesis

**1) The field's unifying question in this window is "what happens after training."** Two of the highest-leverage surveys independently converge on the same move: reframing post-deployment behaviour as a first-class research object rather than a deployment detail. Self-Improving Test-Time Intelligence (2609.01679) proposes *Test-Time Intelligence* as a single axis with two knobs - **state update** (test-time adaptation, test-time learning, memory) and **inference compute** (test-time scaling, search, tools, verifiers) - and shows that the two literatures use different vocabulary for the same phenomena. Unsupervised Post-Training (2608.24982) makes the complementary move for the training phase: it defines UPT as adaptation on unlabeled inputs whose learning signal comes from *same-lineage model artifacts* rather than an external oracle, and catalogs 80 such methods. Together they describe a pipeline where models are increasingly improved by their own outputs at every stage.

**2) Agents became the organising abstraction, and the surveys are now systems surveys.** The strongest 2026 surveys no longer review "agent capabilities" but agent *infrastructure*: a nine-stage skill lifecycle (discovery, authoring, storage, routing, composition, execution/repair, lifelong adaptation, evaluation, security governance) in 2608.29596; four efficiency axes (observation, context/memory, action, planner/runtime) in 2609.02309; a *write-manage-read* memory loop in 2603.07670 and 2602.06052; and two full security taxonomies (2602.10453, 2603.11088). A consistent finding across them: the bottleneck has moved from model capability to **context, memory, routing, and verification**.

**3) Efficiency and safety are being reformulated as pipeline-stage problems.** 2609.10355 organises VideoLLM inference efficiency by the pipeline stage that a method acts on (frame sampling, modality encoding, connector token reduction, LLM prefill/decode). 2609.19445 gives a model/algorithm/system taxonomy for efficient multimodal learning and frames a three-way *Efficiency-Utility-Privacy* trade-off. On the safety side, 2610.00093 argues self-evolving agents change the safety question itself: once experience becomes reusable state, the issue is whether safety properties *survive accumulation, generalisation, and cross-context reuse*, not whether a single response is aligned.

**4) The most reusable contribution of the best 2026 surveys is negative: naming a measurement gap.** 2609.29669 catalogs 160 web-verified benchmarks and finds that **138 of 160 are model-agnostic, only 11 build an explicit world-model vs. VLA contrast, and only 4 carry prediction into executed action** - so the field literally cannot answer its own headline question. 2608.05179 finds code release is now common among AI-scientist systems but reproducibility-grade and claim-verifiable artefacts are rare. 2609.22695 argues the linear representation hypothesis has not been treated as a falsifiable hypothesis at all. This "we cannot yet measure X" genre is arguably the distinctive survey form of the period.

**5) The meta-science is now as quantitative as the science.** In a single cycle: ICML 2026 desk-rejected **497 papers (~2% of submissions)** after watermark-based detection flagged **795 reviews by 506 Policy-A reviewers**; NeurIPS 2026 desk-rejected **178 position papers (18.4%)** and put **123 more (12.7%)** on appeal after Pangram scored **28.2% of submissions at 100% AI**; ICLR 2026 saw a Pangram analysis of **19,490 papers and 75,800 reviews** estimate **~21% of reviews fully AI-generated**; and ICLR 2027 received **60,000+ abstracts in one cycle - more than all ICLR editions from 2013 to 2026 combined**. Peer review, not model capability, is the binding constraint the community now openly documents.

**Cross-cutting open problems** that recur across independent surveys: (i) recursive error amplification when models train on their own outputs; (ii) long-horizon stability and drift; (iii) the absence of independent, held-out evaluation during self-improvement; (iv) benchmark/provider entanglement that prevents attributing gains to a specific capability; (v) agent security at the skill/marketplace supply chain; (vi) reproducibility and claim verification; (vii) the cost of verification being externalised onto reviewers.

## 2. Ranked surveys (29)

Ranking is my judgement, weighted toward (a) breadth of community relevance, (b) comprehensiveness and quantification, (c) conceptual novelty. **[FULL]** = read in full from arXiv HTML.

**1. [A Survey on Self-Improving Test-Time Intelligence: Feedback-Driven Adapting, Learning, and Scaling at Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01679)** [FULL] - cs.LG, 1 Sep 2026. Unifies test-time adaptation, test-time learning and test-time scaling as *Test-Time Intelligence* along two axes (state **update** vs. inference-time **compute**). Taxonomy: feedback signals (entropy/confidence, consistency/augmentation, reconstruction, pseudo-label, external tools/env/users); what is updated (input, partial params, auxiliary params, backbone, external memory); temporal horizons; scaling by compute and by external resources. Open problems: long-horizon stability and model collapse in the wild, general-purpose and low-cost learning/evaluation objectives, efficiency/faster convergence/forward-only, theory, safety, and realistic evaluation protocols. Companion repo: https://github.com/mr-eggplant/awesome_test_time_intelligence.

**2. [Unsupervised Post-Training of Foundation Models: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24982)** [FULL] - 25 Aug 2026. Defines UPT strictly and catalogs **80 strict UPT methods** by the object supplying the update signal: (I) prediction statistic, (II) sample relation, (III) self-generated target, (IV) internal evaluator, plus orthogonal *Input Visibility x Update Persistence* axes. Central open problem is recursive error propagation; recommends separating signal quality from task structure, interrupt confidence/majority amplification (13 of 22 Family-II methods use hard majority), measure target/judge drift, and control initialisation/timing. Inventory frozen May 2026.

**3. [Use and Effects of LLMs in Peer Review: A Randomized Experiment and Survey at ICML 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19420)** [FULL] - cs.HC, 16 Sep 2026. The strongest causal evidence in the window on whether LLM policy actually changes outcomes. At ICML 2026 (24,000+ papers, 17,000 reviewers) reviewers were randomised between a conservative (no-LLM) and permissive policy: **near-zero effects on decisions, scores, and reviewer confidence**, but permissive reviews were **5.5-7% longer**; post-survey **N=1,486** found **22.5%** of conservative-policy reviewers used an LLM anyway and **36.5%** of permissive-policy reviewers admitted a disallowed use. Also documents submission growth (ICML 6,538 in 2023 to 24,661 in 2026, +277%; CHI +112%) and recommends realistic policies, volume management, and conference-provided tools.

**4. [World Models: A Comprehensive Survey of Architectures, Methodologies, Reasoning Paradigms, and Applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00133)** - 28 May 2026. The broadest world-model review: a multi-axis taxonomy over architecture (representation format, dynamics formulation, input modality, learning paradigm, downstream application), training methods, reasoning mechanisms and application settings spanning RL, robotics, autonomous driving, and video generation.

**5. [Autonomous Research Agents: A Survey of AI Scientists and the Verification Gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05179)** - 29 Jun 2026. Screens **125** works, includes **35**, and full-text codes **26** (24 runnable systems, 2 study/position papers) across seven audit dimensions (lifecycle stage, autonomy, evaluation method, released artifacts, human-in-the-loop points, novelty verification, result-selection disclosure). Headline finding: code release is now common, but reproducibility-grade and claim-verifiable artefacts are not - the "verification gap".

**6. [Do World Models Make Better Robots? A Survey of Evaluation Benchmarks for Predictive Embodied Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29669)** [FULL] - 30 Aug 2026. Catalogs **160 web-verified benchmarks (2017-2026)** into four lanes (policy suites, embodied agents, world-model evaluation, prediction-to-action bridges) and shows the field cannot run its own headline comparison: **138/160 model-agnostic, only 11 build an explicit VLA-vs-world-model contrast, only 4 execute predictions**. Proposes an advantage-aware evaluation loop and a four-metric protocol. Explains why "world model" names three different objects, none simultaneously action-conditioned, closed-loop, and VLA-comparable.

**7. [A Survey on the Linear Representation Hypothesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22695)** [FULL] - cs.AI, 19 Sep 2026. A rigorous meta-scientific critique: LRH claims are only well-defined once the **model, representation location, target feature, and evaluation distribution** are fixed (their Definition 4). Separates strong vs. weak vs. causal LRH, surveys last-layer vs. intermediate-layer targets, and isolates two concrete open problems: why intermediate layers show stronger linear structure than the final layer, and why linear representations arise only for certain (model, layer, feature, dataset) tuples (cheap-information retrieval? pretraining co-occurrence? correlation vs. causation?).

**8. [A Systematic Survey of Agentic Skills: Architecture, Lifecycle, and Security](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29596)** [FULL] - 30 Aug 2026 (v2 28 Sep). Formalises skills as externalised procedural knowledge and gives a **nine-stage lifecycle** (discovery, authoring/representation, memory storage, retrieval/routing, composition/orchestration, execution/repair, lifelong adaptation, empirical evaluation, security governance), plus a skill-type taxonomy (instructional/SOP, tool/API, reasoning/planning, and more). Six open challenges: cross-model portability and SLM deployment, sub-second routing over n>10^5 registries, graph-structured compilation with locality-bounded recovery, zero-trust marketplace security and formal verification, lifelong policy-skill co-evolution without procedural drift, and the environmental-feedback boundary.

**9. [Safety in Self-Evolving Agents: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00093)** - cs.CR, 8 Sep 2026. Reframes safety for agents that persist reusable state (parameters, memory, tools, skills, workflows). Introduces the **SAVER** framework - Substrate (where reusable influence lives), Adaptation (how it changes), Violation (which safety attribute is compromised), Exposure (where failures become observable) - and argues the key question is whether safety survives accumulation, generalisation, and cross-context reuse.

**10. [Are LLM Benchmarks Already Contaminated? A Systematic Review of Contamination Detection Methods](https://aclanthology.org/2026.gem-main.50/)** - ACL GEM 2026. Systematic review of **55 studies through late 2025**: a four-tier taxonomy (Exact, Syntactic, Semantic, Task-Level; T1-T4), five detection families (string matching, likelihood-based, membership inference, LLM-prompted detection, benchmark auditing), and synthesis on MMLU/GSM8K/HumanEval/HellaSwag. Key findings: no method is consistently reliable across tiers/access settings/stages; instruction tuning is a persistent blind spot; reported contamination inflation spans roughly **6%-40%**; proposes a Contamination Transparency Card.

**11. [Retrieved But Not Reliable: A Survey on Attacks and Defenses in Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24977)** - cs.CR, 25 Aug 2026. Pipeline-aware RAG security: formal threat models over **corpus, retriever, generator**; attacks grouped by objective (**accuracy, privacy, fairness**) covering corpus poisoning, backdoors, leakage, fairness violations; defenses organised by stage (retrieval, rerank, generation, traceback), plus robustness benchmarks and explainability.

**12. [The Landscape of Prompt Injection Threats in LLM Agents: From Taxonomy to Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10453)** - 11 Feb 2026. SoK with systematic literature review and quantitative analysis: attacks by payload-generation strategy (heuristic vs. optimisation), defenses by intervention stage (text, model, execution), and an evaluation-practice critique showing many defenses/benchmarks share the same limitation.

**13. [The Attack and Defense Landscape of Agentic AI: A Comprehensive Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11088)** - 11 Mar 2026, CSUR. First systematic survey of AI-agent security (design space, attack landscape, defenses) for agents that couple LLMs with non-AI system components; includes case studies and open challenges.

**14. [Reward Modeling for Reinforcement Learning-Based LLM Reasoning: Design, Challenges, and Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09305)** - 10 Feb 2026 (v2 28 Jun). Argues reward modelling is a central architect of reasoning alignment, not an implementation detail, and connects reward design to evaluation bias, hallucination, distribution shift, and learning efficiency.

**15. [From Models to Systems: A Comprehensive Survey of Efficient Multimodal Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19445)** - 16 Sep 2026. Distills **300+ works** into a three-level model/algorithm/system taxonomy (architectural parsimony, execution refinement, hardware-aware orchestration), with a methodological synthesis of cross-layer co-design and an *Efficiency-Utility-Privacy* trade-off, plus an MLLM case study.

**16. [Why Is Video Still So Expensive? A Survey of Inference-Efficiency Mechanisms in Video and Audiovisual LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10355)** - 9 Sep 2026. Covers only methods reporting concrete reductions in parameters, FLOPs, latency, memory, or token count, and organises them by **pipeline stage** (frame sampling, modality encoding, connector token reduction, LLM prefill/decode); assembles literature-reported accuracy-cost comparisons.

**17. [Efficient GUI Agents: A Systems Survey of Observation, Memory, Action, and Runtime Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02309)** - 2 Sep 2026. Argues success alone is the wrong metric; synthesises four efficiency axes (observation, context/memory, action, planner/system) using seed literature plus backward/forward citation chaining, and identifies recurring mechanisms (selective reading, global-to-local visual allocation, recoverable memory, verification-aware control, hybrid runtimes) together with the new overheads they add.

**18. [World-Action Models for Robot Learning and Control: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16074)** - 13 Sep 2026. Robotics-oriented review of World-Action Models that couple future-world prediction with executable action generation; separates WAMs from conventional world models, model-based RL, action-conditioned video generation, and reactive VLA policies; unified taxonomy over representations, transition modelling, action interfaces, architectures, training pipelines, data modalities, scaling; surveys manipulation, navigation, driving, and their benchmarks.

**19. [A Survey on Rubric-Guided Reinforcement Learning for Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27505)** - 27 Aug 2026. Casts constitutions as priors P(R) and rubrics as conditional instantiations R_x ~ P(R|x) in a Bayesian frame, then taxonomises along a prior-posterior axis: constitutional AI, instance-specific rubrics, process-level supervision, self-evolving rubrics, and agentic/multimodal extensions; includes a linguistic analysis of granularity/semantic trade-offs.

**20. [Agent-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05111)** - 8 Jan 2026. Reviews the transition from single-pass LLM-as-a-Judge to agentic judges that plan, call tools, verify against observations, collaborate, and keep memory; motivated by LLM-judge bias and inability to verify.

**21. [Locate, Steer, and Improve: A Practical Survey of Actionable Mechanistic Interpretability in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14004)** - 20 Jan 2026 (v4 Apr). Restructures mechanistic interpretability as an intervention pipeline - localise (diagnosis) then steer - keyed to interpretable objects, and shows downstream gains in alignment and other goals; a corrective to observational-only MI reviews.

**22. [Hallucinations in LLMs: A Lifecycle-Based Survey of Causes, Detection, Mitigation, and Prevention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26168)** - 20 Jul 2026. Three-fold categorisation of hallucination by LLM lifecycle stage - **data-related, training-related, inference-related** - covering causes, detection, mitigation, prevention, and high-stakes domains.

**23. [Memory for Autonomous LLM Agents: Mechanisms, Evaluation, and Emerging Frontiers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07670)** - 8 Mar 2026. Formalises agent memory as a **write-manage-read** loop coupled to perception and action, with a three-dimensional taxonomy (temporal scope, representational substrate, and a third axis) covering 2022 through early 2026.

**24. [A Survey of Agent Memory in the Second Half: Towards Self-Evolving and Long-Horizon Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06052)** - 14 Jan 2026 (v4 4 Aug). Positions memory as *the* substrate for the "second half" of AI (utility in long-horizon, user-dependent settings) rather than passive storage, covering the hundreds of 2025 memory papers.

**25. [LLM Agents for Time-Series: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26226)** - 26 Aug 2026. A problem-driven rather than component-driven taxonomy: forecasting/reasoning, augmentation/synthesis, anomaly detection/diagnosis, and decision support; examines how task requirements shape architecture, tools, and memory; compares reported performance under shared settings.

**26. [AI Alignment through a Game-theoretic Lens: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27910)** - 28 Aug 2026. Organises alignment around game-theoretic elements and three challenges - **preference diversity, alignment priority, temporal dynamics** - and is unusually explicit about where game theory genuinely helps versus where the framing is loose.

**27. [A Survey on Adversarial Attacks and Defenses for Diffusion Models Across Multiple Modalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05503)** - 28 Aug 2026. Claims the first unified review across image, video, and 3D; task-centric taxonomy (modality -> attack/defense -> generative task, chronological within task) plus consolidated datasets, metrics, and benchmarks.

**28. [Dynamic Heterogeneous Graph Representation Learning: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04779)** - 4 Sep 2026. First systematic review of representation learning for dynamic heterogeneous graphs, with a unified discrete-time/continuous-time formalisation preserving both structural semantics and temporal dynamics.

**29. [When Vision Meets Graphs: A Survey on Graph Reasoning and Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03816)** - 3 Sep 2026. A new sub-area review arguing that graph learning still ignores the visual form of graphs (molecular diagrams, network visualisations) despite strong vision and vision-language models.

## 3. Meta-science findings

### 3.1 Peer-review integrity

**ICML 2026 - watermark stings, 497 desk rejections.** ICML ran a two-policy experiment: Policy A (conservative, no LLM use) and Policy B (permissive: understand the paper, polish reviews). Detection used watermarking: a dictionary of **170,000 phrases**, two phrases sampled per submitted PDF (picking probability below one in ten billion), with hidden LLM-only instructions to insert them into the review; frontier models followed the injected instructions **over 80%** of the time in pre-deadline experiments. Results: **795 reviews (~1% of all reviews) written by 506 unique Policy-A reviewers** were flagged, every flag manually verified; **497 papers (~2% of all submissions) were desk-rejected**; the post was later corrected to say these correspond to **398 reciprocal reviewers** (the original said 506; 108 flagged reviewers were not reciprocal reviewers of active submissions). **51 Policy-A reviewers** (about **10%** of the 506) used an LLM in more than half their reviews and were removed from the pool. Reported family-wise error rate **0.0001**. ICML is candid that this only catches careless copy-paste use and is easy to circumvent if known - and it was known for almost the whole review period.
- https://blog.icml.cc/2026/03/18/on-violations-of-llm-review-policies/

**NeurIPS 2026 Position Paper Track - the Pangram audit.** Pangram v3.3.2 (default 250-350-word windows) scored **28.2% (273/969) of PPT submissions at 100%** AI, **42.7% at >=90%**, and **70.5% at >=50%**. Table 1 comparison: NeurIPS PPT 2025 (536 papers) 28.5% / 11.9% / 8.2%; NeurIPS PPT 2026 (971) 70.5% / 42.7% / 28.2%; NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks 2025 (996) 5.6% / 0.8% / 0.4%; NeurIPS Evaluations & Datasets 2026 (996) 43.7% / 9.3% / 2.1%; FAccT 2022 (159, pre-ChatGPT negative control) 0% / 0% / 0%; FAccT 2025 (204) 1.0% / 1.0% / 0.0%. Vendor-reported false-positive rate is **<0.1%**, and Pangram flagged only **1%** of ICLR 2026 accepted papers. Because window size drives the estimate, re-running with ~100-word windows dropped >=90% from 42.7% to **12.7%** and 100% from 28.2% to **2.16%**; on 10 ChatGPT-generated fake position papers, 100-word windows kept **70% recall at >=90%**. Decision thresholds produced **178 desk rejections without appeal (18.4%)** and **123 desk-reject-with-appeal (12.7%)**, later requiring a three-checkpoint audit trail (pre-AI, post-AI, final) plus evidence that AI edits added no substantive content.
- https://blog.neurips.cc/2026/06/02/ai-generated-papers-in-the-neurips-2026-position-paper-track/

**ICLR 2026 - the OpenReview/Pangram scrape.** After Graham Neubig (CMU) publicly questioned LLM-sounding reviews with fabricated references, Pangram (CEO Max Spero) analysed **all 19,490 ICLR 2026 papers and 75,800 peer reviews**: about **21% of reviews were estimated to be entirely AI-generated and more than half showed signs of AI use**. Some authors withdrew submissions. ICLR 2026 senior program chair Bharath Hariharan noted reviewers averaged about **5 papers in two weeks**; Neubig described a review crisis driven by exponential growth in submissions. Reported via the Chinese Academy of Sciences newspaper / Nature coverage.
- https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2025-12-03/doc-infznkss9769206.shtml

**A calibration audit of those detectors.** "Whose Threshold? A Calibration Audit of AI-Text Detectors Applied to ICLR Peer Reviews" (Chao-Hsiang Hung, University of Taipei; Zenodo, 11 Sep 2026) estimates AI-generated/AI-rewritten prevalence in ICLR reviews *after correcting for detector error*, and audits four detectors used at shipped default thresholds versus calibrated operating points. No review text is redistributed; review identifiers + scores are released so the corpus is reconstructible via the OpenReview API - a useful template for privacy-preserving review auditing.
- https://zenodo.org/records/22703692

**ICLR 2026/2027 volume.** ICLR 2026: **19,525 valid submissions**, **779 desk rejects**, **5,042 withdrawals**, **13,763 papers decided**, **18,054 reviewers** submitting **76,139 reviews**, **5,355 accepted**; roughly **20% of submissions had no reciprocal reviewer**. ICLR 2027 received **60,000+ abstracts in one cycle - more than the sum of every ICLR from 2013 to 2026 (~56,000)**; the first ICLR (2013) had **67** submissions. New anti-inflation policies: at most **20 submissions per author**, authors on 3+ submissions must review at least 6 papers, and each submission needs at least one registered reviewer. For scale, recent acceptances: ICLR 2026 5,355; NeurIPS 2025 5,290; ICML 2025 3,260; CVPR 2025 2,872.
- https://blog.iclr.cc/2026/09/02/submission-policies-for-iclr-2027/
- https://www.36kr.com/p/3993020476308230

**Enforceability.** A companion policy argument, "Policies Permitting LLM Use for Polishing Peer Reviews Are Currently Not Enforceable" (NSF PAR), makes explicit the tension ICML and NeurIPS both hit: permissive policies are unverifiable, restrictive policies are unenforced by a measurable fraction of reviewers.
- https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10688471

### 3.2 Benchmark contamination and reproducibility

- **Contamination is pervasive and detection is not solved.** The systematic review of 55 studies finds no consistently reliable detection method across contamination tiers, model-access settings, or training stages; instruction tuning is a persistent blind spot; RL/post-training auditing is only maturing; inflation estimates span **~6%-40%** depending on benchmark and assumptions. https://aclanthology.org/2026.gem-main.50/
- **Reproducibility is degrading, not improving.** "Position: Let's Strengthen Verifiability If We Can't Enforce Reproducibility" (2609.35854, 25 Sep 2026) quantifies that results are hard to reproduce and increasingly so, code is often unavailable, and proposes checkability-oriented reform instead of unenforceable full reproducibility. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35854
- Related: "The cost of reproducibility in artificial intelligence" (ScienceDirect S2666389926001492) and the Nature Computational Science editorial "Reproducibility in the era of large language models" (s43588-026-01061-2). https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-026-01061-2

- **ICML submissions 6,538 (2023) -> 24,661 (2026), +277%**. **CHI 3,182 -> 6,730, +112%**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19420
- **ICLR 2027 abstracts 60,000+**, exceeding the 2013-2026 cumulative total (~56,000); ICLR 2026 was 19,525. https://www.36kr.com/p/3993020476308230

### 3.4 The automated-research audit wave

A distinct 2026 genre asks not "can AI write papers?" but "can AI do research, and can we verify it?" The key instrumented results:

- **Agents' Last Exam (ALE)** - arXiv 2606.05405 (3 Jun 2026) + Berkeley RDI blog (June 2026). Built with **250+ industry experts** (blog: 300+ experts from 100+ institutions), grounded in O*NET/SOC 2018: **55 non-physical occupations in 13 industry clusters, 1K+ tasks** (blog: 1,500+ tasks, 55 occupations). Hardest tier: **average full pass rate below 1%**; the blog reports **0% success for every frontier agent tested, including Fable 5**. On the CLI subset, the best agent passes **25.2%** versus **82.0%** on Terminal-Bench and **59.1%** on SWE-bench-Pro. Cost per task diverges sharply: **Fable 5 ~$15.70, GPT-5.5 ~$3.80, Composer 2.5 ~$1.33** (roughly 4-12x cost for similar performance). Dominant failure mode: agents declare success before verifying. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05405 | https://rdi.berkeley.edu/blog/agents-last-exam/
- **AI4AI-Bench** - arXiv 2608.20318 (20 Aug 2026). Isolates training-algorithm design as the recursive-self-improvement bottleneck: **10 frozen research repositories across 10 training algorithm families**; the agent gets **4 hours on one B300** to rewrite the training algorithm, which is then rerun from scratch for **up to 12 hours** and scored by a hidden evaluator on a scale where **0.1 = the shipped algorithm and 1.0 = the optimum**. Across **29 configurations of 6 systems on all 10 tasks** the mean score was **0.166** and the best system **0.250** - under a fifth of the available distance. Most submissions never change how the model learns; the minority that do average **0.226 vs. 0.126**. More reasoning effort raised that minority from **8% to 64%** of submissions and the mean from **0.094 to 0.196**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20318
- **FARS: A Fully Automated Research System Deployed at Scale** - arXiv 2606.31651 (30 Jun 2026). In its first public deployment FARS produced **166 complete research papers spanning 67 fine-grained AI/ML topics**, preserving intermediate artifacts as an auditable corpus. Evaluation: **282 structured reviews from volunteer reviewers covering 140 papers**, including overall ratings, sub-scores, integrity checks, and LLM-use disclosure. Verdict: review-worthy and occasionally strong, with recurring failure modes in narrow experimental scope, methodological limitations, and integrity issues. (Secondary reporting indicates an automated reviewer was more lenient than the expert panel, mean 5.00 on the same corpus - flagged as lower-confidence, from a paper-content mirror.) https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31651
- **Shadow evaluations** - "Can AI agents conduct open-ended AI research? Early evidence from two case studies", arXiv 2607.27191 (29 Jul 2026). A third evaluation path between narrow verifiable tasks and blind peer review: an agent takes the central open-ended research question of a high-quality **unpublished** paper and the paper's **original authors grade the output**. On **two unpublished NeurIPS 2026 submissions**, frontier agents were given **six days and thousands of dollars of compute**; they completed all engineering without human help but **could not make substantial progress** on the central questions. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27191
- **SAEScientist-Bench** - arXiv 2609.09113 (8 Sep 2026). Tests autonomous mechanistic-interpretability research: given a target concept, an agent designs contrastive probes and navigates a **Gemma Scope dictionary of 131K+ features in Gemma-2-9B-IT** to find the optimal feature, scored against expert references on Neuronpedia. Across **10 agent configurations and 20 tasks**, agents show genuine discovery and lead different dimensions - approaching expert level at separating target concepts from contrastive controls - but **lag substantially in causal generation steering** and frequently misinterpret experimental measurements. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09113
- **AutoDataBench** - arXiv 2609.40097 (30 Sep 2026). Isolates *Data Intelligence* (diagnosis, organisation, construction) in three curated optimisation tasks with non-data factors held fixed, so gains can be attributed to data manipulation rather than frameworks/hyperparameters/compute; also shows reusing trajectories for mid-training improves downstream coding. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40097
- **SurveyReview** - "A Reviewer-Aligned Benchmark for Survey Evaluators", ACM SIGKDD 2026 (DOI 10.1145/3770855.3817505). Extends automated evaluation to judging survey quality itself - relevant because survey-writing was among the first workflows to saturate. https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3770855.3817505
- **The verification gap synthesis** - arXiv 2608.05179 codes 26 AI-scientist systems and finds code release common but reproducibility-grade and claim-verifiable artefacts rare. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05179

## 4. Caveats

1. **Survey ranking is subjective.** I weighted breadth, comprehensiveness/quantification, and conceptual novelty. A different weighting (e.g. subfield depth) would reorder tiers 2 onward substantially.
2. **Preprint status.** The large majority of the 29 surveys are arXiv preprints, not yet peer-reviewed; several are explicitly v1. arXiv HTML v1 may differ from later revisions. Only the ACL GEM contamination review and the KDD SurveyReview entry have venue-track status.
3. **AI-text detection is not ground truth.** The single biggest methodological caveat in Section 3: NeurIPS' own re-analysis moved its headline estimate from 28.2% to 2.16% purely by changing window size, with a measured recall cost. ICML's watermark detects careless copy-paste, not LLM use generally, and is explicitly easy to circumvent. The ICLR "21%" figure is a **vendor (Pangram) analysis** reported through press, not a peer-reviewed estimate - hence the value of the independent calibration audit in Zenodo.
4. **Self-report and enforcement incentives.** ICML and NeurIPS numbers are published by the organisers who also made the enforcement decisions. Desk-reject counts, reviewer-removal counts, and "noncompliance" rates are self-reported; the ICML post contains at least one published correction (506 -> 398 reciprocal reviewers), so treat precise figures as provisional.
5. **Reading depth is uneven.** Six surveys were read in full (2609.01679, 2608.24982, 2609.19420, 2609.29669, 2609.22695, 2608.29596). The remainder were assessed from verified title + abstract + metadata, with structural detail for a few. Taxonomy and open-problem summaries for the non-full-read entries are therefore compressed and could miss nuance.
6. **Secondary and non-English sources.** Some meta-science figures were located through Chinese-language science/tech press (CAS newspaper/Sina, 机器之心/36kr) reporting on primary work (Nature, ICLR blog). Where possible I cite the primary; where the primary is gated (PCMag/Cloudflare, ScienceDirect, ACM DL), the claim rests on the secondary report and is marked as such.
7. **Discovery bias.** Search-based discovery favours well-indexed, English-language, arXiv-published surveys and can miss venue-only reviews (TPAMI, CSUR, IJCV, TMLR) that have no arXiv listing.
8. **Contamination/reproducibility and growth figures mix measurement types.** The 6%-40% contamination range and the 18.4%/21%/28.2% AI-text rates are estimates under different assumptions and are not directly comparable to each other.
