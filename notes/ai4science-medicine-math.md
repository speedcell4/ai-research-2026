# AI for Science, Medicine & Mathematics (Oct 2025 – Oct 2026)

*A depth-and-breadth survey of AI4Science across biology, chemistry/materials, physics/weather, formal mathematics, autonomous discovery agents, drug discovery, clinical AI, and scientific foundation models. Emphasis on mid-2026 and on separating genuine closed-loop discovery from "LLM proposes, human verifies" scaffolding.*

---

## 1. Synthesis: what is genuinely happening, and what is hype

**The center of gravity moved from prediction to closed loops — but "closed" is doing a lot of work.** Through 2025 the story was foundation models that predict properties (AlphaFold-style structure, weather, variant effects). In the Oct 2025–Oct 2026 window the headline systems are *agentic loops*: AI Scientist/Robin, Kosmos, Google's Co-Scientist, AlphaProof Nexus, AlphaEvolve, and self-driving labs. In a handful of cases the loop actually closes in the physical world: Evo 2-derived designs and RFdiffusion/Doudna-style designed proteins were made and assayed in cells; WeatherNext Cyclones is an operational forecast model; Co-Scientist drove a real CVD reactor and grew monolayer semiconductors. But most "autonomous discovery" is better described as *LLM proposes, human or instrument verifies*, or *AI analyzes existing data and writes a report*. Robin's own Nature abstract says it achieves a "semi-autonomous approach"; the Virtual Biotech paper explicitly says "human-guided multi-agent systems"; Kosmos self-reports only 79.4% statement accuracy. The genuinely new capability is not unsupervised genius — it is *scaling the search-and-verification loop* (thousands of rollouts, kernel-checked proofs, robot-run experiments) enough that a small hit rate yields real results.

**Mathematics is the clearest win, because verification is free.** Formal verification in Lean 4 turned LLM unreliability from a fatal flaw into a filter. AlphaProof Nexus resolved 9 of 353 open Erdős problems and 44 of 492 OEIS conjectures at a few hundred dollars each, all kernel-checked [arXiv:2605.22763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22763). AlphaEvolve (with Terence Tao as co-author) improved several of 67 open analysis/combinatorics/geometry/number-theory problems [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2536158123). Anthropic's Claude formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in 11 days / 13M lines of Lean — a striking *engineering* result (30,300 theorems, ~29,500 used) but announced on a vendor blog, not peer-reviewed [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem). Autoformalization-at-scale pipelines (Atlas: 45,000+ Lean declarations from 26 textbooks, [arXiv:2605.29955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29955)) and geometry provers that also *refute* false statements (MechGeo, [arXiv:2608.02295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02295)) show the field is building infrastructure, not just leaderboard stunts.

**Biology, medicine and the physical sciences are more mixed, and the skepticism is warranted.** Genomics had two landmark peer-reviewed foundation models — Evo 2 (9T bp, 1M-token context, cross-domain genome design; [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10176-5)) and AlphaGenome (1 Mb input, single-base regulatory tracks, best-in-class on 25/26 variant-effect evaluations; [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-10014-0)). Protein design crossed into experimental reality (RFdiffusion antibodies with cryo-EM confirmation; ProteinMPNN-redesigned enzymes that evolve better than wild type; Doudna's SynTnpB nucleases active in human/plant cells). Weather AI became *operational*: WeatherNext Cyclones claims a >1-day lead-time advantage over physics models, and WeatherNext 3 forecasts hourly at 0.1° from raw satellite observations. Against that, the best-controlled clinical test in this window was a **null result**: the 93,326-person LungIMPACT RCT found AI prioritization of chest X-rays did **not** shorten time to CT (p=0.31) or diagnosis (p=0.84) [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04253-5). Many press claims ("a team of 37,000 AIs discovered a lung-cancer drug") outrun their papers, which propose *strategies* or report *in vitro* hits. The honest read: AI is decisively accelerating the search/verification parts of science, is beginning to close physical loops in narrow settings, and is *not* yet autonomously producing validated new theory or drugs.

## 2. Ranked list of the 18 most important papers

**1. Advancing Mathematics Research with AI-Driven Formal Proof Search (AlphaProof Nexus)** — George Tsoukalas et al. (Google DeepMind) — arXiv:2605.22763 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22763 — DeepMind preprint, May 21 2026 (v2 Jun 2026).
First large-scale evaluation of Lean-based AI proof search on *open* problems: the best agent autonomously resolved 9 of 353 open Erdős problems, proved 44/492 OEIS conjectures, and is deployed in combinatorics, optimization, graph theory, algebraic geometry and quantum optics. Proofs are kernel-checked by Lean, and a research mathematician (Gergely Bérczi, Aarhus) co-authored the work and curated problems; results are tracked on Terence Tao's Erdős-problems wiki. **Why it matters:** it is the strongest evidence that formal verification makes LLMs usable for genuinely new research mathematics, at a few hundred dollars per problem. **Caveat:** 9/353 is a small fraction, and solved problems are those where search+verification suffices.

**2. A multi-agent system for automating scientific discovery (Robin)** — Ali Ghareeb, Benjamin Chang, Ludovico Mitchener et al. (FutureHouse) — Nature 10.1038/s41586-026-10652-y — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10652-y — Nature, May 19 2026.
Integrates literature-search agents with data-analysis agents so the system generates hypotheses, proposes experiments, interprets results and revises hypotheses. Applied to dry age-related macular degeneration, Robin proposed enhancing retinal pigment epithelium phagocytosis and identified and confirmed **in vitro** efficacy for ripasudil. **Why it matters:** a peer-reviewed, wet-lab-validated closed loop from an AI-originated hypothesis to a therapeutic candidate; 159 citations within months. **Caveat:** the paper itself calls it a "semi-autonomous approach" — experiments are run by humans; efficacy is in vitro only.

**3. Mathematical discovery and exploration can be done at scale (AlphaEvolve)** — Bogdan Georgiev, Javier Gómez-Serrano, Terence Tao, Adam Zsolt Wagner — PNAS 10.1073/pnas.2536158123 — https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2536158123 — PNAS, Sept 30 2026.
Showcases AlphaEvolve's LLM-guided evolutionary search on 67 problems in analysis, combinatorics, geometry and number theory, rediscovering best-known solutions in most cases and improving them in several; in some instances it generalized finite-input results into all-input formulas, and it was combined with Gemini Deep Think and AlphaProof for automated proof generation. **Why it matters:** Tao's involvement and PNAS venue make this the canonical citation for autonomous mathematical construction. **Caveat:** "improved in several" is not a blanket claim; the method depends on a reliable automated evaluator per problem.

**4. Genome modelling and design across all domains of life with Evo 2** — Garyk Brixi et al. (Arc Institute / Stanford) — Nature 10.1038/s41586-026-10176-5 — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10176-5 — Nature, March 4 2026.
A biological foundation model trained on 9 trillion DNA base pairs with a 1-million-token context window and single-nucleotide resolution. It predicts functional impacts of variation zero-shot (noncoding pathogenic mutations, clinically significant BRCA1 variants), and interpretability analyses expose learned representations; the model supports genome-scale design. **Why it matters:** the broadest cross-domain genome foundation model to date (194 citations; 62 authors) and a platform for lab-validated synthetic genomes. **Caveat:** design claims ultimately rest on downstream wet-lab validation, which is still early.

**5. Advancing regulatory variant effect prediction with AlphaGenome** — Žiga Avsec, Natasha Latysheva, Jun Cheng et al. (Google DeepMind) — Nature 10.1038/s41586-025-10014-0 — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-10014-0 — Nature (online 2025; issue 2026).
A unified DNA sequence model taking 1 Mb input and predicting thousands of functional genomic tracks at up to single-base-pair resolution (expression, transcription initiation, accessibility, histone marks, TF binding, contact maps, splicing). It matches or exceeds the strongest external models in 25 of 26 variant-effect evaluations. **Why it matters:** it resolves the long-standing sequence-length vs resolution trade-off and is becoming standard infrastructure for noncoding variant interpretation (254 citations). **Caveat:** human/mouse only, and performance is benchmark-defined.

**6. Operational tropical cyclone forecasting with AI (WeatherNext Cyclones / WN-C)** — Ferran Alet, Tom R. Andersson, Ilan Price et al. (Google DeepMind) — Nature 10.1038/s41586-026-10953-2 — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10953-2 — Nature, Aug 6 2026.
An AI model producing state-of-the-art ensemble forecasts of tropical-cyclone track, intensity and size globally, generating 15-day global weather/cyclone scenarios. Evaluated on 2023–2025 storms, it offers an average lead-time advantage of ≥1 day over leading operational models — comparable to a decade of operational progress. **Why it matters:** AI weather has crossed from research benchmark to a claimed operational advantage on a life-safety task. **Caveat:** operational trust under extreme events and rare storms remains the open question.

**7. WeatherNext 3: Increasing resolution and performance of global weather models with raw observations** — Stephan Rasp, Boris Babenko, Dominic Masters et al. (Google DeepMind) — arXiv:2609.03582 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03582 — preprint, Sept 3 2026.
Produces new forecasts hourly (vs 6-hourly traditional models) at 0.1° resolution by ingesting low-latency geostationary satellite data and station observations directly, rather than only analysis fields. This removes the inheritance of analysis bias and reaches on-par resolution with physics-based global models. **Why it matters:** direct observation ingestion is the missing step for AI weather to rival NWP operationally. **Caveat:** preprint; the authors note evaluation complications from differing TIGGE/MARS resolutions.

**8. Kosmos: An AI Scientist for Autonomous Discovery** — Ludovico Mitchener, Angela Yiu, Benjamin Chang et al. (Edison Scientific) — arXiv:2511.02824 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02824 — preprint, Nov 4 2025.
Runs up to 12 hours of parallel data analysis, literature search and hypothesis generation, using a structured world model to keep a data-analysis agent and a literature agent coherent across ~200 agent rollouts, ~42,000 lines of code and ~1,500 papers per run. Every statement is cited to code or primary literature; independent scientists judged 79.4% of statements accurate, and collaborators estimated one 20-cycle run equalled ~6 months of their research time. **Why it matters:** a concrete data point on long-horizon agent coherence and traceability. **Caveat:** ~1 in 5 statements was inaccurate, and the "6 months" figure is collaborator self-report.

**9. The Virtual Biotech: A multi-agent AI framework for therapeutic discovery and development** — Harrison G. Zhang, P. Eckmann, Jia-Cheng Miao, Andrew B. Mahon, James Zou (Stanford) — Science 10.1126/science.aeg6779 — https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aeg6779 — Science, Sept 17 2026.
An organization of agents spanning target discovery, safety assessment, modality selection and clinical development. Across three decision points, >37,000 agents annotated outcomes from 55,984 trials and found cell-type-specific targets were 48% more likely to reach market with 32% fewer adverse events; it then proposed a lung-cancer therapeutic strategy and dissected a terminated ulcerative-colitis trial. **Why it matters:** large-scale, transparent evidence integration for drug-development decisions. **Caveat:** the paper says "human-guided"; the popular headline that "37,000 AI agents discovered a lung-cancer drug" overstates a *proposed strategy* with no clinical validation.

**10. Accelerating Scientific Research with Gemini in the Real-World (Co-Scientist)** — Samuel Schmidgall, Xiaokai Zhu, Marian Shaw et al. (Google DeepMind) — arXiv:2608.26701 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26701 — preprint, Aug 27 2026.
Extends Co-Scientist from in-silico hypothesis generation to execution-grounded closed loops. In materials it interfaced with a semi-automated CVD reactor to design a safe MXene precursor route (producing a lamellar 2D material) and enabled single-attempt growth of monolayer MoS2, MoSe2 and WS2; in biology it predicted E. coli swarming across IPTG gradients; in CS it discovered an inference-time scaling architecture beating six frontier models. **Why it matters:** one of the few systems actually closing the loop through physical instruments. **Caveat:** the MXene atomic structure was not confirmed; biology matched *unpublished* measurements; results lack replication.

**11. Atomically accurate de novo design of antibodies with RFdiffusion** — Nathaniel Bennett, Joseph Watson, Robyn Ragotte et al. (Baker lab, UW) — Nature 10.1038/s41586-025-09721-5 — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09721-5 — Nature, Nov 5 2025.
Fine-tunes RFdiffusion and couples it with yeast-display screening to design VHHs, scFvs and full antibodies against user-specified epitopes. Cryo-EM confirms designed binding poses for influenza haemagglutinin and C. difficile toxin B, and a high-resolution structure confirms atomic accuracy of designed CDRs. **Why it matters:** a milestone toward fully computational, epitope-specific antibody discovery (136 citations). **Caveat:** not all designs bind; affinity, developability and immunogenicity are unaddressed.

**12. AI-redesigned starting points and outcomes enhance protein evolution** — Nicholas A. Krasnow, Joy A. Xu, ... David R. Liu (Broad/Harvard) — Nature 10.1038/s41586-026-10820-0 — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10820-0 — Nature, July 22 2026.
Uses ProteinMPNN to redesign three botulinum neurotoxin proteases, yielding more stable, fully active variants and — the key idea — more *mutationally robust* starting points for directed evolution. Side-by-side phage-assisted continuous evolution from redesigned vs wild-type enzymes consistently produced higher-activity proteases; evolving an ataxin-2-cleaving protease reached >79-fold greater specificity than the best wild-type-evolved variant. **Why it matters:** establishes a reusable workflow where AI improves the *substrate* for experimental evolution, not just the endpoint. **Caveat:** three enzyme families; generality is plausible but unproven.

**13. Structure and evolution-guided design of minimal RNA-guided nucleases (SynTnpBs)** — Petr Skopintsev, Isabel Esain-Garcia, ... Jennifer A. Doudna (UC Berkeley/IGI) — Science 10.1126/science.aed6123 — https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aed6123 — Science, July 16 2026.
Couples a structure-guided inverse-folding model with evolution-informed residue constraints to generate divergent, active variants of TnpB (a minimal CRISPR-Cas12-like nuclease). High-throughput screening yielded editors retaining or exceeding wild-type activity in bacterial, plant and human cells, and cryo-EM of the most divergent variant revealed new stabilizing RNA/DNA contacts. **Why it matters:** shows AI design can enlarge the *designable* genome-editing space beyond natural scaffolds. **Caveat:** off-target profiles, delivery and in-vivo editing remain untested.

**14. MechGeo: Autoformalizing and Proving Euclidean Geometry in Lean 4** — Hao Shen, Junyu Guo, Tian Cui, Yuxuan Xiao, Lihong Zhi — arXiv:2608.02295 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02295 — preprint, Aug 2026 (43 pages).
A Mathlib-native agentic framework pairing GeoFormalizer (an intermediate GeoIR representation with structural diagnostics) and GeoProver (proof planning, lemma derivation, selective algebraization) where all certificates are checked by Lean's kernel. On 43 historical IMO geometry problems it proves 29; for the other 14 it constructs Lean-verified **counterexamples** and proves repaired statements; it also proves 12/14 of LEAP's Lean-IMO-Bench statements for the first time and covers IMO 2026 Problem 2. **Why it matters:** counterexample-guided autoformalization is a robust template for trustworthy formal geometry. **Caveat:** geometry-specific and heavily algebraization-dependent.

**15. Magenta: Closing the Loop Between Mathematical Reasoning and Lean Verification** — Joshua Ong Jun Leang, Haonan Li, Zheng Zhao et al. (MBZUAI/CMU) — arXiv:2609.11319 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11319 — preprint, Sept 2026 (v2).
A training-free agentic pipeline that turns a natural-language problem into an answer, a Lean 4 statement and a machine-checked proof, using a statement judge (does the formalization preserve the problem?) and an error-attribution judge (math re-derivation vs local Lean repair). Reports 100% on AIME 2025, AIME 2026 and HMMT Feb 2026, and with the open-weight K2-Horizon-7B reasoner solves all six IMO 2026 problems. **Why it matters:** statement adjudication is shown to be essential to prevent *false certificates* — a subtle failure mode. **Caveat:** benchmark/competition setting; IMO 2026 solutions would benefit from independent checking.

**16. Formalizing Mathematics at Scale (AutoformBot / Atlas)** — Ahmad Rammal, Niket Patel, Fabian Gloeckle, Amaury Hayat, Julia Kempe, Rémi Munos, Charles Arnal, Vivien Cabannes — arXiv:2605.29955 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29955 — preprint, May 2026.
A multi-agent system orchestrating thousands of LLM agents with formal-verification tools, dependency-aware scheduling and collaborative version control to translate 26 open-access graduate textbooks into **Atlas**: 45,000+ verified Lean 4 declarations and ~500,000 lines. The authors argue autoformalizing core graduate mathematics is now economically and technically feasible. **Why it matters:** provides the machine-checkable substrate that makes AI mathematics research (and Anthropic's Fermat formalization) tractable. **Caveat:** textbook-level formalization is not research-level theorem discovery.

**17. AI-based chest X-ray prioritization in the lung cancer diagnostic pathway: the LungIMPACT randomized controlled trial** — Nicholas Woznitza, Lesley Smith, Jonathan Rawlinson et al. — Nature Medicine 10.1038/s41591-026-04253-5 — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04253-5 — Nature Medicine, March 24 2026.
A prospective, multicentre RCT randomizing immediate AI prioritization of primary-care chest X-rays by day across 93,326 CXRs. Median time to CT was 53 days in both arms (geometric-mean ratio 0.97, 95% CI 0.93–1.02, p=0.31) and median time to lung-cancer diagnosis was 44 vs 46 days (ratio 0.98, p=0.84); referral, treatment and stage showed no significant differences; AI–radiology discordance was 30.3%. The authors conclude AI prioritization should **not** be deployed in this context. **Why it matters:** the strongest controlled counterweight to clinical-AI enthusiasm in the window — a large, well-powered null. **Caveat:** the null applies to *worklist prioritization* in a UK primary-care pathway, not to AI triage generally.

**18. OMatG-flash: An All-Atom Flow Map with Reinforce Adjoint Matching for Scalable Materials Discovery** — Thomas Egg, Harry Winston Sullivan, Ellad B. Tadmor, Stefano Martiniani — arXiv:2609.26402 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26402 — preprint, Sept 22 2026.
An all-atom flow map for inorganic crystal-structure prediction and de-novo generation that samples candidates with an order of magnitude fewer inference steps and less wall-clock time than state-of-the-art flow/diffusion models at comparable benchmark performance. It applies Reinforce Adjoint Matching to fine-tune the flow map, improving match rates and RMSE on unconditional CSP. **Why it matters:** inference cost is the practical bottleneck in generative materials discovery; this is a Pareto improvement. **Caveat:** the authors report instability and reward collapse at long training times, and call one reparameterization heuristic and not theoretically rigorous.

## 5. Caveats and red flags

1. **Vendor claims outrun peer review.** Anthropic's Fermat formalization and DeepMind's IMO/Erdős announcements are blog/PR-first. The 11-day, 13M-line Fermat claim has no paper, no independent replication, and defines "first complete machine-verified proof" in a way that depends on the Buzzard project's blueprint. Treat as an engineering demonstration, not a verified scientific result.
2. **"Closed loop" is routinely overstated.** Robin is "semi-autonomous" (in vitro, human-run experiments). Virtual Biotech is explicitly "human-guided" and proposed a *strategy*, not a validated drug — the "37,000 agents discovered a lung-cancer drug" framing is wrong. Co-Scientist's MXene structure was unconfirmed and its biology matched unpublished data. Kosmos is data-analysis + literature only, with 79.4% statement accuracy.
3. **Benchmark contamination and saturation in math.** AIME/HMMT scores are not clean measures; IMO-style claims (Magenta "all six", Nemotron "30/42 gold", multiple "perfect scores") are not mutually consistent and are usually self-reported without independent grading. Formal proofs are the exception: Lean kernel checks are trustworthy, but the *statement* being formalized can still be wrong, which is exactly why Magenta's statement judge matters.
4. **Genomic foundation models look less dominant under unified evaluation.** GENEB shows rankings flip across task categories, scale gains are modest and inconsistent, and architecture/pretraining alignment often beats parameter count. Aggregate leaderboards should be distrusted.
5. **Clinical AI evidence is thinner than deployment.** The best-powered RCT here (LungIMPACT, n=93,326) was null for worklist prioritization. DermFM-Zero's reader studies are promising but simulator/multinational-reader designs, not outcome trials.
6. **Materials discovery still scores against proxies.** Stability means DFT/MLIP energy-above-hull; synthesizability and experimental realization are rarely confirmed. OMatG-flash itself reports training instability and calls part of its method heuristic.
7. **Citation counts in this window are early and hype-inflated**, and several venues/dates are preprints that may change through peer review.
8. **Method/coverage caveat.** Hugging Face's Daily Papers API and pages were rate-limited from this environment, so "what the community cared most about" is inferred from news prominence, OpenReview/ICLR oral listings, CERN/ICML virtual sites and early citation counts rather than directly from upvote data. No export.arxiv.org was used.
