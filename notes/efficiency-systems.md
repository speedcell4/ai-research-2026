# Efficiency, Systems & Serving — Survey of Oct 2025 – Oct 2026

*Scope: quantization (low-bit/FP8/FP4), knowledge distillation, speculative decoding, KV-cache compression, sparse/efficient attention, serving & scheduling, kernels/compilers, MoE inference systems, on-device/edge inference, long-context inference, agent inference cost, energy, and hardware co-design. Compiled 2026-10-03.*

*Method note: I mined the parent wave's harvested corpora — 4,486 up-voted HF Daily Papers (Oct 2025 – Oct 2026, data/hf_ranked.json), the full 6,628-title ICML 2026 scrape (notes/_scratch/icml_titles.json), and the arXiv survey/topic harvests — then discovered via web search and fetched abstracts (about 85) and full HTML for 11 key papers directly from arxiv.org (never export.arxiv.org). Everything below is attributed to its source; numbers are as reported by the authors unless marked. Fetched content is treated as untrusted data.*

## 1. Synthesis: what is actually happening

**The center of gravity moved from "an efficiency trick" to "efficiency as the architecture."** The decisive papers of this window are not standalone pruning/quantization methods; they are frontier model reports in which the efficiency mechanism *is* the model design, shipped with kernels and serving stacks. DeepSeek-V4 ([2606.19348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19348)) interleaves Compressed Sparse Attention (CSA) and Heavily Compressed Attention (HCA) for hybrid long-context attention and claims that at 1M-token context its 1.6T-parameter Pro model needs only **27% of the single-token inference FLOPs and 10% of the KV cache of DeepSeek-V3.2** (its 284B Flash variant 10%/7%), with Muon-optimized training, a fine-grained EP mega-kernel, TileLang kernels, on-disk KV tiers and FP4 QAT (2x faster top-k at 99.7% recall). DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash ([2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19969)) pushes KV compression to **890 bytes/token (about 1/4 of V4-Flash)** and a persistent footprint of **about 1/8** via cross-layer KV reuse (CSA2), FP4 KV, and an asymmetric Causal Encoder-Decoder that activates 8B/token at prefill but 16B/token at decode. Kimi K3 ([2607.24653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24653)) ships a 2.8T-parameter MoE (104B activated, 16 of 896 experts) with Kimi Delta Attention and reports about 2.5x better scaling efficiency than K2 at 1M context. Qwen3.8-Next ([2608.30320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30320)) is a 125B/6B-active hybrid of Gated DeltaNet and Qwen Sparse Attention that matches a 397B predecessor on 8 of 14 benchmarks at about 1/9 the training FLOPs. In other words: **the biggest efficiency papers of the year are model cards, not method papers.**

**The second real shift is that sparse attention won the long-context argument, and it only counts when co-designed with kernels.** The dominant design is a lightweight learned *selector* over compressed KV blocks, verified by an exact sparse-attention main branch: DeepSeek's DSA ([2512.02556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556)) → CSA/CSA2, MiniMax Sparse Attention ([2606.13392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13392), 28.4x per-token attention FLOPs reduction at 1M and 14.2x/7.6x measured prefill/decode speedups on H800 via an exp-free top-k + KV-outer kernel), and HiLS ([2607.02980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02980), >64x length extrapolation). Linear/recurrent layers are now *components* of interleaved hybrids, not Transformer replacements. Around this core, the supporting stack matured: FP4 (NVFP4 vs MXFP4) became the deployment format of record; rotations/Hadamard transforms became the standard preconditioner for 4-bit; KV cache became a first-class *systems* object with placement, migration and on-disk tiers ([2607.08057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08057)); speculative decoding shifted from EAGLE-style small AR drafters to parallel/block drafters with semi-autoregressive refinement; and serving moved from a single engine (vLLM/SGLang) toward a fleet-level "control plane" (llm-d) that routes requests, KV state, phases and accelerators ([2609.23130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23130)). The year's most useful *negative* result belongs here: Random Attention ([2609.03430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03430)) shows the KV-eviction selection score contributes almost nothing — random eviction within each head matches the strongest selector while being 32–43% faster under vLLM, because the prompt is the fragile part and the reasoning trace redundantly protects itself.

**What is hype or thin.** (a) **"Efficiency is the largest ICML 2026 bucket."** I verified this against the full 6,628-title scrape: with a strict efficiency/systems regex I count **930 titles (14.0%)**; a broader one gives 997 (15.0%) — matching the parent's about 936. But the cross-cutting **LLM/foundation-models** category is larger (**1,436, 21.7%**), and **reasoning/test-time compute is essentially tied (963, 14.5%)**. So efficiency is the largest *systems/engineering* bucket, not the single largest topic, and it is **not the fastest-growing**: conference share is roughly flat (ICLR 12.3%→13.2% across 2025→2026; NeurIPS 11.5%→10.7% across 2024→2025), while reasoning/RL/agents grew much faster. It is also *diffuse* — quants 83, MoE 84, serving 79, distillation 104, kernels/compilers 48, sparse attention 60, KV cache 32, spec decoding 31, long-context systems 30, energy 39, hardware co-design 9, on-device 6 — and no top ICLR/ICML 2026 award went to it (the closest is the ICLR Honorable Mention *The Polar Express*, on low-precision polar decomposition for Muon). (b) **Speedup numbers are configuration-dependent and usually single-point.** 5–6x speculative-decoding claims (Domino 5.49x/5.8x) and 1.3x kernel claims are measured at one model/backend/sequence length; at high concurrency verification consumes batch capacity. (c) **FP4 "lossless" claims are narrow.** (d) The **on-policy distillation** wave exploded (+166% OpenAlex share) as a cheap way to transfer RL capability, but the best-controlled 2026 analyses find teacher supervision is largely noise and that much of OPD's gain reduces to suppressing low-probability tokens — no teacher required ([2608.31046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31046)). (e) Energy/sustainability work is mostly measurement and synthesis, not new algorithms.

## 2. Ranked papers (18)

### 1. DeepSeek-V4: Towards Highly Efficient Million-Token Context Intelligence — DeepSeek-AI (Anyi Xu, Bangcai Lin, Bing Xue, Bingxuan Wang, et al.) — [arXiv:2606.19348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19348) — technical report (preview), mid-2026
Two MoE models (V4-Pro: 1.6T total / 49B activated; V4-Flash: 284B / 13B activated) with 1M-token context, built on (i) a hybrid attention of Compressed Sparse Attention (CSA, KV compressed along sequence then sparse DSA) and Heavily Compressed Attention (HCA, aggressive compression + dense attention); (ii) Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections; (iii) the Muon optimizer with hybrid Newton–Schulz. The systems stack is as important as the architecture: fine-grained wave-based EP that fuses dispatch/combine into one pipelined kernel (1.50–1.73x speedup, up to 1.96x on RL rollouts) with an open-sourced MegaMoE CUDA kernel, TileLang-generated fused kernels with host codegen, a heterogeneous KV-cache layout with a state cache for sliding-window and uncompressed tail tokens, on-disk KV with three SWA strategies, and FP4 (MXFP4) QAT on MoE weights and the indexer QK path.
**Evidence/why it matters:** at 1M context V4-Pro needs 27% of V3.2's single-token FLOPs and 10% of its KV cache (Flash: 10%/7%); the report also gives explicit hardware-vendor guidance (a compute/bandwidth balance point of about 6.1 TFLOP/s per GB/s, power headroom, push-vs-pull primitives, replacing SwiGLU). This is the closest thing the year has to a full-stack efficiency blueprint, and its FP4-QAT-for-inference/rollout consistency is a template others are copying. Caveat: a technical report with author-reported numbers; independent reproduction is not yet available.

### 2. DeepSeek-V3.2: Pushing the Frontier of Open Large Language Models — DeepSeek-AI (Aixin Liu, Aoxue Mei, Bangcai Lin, Bing Xue, et al.) — [arXiv:2512.02556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556) — model report, Dec 2025
Introduces **DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA)**, an efficient attention that cuts long-context compute while preserving quality, plus a scalable RL framework and a large-scale agentic-task synthesis pipeline. The high-compute variant V3.2-Speciale is claimed to surpass GPT-5 and reach gold-medal level at IMO 2025 and IOI 2025.
**Evidence/why it matters:** this is the paper that made *learned sparse attention over a compressed KV* the default long-context recipe and kicked off the DSA→NSA/MSA→CSA lineage; it is also the efficiency baseline that DeepSeek-V4 measures itself against (the 27%/10% claim is relative to V3.2). Read it for the attention design; the frontier-parity head-to-head is a single vendor evaluation and should be treated as such.

### 3. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression — DeepSeek-AI (Anyi Xu, B. Li, Bangcai Lin, Bing Xue, et al.) — [arXiv:2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19969) — model report, Sep 2026
A 552B-backbone multimodal MoE for up to 1M contexts with a Causal Encoder-Decoder that activates **16B/token during decode but only 8B/token during prefill**, tuned for input-heavy agentic workloads. It combines cross-layer KV reuse in **Compressed Sparse Attention 2 (CSA2)** with **FP4 KV caching**, and adds **SWA Bounded Replay** to bound the persistent cache.
**Evidence/why it matters:** the global KV footprint is **890 bytes/token (about 1/4 of V4-Flash)** and the persistent (SSD/host) footprint about 1/8, while reported quality improves over the V4-Flash baseline — the most aggressive production KV-compression claim in the window. This is the clearest statement that long-context serving cost is now a *KV storage/bandwidth* problem, not a FLOPs problem.

### 4. Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence — Kimi Team (Tongtong Bai, Yifan Bai, Yiping Bao, et al.) — [arXiv:2607.24653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24653) — technical report, Jul 2026 (525 HF upvotes)
A 2.8T-parameter MoE with 104B activated parameters, native vision and a 1M-token window, using **Kimi Delta Attention** and **Attention Residuals** for cross-sequence/cross-depth information flow, and **Stable LatentMoE** activating 16 of 896 routed experts. Reported post-training spans general/agentic/coding RL with multiple reasoning-effort levels, and the report claims about 2.5x better overall scaling efficiency than K2.
**Evidence/why it matters:** the highest-upvoted efficiency paper in the harvested HF set, and a rare full disclosure of trillion-scale serving/training infrastructure (KDA algorithm–system co-design, balanced expert-parallel training, million-token agentic RL). Claims trail top proprietary models, so read the efficiency claims (which are architectural) as stronger than the capability parity framing.

### 5. FlashAttention-4: Algorithm and Kernel Pipelining Co-Design for Asymmetric Hardware Scaling — Ted Zadouri, Markus Hoehnerbach, Jay Shah, Timmy Liu, Vijay Thakkar, et al. — [arXiv:2603.05451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05451) — preprint, Mar 2026
A redesign of the attention kernel for Blackwell (B200/GB200), where tensor-core throughput doubles but shared-memory bandwidth and exponential units do not ("asymmetric hardware scaling"). It uses fully asynchronous MMA pipelines with larger tiles, software-emulated exponentials and conditional softmax rescaling, and tensor memory / 2-CTA MMA to cut shared-memory traffic and atomics in the backward pass.
**Evidence/why it matters:** up to **1.3x over cuDNN 9.13 and 2.7x over Triton**, reaching **1613 TFLOPs/s (71% of theoretical max)** at BF16 on B200. It is the clearest example of the year's co-design thesis — and a reminder that attention speedups now come from matching *asymmetric* hardware, not from FLOP counting.

### 6. MiniMax Sparse Attention (MSA) — Xunhao Lai, Weiqi Xu, Yufeng Yang, Qiaorui Chen, Yang Xu, Lunbin Zeng, et al. — [arXiv:2606.13392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13392) — technical report, Jun 2026 (169 HF upvotes)
A block-sparse attention on top of GQA: a lightweight Index Branch scores KV blocks and selects an independent top-k per GQA group (always keeping the local block), while the Main Branch does exact block-sparse attention over only the selected blocks; a KL loss aligns the index distribution and the selector gradient is detached. It is co-designed with an exp-free TopK kernel and KV-outer sparse attention for tensor-core utilization.
**Evidence/why it matters:** on a 109B model trained on 3T tokens, MSA matches full GQA on downstream/agentic benchmarks while cutting per-token attention FLOPs **28.4x at 1M context** and delivering **14.2x prefill / 7.6x decode** wall-clock on H800. The paper is unusually honest that runtime gains trail FLOPs gains because of index construction/load-balancing overhead — the exact gap most sparse-attention papers hide.

### 7. Towards Efficient Large Language Model Serving: A Survey on System-Aware KV Cache Optimization — Jiantong Jiang, Peiyu Yang, Rui Zhang, Feng Liu — [arXiv:2607.08057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08057) — **ACL 2026 Findings**
The first survey to organize KV-cache work by *system behavior* rather than by model trick, with three axes: execution/scheduling (temporal), placement/migration (spatial), and representation/retention (structural), plus an analysis of cross-behavior co-design affinity.
**Evidence/why it matters:** it gives the field the vocabulary it lacked and is the best single entry point to the KV-infrastructure literature. It is a taxonomy, not new measurements, and it explicitly flags that behavior–objective links are under-quantified.

### 8. Transforms for LLM Quantization: The Great Inversion and Format Co-Design — Ehsan Jokar — [arXiv:2608.25188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25188) — preprint, survey to a June 2026 cutoff
A theory-first survey of the *transform stage* now at the front of every competitive 4-bit pipeline (rotation/scaling/permutation/affine). It formalizes the **Great Inversion**: allocation-flexible transform coding rewards energy *concentration* (the classical KLT result), whereas the grouped shared-scale quantization a real matrix instruction performs rewards within-group *flattening* (Hadamard incoherence) — two prescriptions that provably point in opposite directions under within-group majorization.
**Evidence/why it matters:** it surveys **200 works** and classifies **43 transform methods** by structure/data-awareness/runtime cost, and shows the right pole depends jointly on allocation regime and number format (MXFP4's power-of-two block scale still rewards intra-block rotation; NVFP4's mantissa-carrying scale largely removes the pull). The single most useful conceptual paper in quantization this year; treat its format-specific claims as theory-driven predictions pending systematic benchmarks.

### 9. Random Attention: Rethinking KV Cache Eviction for Efficient Reasoning — Heng Wang, Jielin Qiu, Wenting Zhao, Cheng Qian, Liangwei Yang, Weizhi Zhang, et al. — [arXiv:2609.03430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03430) — preprint, Sep 2026 (152 HF upvotes)
Rather than scoring cached tokens, Random Attention keeps the prompt and evicts uniformly at random within each attention head, computing **no score at all**. Controlled experiments show (i) the prompt is the fragile part of the cache and most of the gap between selectors is whether their signal happened to keep it, and (ii) reasoning traces protect themselves through redundancy in text and across heads.
**Evidence/why it matters:** across four models and six reasoning tasks it matches the strongest baseline while delivering **32–43% higher throughput** in vLLM by skipping the scoring pass. This is the year's most important negative result in KV compression and a direct challenge to the entire scored-eviction literature. Caveat: the evidence is on reasoning models/tasks; the authors themselves note the result depends on trace redundancy and may not transfer to short-answer or non-reasoning decoding.

### 10. MinT: Managed Infrastructure for Training and Serving Millions of LLMs — Mind Lab (Song Cao, Vic Cao, Andrew Chen, Kaijie Chen, et al.) — [arXiv:2605.13779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13779) — technical report, May 2026 (226 HF upvotes)
Managed infrastructure for LoRA post-training and online serving at the scale of "many trained policies over a few expensive base models." Instead of materializing merged checkpoints, it keeps the base resident and moves exported adapter revisions through rollout/update/export/eval/serving/rollback, scaling up to frontier dense and MoE architectures (MLA and DSA attention) beyond 1T total parameters, down to adapter-only handoff (under 1% of base size), and out to 10^6-scale addressable catalogs.
**Evidence/why it matters:** adapter-only handoff cuts measured step time **18.3x** on a 4B dense and **2.85x** on a 30B MoE; packed MoE LoRA tensors improve live engine loading 8.5–8.7x. It directly addresses the emerging "1 base model, N policies" serving workload (personalization, RL fleets, agent skills) that classic vLLM-style serving was not built for.

### 11. Disaggregated Quantization: Specializing LLM Prefill and Decode — Andrei Panferov, Maximilian Kleinegger, Sweta Priyadarshi, Tijmen Blankevoort, Dan Alistarh — [arXiv:2609.26333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26333) — preprint, Sep 2026 (90 HF upvotes)
Splits quantization by serving phase: low-precision *activations* help prompt processing while compact *weights* help memory-bound decoding. "Disaggregated quantization" keeps the decode checkpoint fixed while training/adding a separate compute-native prefill format (e.g., an NVFP4 prefiller for a 1-bit/GUF decode model), and **Offloaded Disaggregated Prefill** streams the extra prefill weights from SSD to amortize loading over prompt length.
**Evidence/why it matters:** with released Qwen3.8-27B GGUF decoders, the NVFP4 prefiller improves 1-bit accuracy by **32.5 points on MMLU-Pro and 35.3 on MMMU-Pro**, and ODP gives **1.78x TTFT speedup at 8K prompt** in llama.cpp; accuracy is validated under vLLM disaggregated serving and shared-weight format disaggregation is tested up to 2.8T parameters. A genuinely new degree of freedom (format-per-phase) that fits the industry's move to prefill/decode disaggregation.

### 12. Unveiling the Potential of Quantization with MXFP4: Strategies for Quantization Error Reduction — Jatin Chhugani, Geonhwa Jeong, Bor-Yiing Su, Yunjie Pan, Hanmei Yang, Aayush Ankit — [arXiv:2603.08713](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08713) — preprint (NVIDIA), Mar 2026
Software-only fixes for the accuracy gap between OCP's MXFP4 and NVIDIA's NVFP4: **Overflow-Aware Scaling (OAS)** enlarges effective dynamic range under power-of-two block scaling, and **Macro Block Scaling (MBS)** adds higher-precision scaling at coarser granularity to preserve outliers.
**Evidence/why it matters:** across LLMs and downstream benchmarks the MXFP4–NVFP4 gap shrinks from **about 10% to under 1% on average** at modest GEMM cost (6.2%), preserving MX's about 12% tensor-core area advantage. This matters because the hardware ecosystem is split between two FP4 formats; making MXFP4 viable is a hardware-portability win, not just an accuracy trick. Caveats: vendor-authored, GEMM overhead measured on specific shapes, and "average gap" masks per-task variance.

### 13. Why Gated DeltaNet Survives 4-Bit Quantization: NVFP4 W4A4 for the Recurrent Half of a Hybrid 27B LLM — Sergii Kozyrev, Davyd Maiboroda — [arXiv:2609.04098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04098) — preprint, Sep 2026 (84 HF upvotes)
Builds "Minima": NVFP4 W4A4 across all 496 linear layers of a hybrid 27B model that interleaves Gated DeltaNet (48 layers) with softmax attention (16), including the GDN gates that prior 4-bit recipes deliberately left at 8/16-bit. A four-part mechanism study explains why the recurrence tolerates it: NVFP4's 16-element blocks localize residual-stream outliers; softplus/sigmoid gate parameterizations compress about 11% GEMM error to about 2% output error; the delta rule holds injected noise at a flat plateau and forgets a state impulse within hundreds of steps; and per-token quantization cost washes out with context.
**Evidence/why it matters:** matches BF16 within seed noise (5-task average −0.52) while being the smallest (17.5 GiB) and fastest-prefill (+14–19%) recipe compared, with RULER retrieval to 64K. A rare *quantization × architecture* mechanism paper, and practically important as hybrids become standard.

### 14. Domino: Decoupling Causal Modeling from Autoregressive Drafting in Speculative Decoding — Jianuo Huang, Yaojie Zhang, Qituan Zhang, Hao Lin, Hanlin Xu, Linfeng Zhang — [arXiv:2605.29707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29707) — preprint, May 2026 (93 HF upvotes)
Speculative decoding's core trade-off is draft quality vs drafting cost: AR drafters model causal dependencies but are sequential; parallel drafters are cheap but weak on intra-block dependency. Domino uses a parallel draft backbone for the whole block, then a lightweight head to refine with prefix-dependent causal information, stabilized by a base-anchored training curriculum.
**Evidence/why it matters:** up to **5.49x end-to-end speedup under Transformers and 5.8x throughput under SGLang** on Qwen3. It is the clearest articulation of the year's spec-decoding pivot from "train a smaller AR drafter" to "parallel draft, then repair causality." Caveats: speedups are heavily batch/acceptance-length dependent, and the SGLang number is a throughput measurement, not a per-user latency one.

### 15. Director: Accelerating Distributed MoE Serving via Online Proactive Expert Placement — Qianli Liu, Kaibin Guo, Zicong Hong, Peng Li, Fahao Chen, Haodong Wang — [arXiv:2607.08782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08782) — **IEEE INFOCOM 2026**
Expert parallelism makes MoE serving latency depend on where experts live, but past-activation-based placement fails under diverse/changing request patterns. Director predicts incoming requests' expert activation (lightweight cascaded predictor or a low-bit quantized replica), then migrates experts online with near-zero downtime during compute-bound phases, using a relaxation-based placement optimizer with a (1+epsilon) approximation and polynomial runtime.
**Evidence/why it matters:** reports **11–55% end-to-end latency reduction** across Mistral/DeepSeek/Qwen MoE models. It is the strongest of a dense 2026 cluster on proactive/preemptive expert placement and disaggregated MoE scheduling.

### 16. The Other Half of the Memory Wall: Serving 35B MoEs from SSD with Trained Routing Prediction — Yu Lin, Yiming Wang, Runyuan Cai, Hanze Liu, Xiaodong Zeng — [arXiv:2609.18063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18063) — preprint, Sep 2026
"Edge0" closes the SSD-offload gap with a **prerouter**: a per-layer head predicts the *next* layer's routing one token ahead, and the prediction is consumed *as* the routing, so the staged expert set equals the routed set (nothing is dropped). An unmerged recovery LoRA, trained on the student path, pays back quality lost to int4 quantization and routing replacement.
**Evidence/why it matters:** on a single 24GB machine it serves a 35B MoE at **20 tok/s inside 3 GiB peak active memory**, within a few points of its fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks, with open checkpoints. This is the best evidence that consumer-hardware frontier serving is a *prediction + quantization* problem, not just an offloading one. Caveat: single-machine, latency-oriented, and the "within a few points" margin is not broken down per task.

### 17. FreeToken: Efficient Edge-Native MoE Serving with Bandwidth-Adaptive Execution — Shuo Yang, Xiaoze Fan, Melissa Pan, Haocheng Xi, Zhe Wang, Shanlin Sun — [arXiv:2608.16157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16157) — preprint, Aug 2026 (86 HF upvotes)
An edge-native MoE serving system that treats a personal machine as a unified elastic platform, continuously mapping layout/loading, expert residency, CPU–GPU execution, agentic state reuse and memory management onto whatever heterogeneous resources are actually available, rather than committing to one offloading strategy.
**Evidence/why it matters:** supports 20+ MoE models and real coding/tool agents from an 8GB laptop GPU to a single workstation GPU, claiming a 35B on a laptop, a 284B on a gaming desktop, and a **753B GLM-5.2 on one workstation GPU**. If reproducible, it is the strongest statement of the year's "open weights → deployable local software" trend; the top-end claims are the ones to audit.

### 18. A Survey of On-Policy Distillation for Large Language Models — Mingyang Song, Mao Zheng — [arXiv:2604.00626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00626) — survey (ongoing work), Apr 2026
Frames static teacher-text imitation as structurally flawed for long reasoning: training on flawless teacher prefixes while generating one's own at inference compounds error, with exposure bias scaling roughly with the square of sequence length; on-policy distillation instead has the teacher score the student's own trajectories, reducing the compounding term toward linear.
**Evidence/why it matters:** OPD is the fastest-growing efficiency-adjacent technique in the window (OpenAlex normalized share **+166%**; 104 ICML 2026 title matches on "distill"), and this survey is the organizing reference. But it is a survey of a moving target and — critically — the strongest 2026 mechanism papers ([Does OPD Really Distill?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31046), [Rethinking OPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13016)) find that a large share of OPD's gain is *not* teacher transfer but self-suppression of low-probability tokens and distributional alignment, so read this survey alongside the skeptical follow-ups.

## 5. Caveats

1. **Evidence is thin on third-party replication.** Almost all 2026 numbers here are author-reported from technical reports or preprints; as of compilation I found no independent reproduction of the DeepSeek-V4 27%/10% claim, the MSA 28.4x claim, or the FreeToken 753B-on-one-GPU claim. Treat frontier-lab efficiency numbers as *claims*.
2. **Single-point speedups.** Most wall-clock claims (5–6x spec decoding, 14x sparse prefill, 1.78x TTFT) are measured at one batch size, sequence length, GPU and backend. High-concurrency serving changes the picture, and verification/scoring passes can erase theoretical gains (MSA and Random Attention both document this honestly; many others do not).
3. **Benchmark composition is heterogeneous.** Quantization "average gap under 1%" and "matches BF16 within seed noise" are averages over selected models/tasks; the variance and the worst case are usually hidden. RULER/needle retrieval is a weak proxy for long-context reasoning.
4. **HF upvotes measure community attention, not importance or correctness** (the highest-upvoted efficiency paper, Kimi K3, is a model report; several rigorous negative results sit far lower). The harvested set is 4,486 papers over 185 days and skews toward big-lab releases.
5. **The ICML count is a title-regex, not a taxonomy.** Titles under-report topics discussed only in abstracts, and my categories overlap heavily (a "sparse-attention serving" paper counts in three buckets). The "largest bucket" claim is therefore sensitive to definition; I gave three definitions rather than assert one.
6. **Distillation is a training/efficiency hybrid.** Much of the 2026 "distillation" wave (on-policy distillation) is post-training/RL methodology, not inference compression; I included it because the area brief lists knowledge distillation, but a reader looking only for serving savings should weight it lower.
7. **Energy/carbon is the least settled subarea.** The papers I found are mostly syntheses/research agendas ([2609.05565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05565) explicitly claims no new measurements) or single-vendor methodologies; there is no agreed per-token energy benchmark yet.
8. **On-device claims are the hardest to verify** because they depend on specific consumer hardware, thermals, and quantization formats; the 753B GLM-5.2-on-workstation claim in particular needs auditing before it is repeated.
9. **I could not use the arXiv API** (parent constraint / rate limits); discovery relied on web search + direct arxiv.org HTML, so some late-September 2026 papers may be missing, and I did not compute citation counts (Semantic Scholar/OpenAlex indexing lags).
