# Domain Applications of AI (Oct 2025 – Oct 2026)
### Finance · Legal · Economics/Markets · Energy · Networking/6G · Geospatial/EO · Agriculture · Games

*Scope: applications and serious evaluations of AI/LLMs **outside** science, medicine and robotics. Compiled 2026-10-03. Discovery via web search; content via arxiv.org/abs + /html. IDs are arXiv unless noted. All claims below are drawn from the papers' own abstracts/results and should be read as author-reported.*

---

## 1. Synthesis — what is real, what is hype

**The real story is benchmarks and harnesses, not headline capabilities.** Every subfield I surveyed converged on the same move: replace static Q&A leaderboards with domain-grounded, closed-loop, tool-using evaluation. Earth-Agent (ICLR 2026) wires 104 expert tools into an MCP ecosystem and ships Earth-Bench (248 curated tasks, 13,729 images, dual-level outcome + trajectory scoring). Orak (ICLR 2026) does the same for games with 12 real titles, MCP plug-and-play agents, an Elo arena and a fine-tuning corpus. 6GAgentGym builds a 42-tool closed-loop network-management gym with a learned NS-3 experiment model; RestoreBench, CONDUCTOR, TraderBench and PLawBench each formalise a previously anecdotal workflow. MCP-style tool calling is now the de-facto substrate across domains. Genuine deployments exist but are narrow and heavily instrumented: CONDUCTOR ran an LLM orchestrator against a real Danish island 60 kV network with a year of smart-meter data, and NAVI-Orbital (Gemma-3 VLM) performed onboard scene classification/description in LEO — a real milestone, but a single satellite system, not a capability proof.

**The hype is concentrated where money and status are attached to "AI agents": trading, economic simulation, and agriculture/EO foundation models.** TraderBench shows that 7/12 models score a flat ~32–34 on crypto across four escalating market-manipulation transforms — i.e. they are "robust" only because they barely trade — and that the conceptual-vs-computational gap is ~54 points (P&L accuracy 80–93 vs Greeks 18–53), persisting even with an options-pricing tool. The Alpha Illusion (a position paper) documents that FinMem's return drops ~71.85% and QuantAgent's Sharpe ~51.48% once the evaluation window crosses model pretraining cutoffs, and that multi-agent debate wins <20% of 36 configurations. A companion audit (Agentic Trading) screens 77 trading-agent studies and finds only 2/19 in the credible subset report extractable time-consistent splits — "protocol incomparability," not reproducible alpha. In economics, Competitive Market Behavior of LLMs replicates Smith's classic double auction with LLM traders and finds no condition reaches full competitive equilibrium; price dispersion stays ~3× the human benchmark, and the most capable models can be the least efficient market participants. Agent Bazaar documents destructive undercutting below unit cost and Sybil deception, and shows hand-built "guardrails" are fragile; only targeted RL produced an economically aligned 9B agent. In agriculture/EO, a systematic study finds general Earth-observation foundation models often lose to a frozen tabular foundation model and degrade under regional shift, and a SIGSPATIAL benchmark finds Prithvi/SpectralGPT/SatMAE all collapse when train/test splits are assigned to unseen U.S. regions.

**Netting out:** the durable contributions are (a) instrumented domain environments, (b) leakage- and execution-aware evaluation protocols, and (c) small, domain-trained or harness-constrained agents. The claims to discount are "alpha," "profitable autonomous trading," "LLMs as valid economic subjects," "foundation models generalise everywhere," and any single-run "first." Legal AI sits in between: frontier models are useful but unreliable — PLawBench sees a universal 6–8% drop under ordered multi-step constraints, and DeonticBench's best hard-subset score is 44.4% (SARA Numeric) / 46.6 macro-F1 (Housing), with RL still failing to make symbolic (Prolog) rule reasoning reliable.

## 2. Ranked papers (14)

> Format: **Title** — First author et al. — arXiv ID + URL — Venue — contribution — evidence.

**1. Earth-Agent: Unlocking the Full Landscape of Earth Observation with Agents** — Peilin Feng et al. — [2509.23141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23141) — ICLR 2026 — First agentic EO framework unifying RGB and raw spectral data inside an MCP tool ecosystem; dynamically orchestrates expert models for geophysical parameter retrieval and spatiotemporal reasoning; ships Earth-Bench with a dual-level protocol that scores both reasoning trajectories and final answers, explicitly penalising tool order and parameter errors.
*Evidence:* 104 tools in five kits; Earth-Bench = 248 expert-curated tasks / 13,729 images / 14 task types (classification→temperature→forecasting); authors report SOTA backbones still score low and that outcome-only metrics miss much of the failure. Strong venue, systematic trajectory-level eval.

**2. Orak: A Foundational Benchmark for Training and Evaluating LLM Agents on Diverse Video Games** — Dongmin Park et al. — [2506.03610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03610) — ICLR 2026 — 12 commercial video games across all six genres with MCP plug-and-play agentic modules, leaderboards, pairwise Elo arenas, ablations of modality/agent strategy, and a released expert-trajectory fine-tuning set that transfers gameplay meta-knowledge.
*Evidence:* 15 LLMs tested; all small open models (<8B) score ~0 on Pokémon/Minecraft/Stardew/StarCraft; fine-tuned LLaMA-3.2-3B improves on unseen games (3/5) and even on WebShop-E/H (0→8.4%/12.6%); real-time latency kills most models (all fail at hard difficulty; only GPT-4o stays perfect at easy); high-quality data beats data quantity.

**3. DeonticBench: A Benchmark for Reasoning over Rules** — Guangyao Dou et al. — [2604.04443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04443) — preprint — 6,232 tasks over U.S. federal tax, airline baggage, immigration and state housing law, with an optional LLM→Prolog→SWI-Prolog solver workflow and reference Prolog for every instance; exposes systematic failure modes in statute-grounded reasoning and in RL for symbolic program generation.
*Evidence:* best hard-subset results only 44.4% (SARA Numeric) and 46.6 macro-F1 (Housing); increased reasoning effort does not consistently help; SFT/DPO improve Prolog generation quality but not end-to-end accuracy; RL "still fail[s] to solve these tasks reliably."

**4. Competitive Market Behavior of LLMs** — Pawel Struski et al. — [2609.02580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02580) — preprint (cs.MA) — Replicates Smith (1962) double-auction experiments with LLM traders across three model populations and compares to human and theoretical benchmarks, measuring convergence, allocative efficiency and individual order behaviour. Introduces a reusable simulation framework and a "market-institution alignment" framing.
*Evidence:* no condition converges fully; α (price dispersion) stays 11.7–28.1 vs human 3.5–11.8 at round 5; best efficiency 0.91, GPT Large 0.36–0.67 because it only makes $0.01 improvements and never crosses the spread; the smallest model produced the most efficient market; second-mover anchoring makes agents concede rent.

**5. TraderBench: How Robust Are AI Agents in Adversarial Capital Markets?** — Xiaochuang Yuan et al. — [2603.00285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00285) — ICLR 2026 Agents-in-the-Wild Workshop (submitted) — Four-section finance benchmark (knowledge retrieval, analytical reasoning, options, crypto) built on A2A + six MCP servers, with judge-free performance scoring for trading, four progressive crypto-manipulation transforms, and a decomposed options score (P&L/Greeks/risk). Also quantifies LLM-judge variance on domain tasks.
*Evidence:* 7/12 models flat at ~32–34 across all manip transforms (<1 pt variation) = inert strategies; extended thinking gives +26.1 on retrieval but ~0 on trading (+0.3 crypto, −0.1 options); conceptual-vs-computational gap ≈54 pts and survives tool access (GPT-4o P&L 87.8 vs Greeks 17.8); three LLM judges spread 11 points overall.

**6. The Alpha Illusion: Reported Alpha from LLM Trading Agents Should Not Be Treated as Deployment Evidence** — Yuxuan Ye et al. — [2605.16895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16895) — preprint (position) — Systematises the confounds behind headline LLM-trading returns (temporal contamination, unmodelled frictions, short-window Sharpe uncertainty, narrative fitting, parametric priors) and proposes structural validity tests before any alpha claim is treated as evidence; a needed counterweight to FinCon/FinMem/TradingAgents-style headlines.
*Evidence:* FinMem total return −71.85% and QuantAgent Sharpe −51.48% once the window crosses the pretraining cutoff; multi-agent debate wins <20% of 36 model×benchmark configurations and more rounds/agents can degrade; FinBen's FinTrade Sharpe is used as an "anchor," not proof; LLM financial knowledge is firm-size/recency biased (54.17% large-cap 2017 vs 6.32% 1995).

**7. Agentic Trading: When LLM Agents Meet Financial Markets** — Yihan Xia et al. — [2605.19337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19337) — preprint (survey/audit) — A protocol-coded evidence map of 77 LLM-trading studies reframed as expert-system decision pipelines, with a strict inclusion boundary ("Action Output + Closed-Loop Evaluation") and an audit-oriented evidence matrix rather than a leaderboard.
*Evidence:* only 2/19 studies in the credible primary subset report extractable time-consistent split protocols — i.e. the headline finding is protocol incomparability. Best single citation for "the trading-agent literature is not yet comparable."

**8. CONDUCTOR: An LLM-Orchestrated Digital Twin for Uncertainty-Aware Distribution Grid Operations** — Antonio Alcántara et al. — [2606.24609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24609) — preprint — An open-weights LLM orchestrates power-flow analysis and optimisation solvers, adding uncertainty-aware studies (probabilistic security assessment, robust corrective dispatch, flexibility envelopes/hosting capacity); tested on a real network rather than a synthetic toy.
*Evidence:* Bornholm 60 kV real Danish island network + one year of smart-meter data; 67/68 prompts (98.5%) answered correctly on first attempt, 14/15 behavioural categories at 100%; tool descriptions alone were sufficient (ablating them did not change accuracy), and the key failure mode is over-acting/refusal calibration, not answer accuracy.

**9. RestoreBench: Can AI Agents Restore Power Flow Convergence?** — Riccardo Mansutti et al. — [2609.00384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00384) — Workshop on AI Foundations for Power Grids (preprint) — Evaluates LLM/agent architectures on a genuinely multi-step engineering task: diagnosing and repairing deliberately non-convergent power-flow cases within a constrained control action space, across chatbot / single-agent / multi-agent designs.
*Evidence:* two grids, 46 cases each, seven LLMs; tool-augmented agents beat the chatbot baseline, but multi-agent coordination "significantly increases cost without systematically improving performance" — single-agent is the efficient point; failure modes split into non-convergence, structured-output failures, invalid actions and timeouts (DeepSeek V3.2 frequently fails the terminal-output contract).

**10. Agent Bazaar: Enabling Economic Alignment in Multi-Agent Marketplaces** — Seth Karten et al. — [2605.17698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17698) — preprint — A multi-agent B2C/C2C simulation framework that defines "economic alignment" (preserving market stability and integrity) and isolates two failure modes: Algorithmic Instability ("The Crash": firms amplify price volatility until collapse) and Sybil Deception ("The Lemon Market": one agent with many seller identities floods fraudulent listings).
*Evidence:* LLM agents undercut below unit cost (opposite of human collusion pathology); hand-built stabilising "harnesses" help but remain fragile; REINFORCE++ with an adaptive curriculum yields a 9B model that beats all evaluated frontier/open-weight models; giving consumers more price visibility amplifies undercutting rather than fixing it.

**11. PLawBench: A Rubric-Based Benchmark for Evaluating LLMs in Real-World Legal Practice** — Yuzhen Shi et al. — [2601.16669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16669) — preprint — Models three real legal workflows (public consultation, ...) with fine-grained, rubric-based legal reasoning evaluation instead of coarse single-dimensional accuracy; shows rubric-free scoring can mark legally flawed but superficially plausible answers as correct.
*Evidence:* rubric scoring catches right-looking-but-flawed reasoning that accuracy-only evaluation misses; distinct capability profiles — GPT-5.2/Claude-4.5 lead on instruction adherence/procedural stability, Gemini/Qwen3-Max lead on deep deduction; a universal 6–8% drop under ordered multi-step ("syllogism") constraints, worst on the reasoning dimension. (Together with DLawBench 2606.13931, Magis-Bench 2605.08437, CLAUSE 2511.00340, LegalRewardBench 2609.14739 this marks legal's shift to multi-turn/grounded/abstention-aware evaluation.)

**12. CrossEarth-SAR: A SAR-Centric and Billion-Scale Geospatial Foundation Model for Domain Generalizable Semantic Segmentation** — Ziqi Ye et al. — [2603.12008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12008) — preprint — A billion-scale SAR vision foundation model with a physics-guided sparse MoE, pretrained on a 200K weakly/fully supervised SAR corpus, plus a 22-sub-benchmark / 8-domain-gap suite — the first unified standard for SAR domain-generalisation segmentation.
*Evidence:* SOTA on 20/22 settings; +8.4% mIoU (VV→full-pol), +15.5% (HH→full-pol), +11.9% (full→HH), and a 90M variant beats 300M DINOv2/v3 by 11.7% on HH2F; complex-to-real transfers +5.1%/+4.8%. Note: no peer-review venue listed, and gains are on the authors' own benchmark suite.

**13. 6GAgentGym: Tool Use, Data Synthesis, and Agentic Learning for Network Management** — Jiao Chen et al. — [2603.29656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29656) — preprint — A closed-loop 6G network-management gym with 42 typed tools (read-only vs state-mutating), an NS-3-calibrated learned experiment model, and a data-synthesis + SFT/RL pipeline; accompanied by 6GAgentBench. Effectively the network-operations analogue of Orak.
*Evidence:* 53k trajectories across five domains (slicing, edge offloading, UAV control, degradation recovery, multi-agent coordination) with a 30/45/25 L1/L2/L3 difficulty mix; SFT + online RL lets an 8B open model match GPT-5's overall success rate and do better on long-horizon tasks. Front-running task selection (exclude >80% zero-shot) is a sensible anti-saturation filter.

**14. Foundation Models Meet Agriculture: Challenges Beyond Pretraining** — Vishal Nedungadi et al. — [2608.30392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30392) — preprint — A systematic, deterministic multi-seed evaluation of Earth-observation foundation models on agricultural monitoring, arguing the failures are structural (landscape heterogeneity, modality mismatch, task-specific adaptation) rather than mere geographic shift; finds a domain-agnostic tabular FM competitive with or better than EO FMs.
*Evidence:* FMs lose up to ~0.16 macro-F1 vs baselines in Kenya/France even at ~100% modality coverage; under low modality coverage (Argentina) the split is severe; only the fully-covered Germany phenology task shows frozen-FM features beating a random forest. Pairs with a SIGSPATIAL-submitted benchmark (2606.29664) where Prithvi/SpectralGPT/SatMAE all degrade when train/val/test are assigned to unseen U.S. regions.

## 4. Papers I read IN FULL (4)

1. **Competitive Market Behavior of LLMs** (2609.02580) — full HTML text incl. all result tables, behavioural analysis and the CoT lexical analysis. The most rigorous "LLM agents as economic actors" paper I found: explicit human and theory benchmarks, 10 sims/condition, honest about the design not establishing a scale effect.
2. **TraderBench** (2603.00285) — full text incl. the judge-reliability section and per-model leaderboard. Directly reusable for anyone tempted to quote a trading-agent number.
3. **DeonticBench** (2604.04443) — full text incl. hard-set construction, Prolog pipeline and full results matrix. The clearest evidence that RL does not yet solve statute-grounded symbolic reasoning.
4. **Orak** (2506.03610) — full text incl. per-game results, battle arena, modality ablation, fine-tuning/OOD and latency study. Strongest positive result in this review (meta-knowledge transfer), with plenty of caveats in its own limitations section.

## 5. Caveats

- **Discovery, not a census.** This is a targeted sweep of a very large space; many strong papers (especially non-English/regional venues and IEEE/ACM journals) are almost certainly missed. Venue labels come from arXiv comments + proceedings links; a "preprint" may have been accepted since.
- **Peer review varies wildly.** Only Earth-Agent and Orak are confirmed top-conference papers here; several others are preprints or workshop submissions. Treat benchmark SOTA numbers accordingly.
- **Author-reported evidence.** All numbers are from the papers. I did not rerun any code. Contamination/leakage is a live risk precisely in finance, so even the critiques should be read critically.
- **Finance is a minefield.** Headline Sharpe ratios are not deployment evidence (Alpha Illusion, Agentic Trading); the field lacks standard split/execution protocols. I deliberately ranked the skeptics highly.
- **"First" claims** (NAVI-Orbital onboard VLM, Earth-Agent's agentic EO, CrossEarth-SAR's billion-scale SAR FM) are as-reported and hard to verify independently.
- **Simulation ≠ reality.** Economic, agricultural and market results are largely simulation/benchmark-based; real-world deployment evidence (CONDUCTOR) is rare and single-site.
- **Search-tool bias.** web_search surfaced a mix of Semantic Scholar, publisher and arXiv mirrors; I avoided export.arxiv.org per instructions and verified abstracts on arxiv.org/abs where possible.
- **Cutoff.** Items dated through late Sept 2026; anything appearing in the final days of Sept / early Oct 2026 may be missed.
