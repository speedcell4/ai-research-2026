## 1. Synthesis — what is genuinely happening, and what is hype

**The single most important fact in this area is that the "post-Transformer" story is, empirically, a *hybridization* story, not a replacement story.** The most comprehensive artifact of the year, the survey *The Evolution of Attention in Large Language Models* ([2609.39661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39661)), reconstructs 59 release-level records across 14 model lineages plus 11 open-weight endpoints and reaches an explicitly non-triumphal conclusion: attention design shows **"diversification rather than convergence"** and **"increasing hybridization"**, with network *depth* emerging as the coordination axis for memory (not a single winning token-mixing primitive). The frontier models confirm this. Kimi K3 is a 2.8T-parameter MoE with 104B activated, built on **Kimi Delta Attention + Gated MLA + Attention Residuals + Stable LatentMoE (16 of 896 routed experts)**, trained with **Per-Head Muon**, and reports ~2.5× better scaling efficiency than K2 ([2607.24653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24653)). Qwen3.8-Flash-Next is a 125B/A6B MoE whose token mixing is a **layer-wise hybrid of Gated DeltaNet and global attention (one full-attention layer in four)**, with full-attention layers later swapped for Qwen Sparse Attention ([2608.30320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30320)). NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Super/Ultra are explicitly **MoE hybrid Mamba-Transformer** models ([2604.12374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12374), [2606.15007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15007)), and MiniMax deployed blockwise sparse attention on top of GQA for million-token contexts ([2606.13392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13392)). So the honest reading is: *full attention is retained at strategic depth while recurrent/linear/sparse layers absorb the bulk of the sequence work*; the viral "Transformer is dead" framing is contradicted by the shipped architectures themselves.

**The second real trend is that the data wall became a first-class research object rather than a talking point.** The ICLR 2026 Oral *Pre-training under infinite compute* ([2509.14786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14786)) states the premise outright — web text grows ~1.03×/year while pretraining compute grows ~4×/year — and shows that standard data-constrained recipes (more epochs, more parameters) eventually overfit, that the **optimal weight decay is ~30× larger than standard practice**, and that a recipe combining epoching, regularization, parameter scaling and *ensembling* reaches a 200M-token loss asymptote with **5.17× less data**; ensembles can be distilled into an 8×-smaller student retaining 83% of the benefit. Alongside this sit *Prescriptive Scaling Laws for Data Constrained Training* ([2605.01640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01640), which models an additive overfitting penalty under repetition and shows that beyond a point further repetition is counterproductive), *Relative Scaling Laws* ([2510.24626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24626), matched-compute IsoFLOP sweeps showing scale is **not** a universal equalizer), and a genuinely theoretical treatment of the "less is more" paradox ([2511.03492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03492)). On the corpus side the year produced Common Corpus (~2T open/permissively licensed tokens, ICLR 2026 Oral, [2506.01732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01732)), Nemotron-CC-Math (133B math tokens, [2508.15096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15096)), and MixtureVitae (permissive-first web scale, [2509.25531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25531)). Data quality/curation is no longer infrastructure trivia; it has its own scaling theory.

**The third real trend is that Muon-class (matrix-aware) optimizers crossed from curiosity to frontier practice, and the theory/scaling community noticed.** *The Polar Express* ([2505.16932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16932)) received an ICLR 2026 Outstanding Paper **Honorable Mention** for replacing heuristic Newton–Schulz iterations in Muon with a minimax-optimal polynomial update that survives bfloat16 ([ICLR blog](https://blog.iclr.cc/2026/04/23/announcing-the-iclr-2026-outstanding-papers/)). NVIDIA's *SOAP, Muon, and Beyond* ([2607.20548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20548)) fixes SOAP instability at large batch sizes with per-step QR orthogonalization and reports stable multi-billion-parameter / trillion-token training; and *Same Architecture, Different Capacity* ([2605.21803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21803)) shows the *same* architecture realizes different spectral scaling exponents under AdamW vs Muon (hard-rank β = 0.44 vs 1.02 on tail representations). Muon also appears inside Kimi K3 as "Per-Head Muon." At the same time, the year's optimizer survey ([2608.28557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28557)) and the OmniOpt benchmark/survey ([2607.04033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04033)) both resist triumphalism: the landscape is fragmented and most claimed gains are architecture- and scale-specific.

**Real vs. hype.** *Real:* (1) hybrid attention/SSM stacks shipping at frontier scale, with full attention deliberately retained; (2) MoE as the default and **sparsity as a new scaling axis** — *Optimal Sparsity of MoE LMs for Reasoning* (ICLR 2026 Oral) shows that at equal loss, more **active** FLOPs improves reasoning while memorization and reasoning want different tokens-per-parameter, revising classical compute-optimal advice; (3) Muon/matrix-aware optimizers validated at scale; (4) data-constrained recipe science with reproducible interventions and ~5× data-efficiency gains; (5) sharp theory — succinctness, coverage, memorization capacity (~3.6 bits/parameter), optimizer-induced spectral scaling. *Overclaimed:* (a) **"a new architecture replaces the Transformer"** — the shipped systems are hybrids that keep softmax attention, and the survey's own summary is diversification, not convergence; (b) **small-scale architecture demos with huge community upvotes** — HRM-Text (322 upvotes, [2605.20613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20613)) is explicitly an "empirical existence proof" at small scale, and BDH-CQ (796 upvotes, [2608.09888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09888)) is a 150M-parameter ARC-AGI cost-efficiency result, not a general capability win; (c) **sparse-attention "free lunch" claims** — *Full Attention Strikes Back* ([2605.16928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16928)) argues full-attention LLMs are *already* intrinsically sparse and need adaptation, which cuts both ways; (d) **single-family scaling laws** — *Relative Scaling Laws* had to be run across 255 models at matched compute to reveal that aggregate curves hide diverging subpopulation gaps, and the paper was desk-rejected despite a 7.5 review score, a reminder that scaling-law results attract unusually high variance; (e) **expressivity ⇒ capability** inferences — Mamba-3's state-tracking wins and the Laplacian-smoothing theory for Mamba are real but narrow, and Mamba-3's headline gains are measured at ~1.5B scale.

## 2. Ranked most-important papers

1. **Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence** — Kimi Team (Tongtong Bai et al.) — [2607.24653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24653) — arXiv technical report (2026-07-27); HF Daily Papers 525 upvotes. A 2.8T-parameter MoE with 104B activated, native vision and a 1M-token context, built on Kimi Delta Attention + Attention Residuals and Stable LatentMoE (16 of 896 routed experts), trained with Per-Head Muon. **Evidence/why it matters:** reports ~2.5× better scaling efficiency than Kimi K2 and frontier-level long-horizon coding (Terminal-Bench 2.1 88.3, behind GPT-5.6 Sol's 88.8) while still trailing top proprietary models; it is the clearest single artifact that the 2026 frontier is *hybrid attention + huge sparse MoE + matrix-aware optimizer*, not a new primitive. **Read: full-text HTML (selected sections).**

2. **Mamba-3: Improved Sequence Modeling using State Space Principles** — Aakash Lahoti, Kevin Y. Li, Berlin Chen et al. (CMU/Princeton/Together) — [2603.15569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15569) — ICLR 2026 **Oral** ([proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/8abd2043b71a074278d5f687947bff9c-Abstract-Conference.html)); HF Daily Papers. Three SSM-viewpoint changes — exponential-trapezoidal discretization (a more expressive recurrence), complex state updates for state tracking, and a multi-input/multi-output formulation for hardware-efficient decoding. **Evidence:** at 1.5B scale Mamba-3 (MIMO) improves downstream accuracy by **+2.2 over Transformers, +1.9 over Mamba-2, +1.8 over Gated DeltaNet**, and with state size 64 matches Mamba-2's perplexity at state size 128; it solves synthetic state-tracking tasks Mamba-2 cannot. **Caveat:** headline numbers are small-model; it does not displace attention at frontier scale. **Read: full-text HTML.**

3. **Pre-training under infinite compute** — Konwoo Kim, Suhas Kotha, Percy Liang, Tatsunori Hashimoto — [2509.14786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14786) — ICLR 2026 **Oral**. Studies the fixed-data / unbounded-compute regime, shows epoching and parameter scaling eventually overfit, and fixes it with much stronger regularization (**optimal weight decay ~30× standard**). **Evidence:** the regularized recipe follows a clean power law in parameter count, so the authors estimate its *asymptote* rather than a fixed-budget point; ensembling lowers the asymptote further, and the best combined recipe reaches the 200M-token asymptote with **5.17× less data**, with gains transferring to downstream evals (+9% pre-training eval; 17.5× data-efficiency on math mid-training). **Why it matters:** it reframes scaling theory for a compute-rich, data-poor future and is the reference point for every data-constrained recipe paper this year. **Read: full-text HTML.**

4. **The Polar Express: Optimal Matrix Sign Methods and their Application to the Muon Algorithm** — Noah Amsel, David Persson, Christopher Musco, Robert M. Gower — [2505.16932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16932) — ICLR 2026 **Oral + Outstanding Paper Honorable Mention** ([award blog](https://blog.iclr.cc/2026/04/23/announcing-the-iclr-2026-outstanding-papers/)). Replaces the heuristic Newton–Schulz polynomial used for the polar decomposition inside Muon with a per-iteration minimax-optimal polynomial, proved worst-case optimal, with explicit finite-precision (bfloat16) handling. **Evidence:** consistent validation-loss improvements when training GPT-2 on 1B FineWeb tokens across learning rates; the ICLR committee called the empirical gains "at times modest" but praised the principled methodology for improving one of the field's most popular optimizers. **Read: abstract + award page; full text via [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2505.16932).**

5. **Transformers are Inherently Succinct** — Pascal Bergsträßer, Ryan Cotterell, Anthony Widjaja Lin — [ICLR 2026 Outstanding Paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/5f7804e8855efe5554025217abc49315-Abstract-Conference.html) (no arXiv version found) — ICLR 2026 **Outstanding Paper** ([award blog](https://blog.iclr.cc/2026/04/23/announcing-the-iclr-2026-outstanding-papers/)). Proposes *succinctness* as the right measure of Transformer expressive power, proving Transformers represent formal languages far more succinctly than finite automata or LTL — at the cost that verification is provably EXPSPACE-complete. **Why it matters:** the award committee explicitly read it as an explanation for why Transformers remain dominant versus RNNs/alternatives; it is the strongest theoretical counterweight to architecture-replacement narratives. **Read: abstract + proceedings page + award rationale (full text not retrievable).**

6. **The Coverage Principle: How Pre-Training Enables Post-Training** — Fan Chen, Audrey Huang, Noah Golowich, Sadhika Malladi et al. (MIT/MSR) — [2510.15020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15020) — ICLR 2026 **Oral**. Argues cross-entropy is a poor predictor of downstream performance and replaces it with **coverage** — the probability mass the pretrained model puts on high-quality responses — which is necessary and sufficient for post-training and Best-of-N test-time scaling to work. **Evidence:** proves coverage generalizes in a nearly horizon-independent way for autoregressive linear models (unlike cross-entropy), and gives provable interventions: model/checkpoint selection, gradient normalization, and decoding strategies. **Why it matters:** it is the year's cleanest theory linking pretraining to post-training/test-time compute, and it explains why small curated datasets can beat large ones. **Read: full-text HTML (selected sections).**

7. **Scaling with Collapse: Efficient and Predictable Training of LLM Families** — Shane Bergsma, Bin Claire Zhang, Nolan Dey et al. (NVIDIA) — [2509.25087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25087) — ICLR 2026 Poster (rating 8.0); [proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/b396d8821d3ac740db8b887b5eda6f18-Abstract-Conference.html). Shows whole training-loss *curves* collapse onto a universal trajectory across scales — but only when width, depth, LR, batch size and weight decay are jointly set optimally for the data budget. **Evidence:** collapse becomes a diagnostic (deviation flags pathologies) and enables early stopping for large-scale hyperparameter tuning; used to train the "Celerity" LLM family. **Why it matters:** turns a striking empirical curiosity into a practical compute-efficiency tool and constrains how much of scaling can be treated as a surface-level fit. **Read: abstract + ICLR abstract corpus.**

8. **Gated Attention for Large Language Models: Non-linearity, Sparsity, and Attention-Sink-Free** — Zihan Qiu, Zekun Wang, Bo Zheng et al. (Qwen/Alibaba) — [2505.06708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06708) — **NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper** ([award blog](https://blog.neurips.cc/2025/11/26/announcing-the-neurips-2025-best-paper-awards/)). A head-specific sigmoid gate applied after scaled dot-product attention consistently improves 30 variants across 15B MoE and 1.7B dense models trained on 3.5T tokens, improving stability, tolerating larger LRs, and mitigating attention sinks and massive activations. **Evidence/why it matters:** the same SDPA-output gating is used in Qwen3-Next; this is the year's best example of a cheap architectural change with industrial-scale evidence and immediate adoption, and it strengthens the "hybrid refinements, not replacements" reading. **Read: abstract + NeurIPS award rationale.**

9. **MiniMax Sparse Attention** — Xunhao Lai, Weiqi Xu, Yufeng Yang et al. (MiniMax) — [2606.13392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13392) — arXiv technical report (2026-06-11); HF Daily Papers 169 upvotes. A blockwise sparse attention on top of GQA: a lightweight Index Branch scores KV blocks and independently selects Top-k per GQA group (group-specific retrieval), and a Main Branch does exact block-sparse attention over the selected blocks. **Why it matters:** a rare fully-deployed sparse-attention design for hundred-thousand-to-million-token agentic/repo workloads; it is evidence that the systems bottleneck (not a new primitive) is driving attention research. **Read: abstract.**

10. **SOAP, Muon, and Beyond: Pushing LLM Pretraining Scales** — Mikail Khona, Aditya Vavre, Boxiang Wang et al. (NVIDIA) — [2607.20548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20548) — arXiv preprint (2026-07-13). Identifies SOAP instabilities at large batch sizes and fixes them with per-step QR orthogonalization plus better preconditioning, then runs a unified SOAP/Muon/AdamW study using update-RMS matching for fair LR transfer, including an empirical audit of Muon's orthogonalization quality. **Evidence:** stable multi-billion-parameter, trillion-token pretraining without loss spikes (MXFP8 settings). **Why it matters:** the strongest 2026 industrial evidence that higher-order preconditioned optimizers now work at scale — which is why Muon-family methods appear in frontier training stacks. **Read: full-text HTML (selected sections).**

11. **Optimal Sparsity of Mixture-of-Experts Language Models for Reasoning Tasks** — Nakamura, Ishikawa et al. — [ICLR 2026 Oral](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/ab5492f57995409351cbf1a1cdf5f1a4-Abstract-Conference.html). Trains MoE families varying total parameters, active parameters and top-k under fixed compute to separate pretraining loss from downstream accuracy. **Evidence:** two principles — (i) at equal loss, models with more **active** FLOPs reason better; (ii) memorization improves with more parameters while reasoning has an optimal tokens-per-parameter, and neither GRPO post-training nor more test-time compute changes this. **Why it matters:** it revises the classical compute-optimal picture by adding a sparsity axis, exactly the axis frontier MoE systems are now tuned on. **Read: abstract + ICLR corpus.**

12. **SMELT: Scaling Laws for Compute-Matched MoE Looped Transformers** — Shaowen Wang, Ge Zhang, Kairong Luo et al. — [2609.01343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01343) — arXiv preprint (2026-09-01); HF Daily Papers 80 upvotes. Studies layer looping in MoE Transformers while matching per-token FLOPs, non-embedding parameters and KV cache; the SMELT recipe loops the middle half of layers twice. **Evidence:** scales to 54B non-embedding parameters, fits a separate Chinchilla-style law per architecture, and saves **6.8–18.0% of training FLOPs** on the compute-optimal frontier, with downstream gains beyond what validation loss predicts. **Why it matters:** it is one of the few architecture-vs-FLOPs studies that controls the budget honestly, and it argues looping is a real efficiency margin rather than a free-lunch depth trick. **Read: abstract.**

13. **Nemotron 3 Super: Open, Efficient Mixture-of-Experts Hybrid Mamba-Transformer Model** — NVIDIA — [2604.12374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12374) — arXiv technical report (2026-04-14); companion *Nemotron 3 Ultra* [2606.15007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15007). Open-weight hybrid MoE + Mamba-Transformer stack aimed at agentic reasoning. **Why it matters:** an independent (non-Qwen, non-Kimi) production confirmation that Mamba/attention interleaving is being standardized in open frontier-class models, not just proposed in papers. **Read: abstract.**

14. **In-Place Test-Time Training** — Guhao Feng, Shengjie Luo, Kai Hua et al. — [2604.06169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06169) — ICLR 2026 **Oral**. Makes an LLM's *final MLP projection matrix* the adaptable "fast weights" at inference, with a next-token-prediction-aligned objective and chunk-wise updates compatible with context parallelism. **Evidence:** a drop-in enhancement enables a 4B model to handle contexts up to 128K better, and pretraining from scratch beats prior TTT approaches. **Why it matters:** TTT moved from a memory module rewrite to a lightweight, architecture-compatible add-on — a pattern that recurs across the year's "efficiency-adjacent" architecture work. **Read: abstract + ICLR corpus.**

15. **Common Corpus: The Largest Collection of Ethical Data for LLM Pre-Training** — Pierre-Carl Langlais, Pavel Chizhov, Catherine Arnett et al. — [2506.01732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01732) — ICLR 2026 **Oral** ([proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/2b5c5689fae6fa9a4883e73e511d52c8-Abstract-Conference.html)). Assembles ~2T tokens of uncopyrighted/permissively licensed multilingual and code data with documented provenance and filtering. **Evidence:** two small models trained on it perform comparably to peers of their size. **Why it matters:** legal compliance has become a first-class pretraining-data constraint; Common Corpus is the anchor open resource for that world. **Read: abstract.**

16. **Superposition Yields Robust Neural Scaling** — Yizhou Liu, Ziming Liu, Jeff Gore — [2505.10465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10465) — NeurIPS 2025 **Oral** ([proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/e97ac22927560eb2de6b658498cbc575-Abstract-Conference.html)). Uses weight decay to control the degree of representation superposition in Anthropic-style toy models. **Evidence:** when superposition is weak the loss follows a power law only for power-law feature frequencies, but under **strong superposition the loss generically scales inversely with dimension** across a broad class of frequency distributions because of geometric overlap between representation vectors. **Why it matters:** a mechanistic origin story for neural scaling that does not require the data to have a special spectrum. **Read: abstract.**

17. **How much do language models memorize?** — John X. Morris, Chawin Sitawarin, Chuan Guo et al. (Meta FAIR / Google DeepMind / Cornell / NVIDIA) — [2505.24832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24832) — ICML 2026 **Distinguished Paper Honorable Mention** ([ICML 2026 award news](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3883532461961473), [poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/62989)). Formally separates *unintended memorization* (information about a specific dataset) from *generalization* (information about the true data-generating process) and measures capacity by eliminating generalization. **Evidence:** estimates **~3.6 bits per parameter** for GPT-style models; across hundreds of 0.5M–1.5B models, models memorize until capacity fills and then grokking begins. **Why it matters:** gives pretraining a quantitative capacity budget and connects memorization, scaling and grokking in one framework. **Read: abstract + award summary.**

18. **Why Less is More (Sometimes): A Theory of Data Curation** — Elvis Dohmatob, Mohammad Pezeshki, Reyhane Askari-Hemmat — [2511.03492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03492) — ICLR 2026 Poster (rating 7.5) ([proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/5af28a311a6ae8b30ab4941a96675fa2-Abstract-Conference.html)). Models curation with an imperfect oracle that selects examples by difficulty and correctness, deriving exact test-error scaling laws for label-agnostic and label-aware rules. **Evidence:** identifies precise phase-transition curves in data size × quality where small curated datasets beat full datasets, validated on ImageNet, and reconciles conflicting LIMO/s1-style "less is more" observations in LLM math reasoning. **Why it matters:** it turns the data-curation wars into a testable theory with conditions, rather than a recipe debate. **Read: full-text HTML.**

- **OmniOpt: Taxonomy, Geometry, and Benchmarking of Modern Optimizers** — Siyuan Li et al. — [2607.04033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04033) — survey/benchmark of 100+ optimizers; unifies them via norm-constrained linear minimization oracles.
- **Blog: Survey of Optimizers** — Ruoran Xu — [2608.28557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28557) — organizes 2025–26 optimizers along temporal estimation, update geometry, horizon management, and representation/systems; explicitly non-triumphal.
- **WSM: Decay-Free Learning Rate Schedule via Checkpoint Merging** — [ICLR 2026 Oral](https://openreview.net/forum?id=HhThhjKyfw) — LR decay re-expressed as principled model averaging; +3.5 MATH, +2.9 HumanEval, +5.5 MMLU-Pro over WSD.
- **Taming Momentum: Low-Rank Optimizer States (LoRA-Pre)** — [ICLR 2026 Oral](https://openreview.net/forum?id=9Q0dNBYeEY) — recasts Adam/Muon EMA as online linear regression and stores momentum in a low-rank subspace (1/8 rank), from 60M to 1B.
- **How Learning Rate Decay Wastes Your Best Data in Curriculum-Based LLM Pretraining** — [ICLR 2026 Oral](https://openreview.net/forum?id=T5wkZJqzkz) — curriculum's advantage disappears under LR decay; model averaging recovers it.
- **StochasTok** — Sanghyun Ahn et al. — [2506.01687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01687) — stochastic token splitting during training improves subword tasks (character counting, substring identification) and can be applied post-hoc to existing models. Tokenizer research remains thin relative to architecture/data work.
- **In Search of Adam's Secret Sauce** — [NeurIPS 2025 Oral](https://openreview.net/forum?id=CH72XyZs4y) — mechanistic decomposition of Adam.
- **Does Reinforcement Learning Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model?** — Yang Yue et al. — NeurIPS 2025 **Best Paper Runner-Up** ([award blog](https://blog.neurips.cc/2025/11/26/announcing-the-neurips-2025-best-paper-awards/)) — the strongest post-training-theory negative result of the window: RLVR improves sampling efficiency but does not expand the base model's reasoning boundary, whereas distillation does.
- **DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash** — DeepSeek-AI — [2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19969) — pushing KV-cache compression for input-heavy long-horizon agents; the DeepSeek V4 line is widely discussed but the model's main report was not retrievable as a citable arXiv page during this run (Chinese-language coverage: [qbitai](https://www.qbitai.com/2026/04/406809.html)).
- **Gemma 4 Technical Report** — Gemma Team — [2607.02770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02770) — dense + MoE open weights, 2.3B–31B, natively multimodal; useful as an open reference point for 2026 pretraining recipes.

## 5. Caveats

- **Rate limiting shaped coverage.** The parent's arXiv API job caused HTTP 429 on arxiv.org/search and export.arxiv.org throughout. I could still fetch arxiv.org/abs/<ID> and arxiv.org/html/<ID> directly, but *discovery* depended on web search and the shared local corpora. A small number of genuinely important papers may be missing simply because I never learned their IDs.
- **HF upvotes are a popularity signal, not quality.** The top of the HF Daily Papers chart in this window is dominated by agents/RSI/multimodal; within my area the highest-upvote items are small-scale architecture existence proofs (BDH-CQ 796, HRM-Text 322, NCP 330). I deliberately demoted these relative to their upvotes and tried to say why.
- **Conference corpora cutoffs.** ICML 2026 in the local data is partial (418 of ~6,600 accepted titles), and ICLR/NeurIPS ratings are reviewer scores, not outcomes; "Desk Reject" papers can carry high ratings. Venue attributions come from these corpora and award pages and could be wrong in edge cases.
- **Small-scale is not general.** Mamba-3's headline deltas are at ~1.5B; HRM-Text, BDH-CQ and most "new architecture" results are at ≤ a few billion parameters. None of the non-hybrid alternatives has a credible frontier-scale win in what I saw.
- **Scaling-law fragility.** Relative Scaling Laws was desk-rejected despite a 7.5 score; Scaling with Collapse reports a collapse condition (optimal hyperparameters) that is easy to violate; data-constrained laws (5.17×, 30× weight decay) come from a single data seed of 200M tokens and one research group's compute budget.
- **Single-lab optimizer evidence.** SOAP/Muon, Polar Express, and optimizer-induced spectral scaling laws are each from one group (NVIDIA, or one academic collaboration) with architecture-specific setups; fair LR transfer across optimizers is itself an open methodological issue.
- **Selective reporting risk in frontier reports.** Kimi K3, Qwen3.8-Next, Nemotron 3 and MiniMax reports are self-reported technical reports. Their comparisons are chosen by the labs; e.g. K3's own report concedes it trails top proprietary models on overall performance.
- **Undefined terms of art.** "Infinite compute," "succinct," "coverage," "superposition" all have precise meanings inside their papers that differ from colloquial use; I have tried to attribute each to its source rather than generalizing.
