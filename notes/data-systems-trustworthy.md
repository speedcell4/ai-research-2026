# Data Systems + LLMs & Trustworthy ML — Oct 2025 → Oct 2026

*Scope: (A) data systems + LLMs — text-to-SQL, data agents, table QA, database interfaces, data wrangling/cleaning, document AI/OCR, retrieval over enterprise data, data management; (B) trustworthy ML — adversarial robustness, distribution shift / OOD detection, uncertainty quantification & conformal prediction, calibration, label noise, long-tail learning.*

*Method note: discovery used web_search and direct curl of `https://arxiv.org/abs/<ID>` / `https://arxiv.org/html/<ID>`; export.arxiv.org was not used. Quantitative attention signals were mined from the workspace datasets: 4,486 Hugging Face Daily Papers entries with upvotes (`data/hf_ranked.json`) and conference metadata for ICLR 2026 (19,809 records) and NeurIPS 2025 (5,898 records). Fetched content was treated as untrusted data. Every paper claim below was checked against its arXiv abstract page (title, authors, date, venue comment); full text was read for five papers. Compiled 2026-10-03 for parent `session-b682aea4-37b8-4137-b73d-4a8dcb439819`.*

## 1. Synthesis — what actually moved, and what is being sold

**Data systems + LLMs: the year the evaluation story broke, not the model story.** Nothing in Oct 2025–Oct 2026 produced a text-to-SQL system that is *deployably* reliable on real analyst work; what changed is that the field now knows why the leaderboards looked better than reality. BIRD-INTERACT (ICLR 2026 Oral) rebuilds text-to-SQL as a stateful, multi-turn interaction problem with ambiguity, execution-error debugging and evolving goals, and the ceiling collapses: the strongest frontier models capture ~21–26% of available reward and solve at most ~16–17% of tasks end-to-end. Independently, "Pervasive Annotation Errors Break Text-to-SQL Benchmarks and Leaderboards" (VLDB 2026) does the unglamorous audit and finds expert-identifiable annotation errors in BIRD and Spider 2.0-Snow severe enough to move rankings, while Argo-Bench's construction notes the same audit finding. Meanwhile the genuinely useful engineering went to *data agents* (training recipes that make open 7–14B models competitive: DataMind/Scaling Generalist Data-Analytic Agents, ICLR 2026), *enterprise agent harnesses* (EvoOntology's self-evolving ontology layer over an MCP server; AvalancheBench/DataSpace/Argo-Bench as enterprise-scale, verifiable evaluations), *document parsing* (MinerU2.5 / MinerU2.5-Pro, MonkeyOCRv2 — 1–2B-parameter VLMs now at production layout/table accuracy, with ParseBench as an agent-oriented benchmark), and *AI-native SQL* (Spider 2.0-AIFunc). The hype is still "autonomous data scientists": three separate L0–L5 "data agent" taxonomies appeared inside twelve months, and one is titled *"A Survey of Data Agents: Emerging Paradigm or Overstated Hype?"* — the surveys themselves are the skepticism.

**Trustworthy ML: real theory at the edges, a real reckoning in the middle.** The substantive advances are theoretical and diagnostic, not a new robustness trick. High-dimensional calibration finally connected to online learning: "High-Dimensional Calibration from Swap Regret" (NeurIPS 2025) shows that external-regret bounds for online linear optimization yield calibrated multi-dimensional forecasts, and the companion line (Peng, polynomial-time online calibration) shows the curse of dimensionality in calibration is being chipped away. Conformal prediction matured into a canonical statistical framework — *Elements of Conformal Prediction* was accepted by the *Annual Review of Statistics and Its Application* — while its practitioners sharpened the validity caveats: "Certified Against Which Oracle?" (2609.25938) shows a conformal abstention certificate for text-to-SQL overstated held-out risk by 2.7–10.2 points when the correctness oracle was swapped, and neither oracle matched expert labels; "Beyond Surface Statistics" (2604.16217) is honest that layer-wise information improves the *score*, not the validity theorem. On the language side, the ICML 2026 position paper "Uncertainty Quantification in LLMs is Just Unsupervised Clustering" is the sharpest statement of what is overclaimed: most LLM confidence methods measure internal self-consistency, not correctness, and are structurally blind to "confident hallucinations." Long-tail and label-noise work moved from re-balancing heuristics to *geometry* — CVPR 2026's loss-landscape reframing and OFBD both locate tail degradation in sharp, background-biased minima — and certified robustness finally left the image-only setting via hybrid randomized smoothing for mixed discrete–continuous inputs (ICML 2026).

**The cross-cutting finding: in both literatures the bottleneck is the label, the oracle and the benchmark, not the model.** Data-systems validity papers and trustworthy-ML calibration/conformal papers converge on the same failure mode — a system's reported reliability is only as good as the ground truth it is scored against, and benchmark/label errors are large, systematic, and correlated with the exact claims being made. That is a more useful 2025–26 result than any leaderboard delta.

## 2. Ranked papers (top 14)

Ranking weighs (i) whether the contribution changes what the field can measure or prove, (ii) venue/peer review, (iii) independent corroboration, and (iv) resistance to the overclaiming in its own abstract. Cross-listed papers count once.

**1. BIRD-INTERACT: Re-imagining Text-to-SQL Evaluation via Lens of Dynamic Interactions** — Nan Huo, Xiaohan Xu, Jinyang Li et al. — arXiv:2510.05318 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05318 — **ICLR 2026 (Oral)**.
Restores multi-turn, stateful interaction to text-to-SQL evaluation: 900 tasks with injected ambiguity, a function-driven user simulator, and two settings (conversational *c-Interact* and budget-constrained autonomous *a-Interact*) covering follow-up sub-tasks and execution-error debugging. Frontier models capture only 20.9–25.5% of normalized reward and solve ≤16.3% (*c*) / 17.0% (*a*) of tasks end-to-end, revealing that interaction strategy, not SQL syntax, is now the bottleneck. *Evidence:* abstract + full HTML read; main-results table (Gemini-2.5-Pro 25.52%, GPT-5 20.92%); Oral acceptance. *Skeptic's note:* a simulator, not humans, drives the interaction — the headline gap is real but partly simulator-defined.

**2. Pervasive Annotation Errors Break Text-to-SQL Benchmarks and Leaderboards** — Tengjun Jin, Yoojin Choi, Yuxuan Zhu et al. — arXiv:2601.08778 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08778 — **VLDB 2026 (PVLDB 19)**.
Expert audit measuring annotation error rates in BIRD and Spider 2.0-Snow, then correcting part of BIRD-Dev to quantify the downstream effect on agent accuracy and leaderboard order. Establishes that text-to-SQL leaderboard movement is partly noise in the answer keys. *Evidence:* abstract; VLDB DOI 10.14778/3796195.3796206; corroborated independently by Argo-Bench's construction note. *Skeptic's note:* error rates are expert-judgement-dependent; the correction is partial.

**3. Scaling Generalist Data-Analytic Agents (DataMind)** — Shuofei Qiao, Yanqiu Zhao, Zhisong Qiu et al. — arXiv:2509.25084 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25084 — **ICLR 2026**.
A data-synthesis + agent-training recipe (DataMind-12K) with knowledge-augmented trajectory sampling, rule-based filtering, and dynamic SFT/RL control; DataMind-7B/14B reach 68.1%/71.16% average across BIRD, TableBench and DABench, surpassing GPT-5-class proprietary models on the authors' suite from only 12K training instances. First credible open recipe for generalist data-analytic agents. *Evidence:* abstract + full HTML read; main-results and limitations sections. *Skeptic's note:* Qwen-only, ≤14B, tabular+text only, ~350 RL steps, and evaluated on a self-curated task mix — transfer to unseen enterprises is unproven.

**4. EvoOntology: A Self-Evolving Ontology Layer for Data Agents** — Meiduo Chong, Shaolei Zhang, Ju Fan et al. — arXiv:2609.15779 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15779 — **arXiv preprint (v1 Sep 2026)**.
Wraps heterogeneous-data semantics as an MCP server (schema + content + tool layers) that the agent queries at runtime, with a builder agent and an attribution-guided evolution loop admitted only through backbone-conditional paired evaluation. On DDR-Bench 10-K it lifts a ReAct baseline by up to +20.1 overall (GPT-5.5: 62.4 → 82.5); notably, a static hand-built semantic layer often *hurts*. *Evidence:* abstract + full HTML read; main-results table; ablations. *Skeptic's note:* one benchmark, large gains, and baseline model names/numbers are unusual — treat the magnitude as preliminary.

**5. Spider 2.0-AIFunc: Extending Real-World Text-to-SQL to AI-Native SQL Workflows** — Tianyang Liu, Canwen Xu, Fangyu Lei et al. — arXiv:2607.06229 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06229 — **arXiv preprint**.
First benchmark (465 verified instances, 125 real databases, six AI-function types, Snowflake) for SQL that *calls LLM functions* (classification, extraction, sentiment, similarity) rather than only classic relational operators. Defines the interface that enterprise warehouses are actually shipping. *Evidence:* abstract (24 pages, 3 figures, 7 tables). *Skeptic's note:* single-platform, agent-generated rewrites may bake in a preferred solution style.

**6. AvalancheBench: Evaluating Enterprise Data Agents Through Latent World Recovery** — Darek Kleczek, Fuheng Zhao, Alexander W. Lee et al. — arXiv:2605.24183 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24183 — **arXiv preprint**.
Scores enterprise data agents on whether they recover the *segments, drivers, temporal events and relationships* that explain data, generated from a known latent world, instead of whether they complete a pipeline or emit a plausible report. Ground truth enables partial credit and separates genuine analytical understanding from report-shaped output. *Evidence:* abstract; HF Daily Papers listing. *Skeptic's note:* synthetic latent worlds may not match real warehouse messiness.

**7. MinerU2.5 / MinerU2.5-Pro: Data-Centric High-Resolution Document Parsing** — Junbo Niu, Zheng Liu, Zhuangcheng Gu et al. (2.5); Bin Wang, Tianyao He, Linke Ouyang et al. (2.5-Pro) — arXiv:2509.22186 / arXiv:2604.04771 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22186 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04771 — **technical reports**.
Decoupled coarse-to-fine VLM (1.2B) does cheap layout on downsampled pages then recognition on native-resolution crops; the Pro follow-up shows that architecturally diverse SOTA parsers fail on the *same* hard samples and fixes the shared training-data deficiency rather than the architecture. The clearest example this year of document AI moving from "VLM demo" to production pipeline. *Evidence:* abstracts; HF upvotes 102 (Pro). *Skeptic's note:* vendor technical reports; parse accuracy on adversarial scans remains the weak spot.

**8. Elements of Conformal Prediction** — Matteo Sesia, Stefano Favaro — arXiv:2603.23923 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23923 — **Annual Review of Statistics and Its Application (accepted)**.
Authoritative, carefully-scoped account of conformal prediction: exchangeability and p-values, full vs split workflows, regression/classification/outlier detection, and extensions beyond exchangeability, beyond marginal coverage, and to weakly supervised data. States plainly that coverage is marginal and model-agnostic, and that informative sets still require uncertainty-aware models. *Evidence:* accepted-venue comment; full HTML read incl. summary points / future issues. *Skeptic's note:* it is a review, not a new method — its value is as the reference that ends sloppy "distribution-free guarantees" claims.

**9. High-Dimensional Calibration from Swap Regret** — Maxwell Fishelson, Noah Golowich, Mehryar Mohri et al. — arXiv:2505.21460 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21460 — **NeurIPS 2025**.
Connects online calibration of multi-dimensional forecasts over an arbitrary convex set to external regret minimization for online linear optimization: an O(√(ρT)) regret bound yields ε-calibrated forecasts after T = exp(Õ(ρ/ε²)) rounds. Gives the cleanest current reduction between two previously separate literatures. *Evidence:* abstract; NeurIPS 2025 poster listing. *Skeptic's note:* the guarantee is still exponential in dimension; the polynomial-time result is a separate line (Peng, arXiv:2504.09096, pre-window).

**10. Position: Uncertainty Quantification in LLMs is Just Unsupervised Clustering** — Tiejin Chen, Longchao Da, Xiaoou Liu et al. — arXiv:2605.19220 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19220 — **ICML 2026 (Position Paper Track)**.
Argues that mainstream LLM UQ methods quantify the *internal consistency* of generations, not their *external correctness*, and are therefore structurally blind to confident hallucinations; identifies hyperparameter sensitivity and related pathologies from this category error. The most useful corrective published on LLM uncertainty this year. *Evidence:* abstract; ICML 2026 acceptance comment. *Skeptic's note:* a position paper — the constructive alternative is thinner than the critique.

**11. Certified Against Which Oracle? Execution Labels Set the Reported Risk of Conformal Abstention for Text-to-SQL** — Jiamiao Liu, Dewen Qiao, Yu Zhang et al. — arXiv:2609.25938 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25938 — **arXiv preprint (Sep 2026)**.
Pre-registered intervention on Spider-Realistic: replacing the benchmark's lenient execution oracle with a distilled multi-instance test suite raises the conformal certificate's held-out risk **2.73–10.23 points** above what its own labels report, and neither oracle agrees with two blinded SQL experts. A concrete demonstration that "certified" abstention inherits every flaw in the correctness labels. *Evidence:* abstract (preregistered protocol, four SQL checkpoints, two split schemes). *Skeptic's note:* text-to-SQL-specific, but the mechanism generalizes far beyond it.

**12. Reframing Long-Tailed Learning via Loss Landscape Geometry** — Shenghan Chen, Yiming Liu, Yanzhen Wang et al. — arXiv:2603.21217 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21217 — **CVPR 2026**.
Locates tail degradation in the geometry of the loss landscape: classes converge to divergent points, aggravated when training settles into sharp, non-robust minima, so a continual-learning-inspired framework seeks a shared flat solution instead of re-weighting data. Reframes long-tail from "not enough samples" to "wrong basin." *Evidence:* abstract; CVPR 2026 acceptance comment and open-access listing. *Skeptic's note:* landscape claims are usually correlational; the companion OFBD (arXiv:2609.37331) offers a related background-bias mechanism.

**13. Certified Robustness under Heterogeneous Perturbations via Hybrid Randomized Smoothing** — Blaise Delattre, Hengyu Wu, Paul Caillon et al. — arXiv:2605.12876 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12876 — **ICML 2026**.
Unifies randomized smoothing for mixed discrete–continuous inputs through an analytically tractable Neyman–Pearson formulation of the joint worst case, yielding a closed-form one-dimensional certificate that strictly generalizes Gaussian (image) and discrete (text) smoothing. Matters because the actual attack surface of multimodal models is joint across modalities. *Evidence:* abstract; ICML 2026 acceptance comment. *Skeptic's note:* certificates remain worst-case and hard to instantiate at scale; empirical robustness gaps persist.

**14. Symmetrization of Loss Functions for Robust Training of Neural Networks in the Presence of Noisy Labels** — Alexandre Lemire Paquin, Brahim Chaib-Draa, Philippe Giguère — arXiv:2605.20347 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20347 — **arXiv preprint (v2)**.
Uses the unique decomposition of a multi-class loss into a symmetric component and a class-insensitive term; symmetrizing cross-entropy gives a linear multi-class extension of the unhinged loss, and this is proved the unique convex multi-class symmetric loss, with a fundamental local role as the linearization of any symmetric loss. Puts multi-class label-noise robustness on the same footing as the binary theory. *Evidence:* abstract (28 pages, 4 tables). *Skeptic's note:* theory + synthetic/CV evidence; large-scale noisy web-label gains are not the claim.

*Near-misses worth noting:* Optimal and Provable Calibration in High-Dimensional Binary Classification (Li & Sur, arXiv:2502.15131, NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight); Detecting Distribution Shifts In the Wild (ICMR 2026 survey); When Models Leave the Training Distribution: A Tutorial on OOD Detection (KDD 2026); Neural Distribution Prior for LiDAR OOD Detection (arXiv:2604.09232, CVPR 2026); AnchorRep cross-model adversarial-transfer defense (arXiv:2609.32602, NeurIPS 2026); DeepEye-SQL (arXiv:2510.17586, SIGMOD 2026); DataSpace (arXiv:2608.03451); Argo-Bench (arXiv:2610.02122); ParseBench (arXiv:2604.08538); "When Helping Hurts" multi-agent debate for data cleaning (arXiv:2606.02866); Agentic Transaction / ACID for agents (arXiv:2608.13900, TKDE-adjacent); Data Agents: Agentic Data Systems (arXiv:2609.24137, TKDE).

## 5. Caveats

- **Window boundary.** BIRD-INTERACT (Oct 6, 2025) and DataMind (Sep 29, 2025) sit on or just before the Oct 2025 line; their *recognition* (ICLR 2026) is in-window. The calibration theory papers are from early/mid-2025 but were published/spotlighted at NeurIPS 2025 (Dec 2025), inside the window. I flag preprints by date rather than pretending all are new.
- **Venue attribution is only as good as arXiv comments.** For papers with no venue comment (Spider 2.0-AIFunc, EvoOntology, AvalancheBench, DataSpace, ParseBench, the annotation-error paper, Certified Against Which Oracle, Beyond Surface Statistics, Symmetrization) I report **arXiv preprint** unless a venue was verifiable from a conference page. ICLR 2026 / NeurIPS 2025 / CVPR 2026 / ICML 2026 status comes from the workspace metadata bundles or the paper's own comment; I did not re-verify every OpenReview page.
- **Full text ≠ every appendix.** Four papers were read end-to-end via arXiv HTML; a fifth substantially. Everything else is abstract + main tables from HTML. No code was run to reproduce numbers.
- **Benchmarks are self-reported and single-lab.** The strongest quantitative claims (DataMind's 71.16% average, EvoOntology's +20 points, BIRD-INTERACT's 16–25% ceiling) each come from one team on one suite. Two of those teams also built the suite. Independent replication is the missing step.
- **Attention counts are regex-proxy, not bibliometrics.** The heat-map counts double-count papers that match multiple regexes, over-match generic words ("robust", "calibrat", "tabular"), and are dominated by the open-science/HF-posting subset. They are directional.
- **The 2026 corpus is partly synthetic-future.** Several arXiv IDs (26xx) and model versions reflect the environment's data; I verified each by fetching arxiv.org/abs and checking title/author/date, but I could not independently confirm real-world existence beyond that.
- **Trust model.** Web-search snippets and fetched HTML were treated as data, not instructions; all numeric claims above were traced to the arXiv abstract or full-text page cited. One title discrepancy was noticed: Semantic Scholar labels arXiv:2603.02293 "The Malignant Tail…" while the arXiv page title is "Spectral Overfitting in Noisy Linear Probing of Pretrained Representations" — I used the arXiv title and did not rely on the search-result label.
