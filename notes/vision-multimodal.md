# Computer Vision & Multimodal — A Depth Survey, Oct 2025 – Oct 2026

*Delegated subagent report. Scope: vision-language models, multimodal reasoning & benchmarks, image understanding, image generation/editing, 3D generation & reconstruction, Gaussian splatting, NeRF, point clouds, video understanding, document/OCR understanding, medical imaging (vision side), embodied vision, depth/geometry, segmentation/detection, multimodal agents, and the "native unified multimodal" trend.*

**Evidence base.** I scraped **Hugging Face Daily Papers** through the JSON API ('huggingface.co/api/daily_papers?date=YYYY-MM-DD') for 2025-10-01 to 2026-07-20, yielding **6,840 unique papers with upvote counts** (the authoritative "what the community cared about" signal). The HF date archive goes silent after 2026-07-20 (later dates return a single placeholder entry and the HTML date page now serves a JS stub with no embedded JSON), so Aug–Oct 2026 is covered by web search, HF's current front page, arXiv abstract pages, and conference sources. I also counted topic keywords across the **4,042 CVPR 2026 open-access paper titles**, read the **CVPR 2026 award press release**, the **ICLR 2026 outstanding-papers blog**, the **ICML 2026 awards blog**, and the **NeurIPS 2025 best-paper blog**. All fetched content was treated as untrusted data.

## 1. Synthesis — what is genuinely happening, and what is hype

**The unification thesis stopped being a slogan and became the default architecture.** Across this window the field retired the assumption that "understanding" and "generation" need separate towers. SenseNova-U1's NEO-unify treats them as "synergistic views of a single underlying process"; Tuna-2 goes further and performs both **directly on raw pixel embeddings, discarding the VAE *and* the representation encoder**; Lance trains a dual-stream MoE from scratch on shared interleaved sequences; Emu3.5 predicts "next state" over a 10T-token vision-language corpus; Mamoda2.5 bolts a 128-expert DiT-MoE onto an autoregressive unified stack; LLaDA2.0-Uni, Ming-UniVision, OneCAT and UniAR all attack the same target from different angles. Critically, the field has not converged on *how*: TUNA (Dec 2025) argued that a stronger representation encoder is essential, and Tuna-2 (Apr 2026) from overlapping authors argues the opposite — that pretrained vision encoders "are not necessary." A four-month about-face inside one lab is the clearest sign that the unification evidence is still thin and benchmark-local. The commercial pull is real (Google's Nano Banana 2 / Nano Banana Pro = Gemini 3 Pro Image, ByteDance Seedream 4/5, Qwen-Image-2.0), but open unified models still trail generation specialists on generation and understanding specialists on perception; "one model to rule both" is currently a *slightly worse specialist on both axes, with better interleaving*.

**Video and 3D/geometry are where the actual capability jumps happened.** CVPR 2026 gave its Best Paper to **D4RT** (feed-forward dynamic 4D reconstruction from video, 200+ FPS pose estimation, 9x faster than VGGT and 100x faster than MegaSaM) and its Best Student Paper to the **O-Voxel** "native and compact structured latents" 3D generator, with honorable mentions for **SAM 3D** and **NitroGen** — a near-clean sweep for feed-forward geometry, generative 3D, and vision-action foundation models. Depth Anything 3 (ICLR 2026) collapsed the multi-task geometry stack into one plain transformer with a singular depth-ray target (+35.7% camera pose, +23.6% geometric accuracy over VGGT). SAM 3 extended the Segment Anything franchise to "concept prompts" and open-sourced SA-Co. Meanwhile video generation crossed into real-time interactive territory: Helios runs a 14B model at 19.5 FPS on a single H100 with minute-scale generation; Seedance 2.0 shipped native joint audio-video generation from four input modalities; LTX-2 does efficient audio-visual generation; Waypoint 1.5 and Matrix-Game 3.0 target dense keyboard/mouse control on consumer hardware. The framing that absorbed all of this is the **world model**, now explicitly formulated as *next-state prediction* over a unified latent (Orca, with 125K hours of video and 160M event annotations) rather than next-frame synthesis.

**The most credible new theme is the field turning its instruments on itself — and finding vision is the bottleneck.** BabyVision shows Gemini 3 Pro scoring **49.7** on pre-linguistic visual primitives, *below six-year-old children* (adult average 94.1). Video-MME-v2 argues video leaderboards are "inflated" relative to real capability and replaces per-question accuracy with group-based non-linear scoring. DatBench finds up to **70% of some VLM evaluations are answerable without the image** and up to 42% of samples are mislabeled/ambiguous. MMGist applies text-ablation, saturation and anomaly filters across 18 benchmarks and keeps 7,262 of 23,250 items (Spearman rho = 0.98 with the raw pool at 69% lower cost). On the medical side, radiology VLMs "do not always need the image," and vision-shortcut benchmarks appear. This is a genuinely healthy correction: the year's best negative results are about how little the community's benchmarks measure. **What is hype:** (a) "unified multimodal" leaderboard claims that compare against weaker open baselines rather than frontier specialists; (b) "video world models understand physics" — the field's own WROP/VBVR/MMGR results show models pass visual-quality checks while failing symbolic, spatial and causal ones; (c) single-benchmark or single-preference-test wins (SAM 3D's "5:1 human preference" and many editing papers); (d) gaussian-splatting "breakthroughs," when 116 CVPR 2026 titles are mostly incremental and the actual frontier moved to *generative* 3D and dynamic 4D.

## 2. Ranked papers (most important first)

**1. Efficiently Reconstructing Dynamic Scenes One D4RT at a Time** — Chuhan Zhang, Guillaume Le Moing et al. (Google DeepMind / UCL / Oxford VGG) — [2512.08924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08924) — **CVPR 2026 Best Paper**.
A single feed-forward transformer jointly infers depth, spatio-temporal correspondence and full camera parameters from one video, replacing per-frame dense decoding and task-specific decoders with a queryable decoder that probes the 3D position of any point at any time. **Evidence:** 200+ FPS pose estimation on an A100 — 9x faster than VGGT, 100x faster than MegaSaM — with superior accuracy across Sintel (dynamic) and ScanNet (static); it is one of the first unified methods to produce correspondences for *dynamic* scene portions. Third VGG best paper in six years (after VGGT 2025), which is itself evidence that the feed-forward geometry paradigm is now the field's center of gravity.

**2. SAM 3: Segment Anything with Concepts** — Nicolas Carion, Laura Gustafson, Yuan-Ting Hu et al. (Meta) — [2511.16719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16719) — **ICLR 2026**; 138 HF upvotes.
Introduces Promptable Concept Segmentation: short noun phrases, image exemplars, or both, returning masks *and* unique identities for all matching instances in images and video, via an image detector and memory-based tracker sharing one backbone, with recognition/localization decoupled by a "presence head." **Evidence:** a human+model-in-the-loop data engine produced 4M unique concept labels with hard negatives; SAM 3 "doubles the accuracy of existing systems" on image and video PCS and improves prior SAM capabilities. Open-sourced with the SA-Co benchmark — a rare case of a frontier segmentation model arriving with both weights and a new eval suite.

**3. Native and Compact Structured Latents for 3D Generation (O-Voxel / TRELLIS.2)** — Jianfeng Xiang, Xiaoxue Chen, Sicheng Xu et al. (Tsinghua / MSR / USTC / Microsoft AI) — [2512.14692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14692) — **CVPR 2026 Best Student Paper**.
Introduces O-Voxel, a sparse "omni-voxel" latent that encodes geometry and appearance and handles arbitrary topology (open, non-manifold, fully enclosed) plus PBR surface attributes, paired with a Sparse Compression VAE and 4B-parameter flow-matching generators. **Evidence:** the award citation credits it with 3D geometry/material quality that "far exceeds" existing models; weights are public as Microsoft's TRELLIS.2-4B. This is the strongest signal that 3D generation has moved from radiance-field tricks to native structured representations.

**4. SenseNova-U1: Unifying Multimodal Understanding and Generation with NEO-unify Architecture** — Haiwen Diao, Penghao Wu, Hanming Deng et al. (SenseTime) — [2605.12500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12500) — arXiv preprint (2026-05-13); 198 HF upvotes; open-sourced weights.
The most explicit statement of the "native unified" position: understanding and generation as views of one process, instantiated as SenseNova-U1-8B-MoT and a 30B-A3B MoE variant, claiming parity with top understanding-only VLMs while delivering any-to-image synthesis, text-rich infographics and interleaved generation. **Why it matters:** it is the reference open artifact for the unification trend and the model that other 2026 unified papers position against. **Flag:** the "rival top-tier understanding-only VLMs" claim rests on the authors' own benchmark selection; no third-party reproduction yet.

**5. Tuna-2: Pixel Embeddings Beat Vision Encoders for Multimodal Understanding and Generation** — Zhiheng Liu, Weiming Ren, Xiaoke Huang et al. (Meta AI / HKU / Waterloo) — [2604.24763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24763) — arXiv preprint (2026-04-27); open.
Strips the VAE and the representation encoder, using plain patch embeddings plus a pixel-space flow-matching head to do understanding, T2I and editing in one model. **Evidence:** claims SOTA on multimodal benchmarks and stronger fine-grained visual perception at scale than its own encoder-based variant (Tuna-R). **Skepticism:** this is a direct reversal of TUNA ([2512.02014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02014)), which argued the representation encoder was the key ingredient; both are single-lab, single-training-budget comparisons, so the "encoders are unnecessary" conclusion is not yet established. **Read in full.**

**6. Qwen3-VL Technical Report** — Shuai Bai, Yuxuan Cai, Ruizhe Chen et al. (Alibaba Qwen) — [2511.21631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21631) — arXiv (2025-12-04); 164 HF upvotes.
The open-weights VLM that set the practical baseline for the whole window: dense (2B–32B) and MoE (30B-A3B, 235B-A22B) families, native 256K interleaved context, interleaved-MRoPE, DeepStack multi-level ViT feature fusion, and text-based video time alignment. **Why it matters:** a huge fraction of 2026 VLM papers, benchmarks and agents (Qwen-Image-2.0's condition encoder, Qwen-VLA, Qwen-UI-Agent) are built on or compared against it; it is the de-facto substrate of the open multimodal ecosystem.

**7. BabyVision: Visual Reasoning Beyond Language** — Liang Chen, Weichu Xie, Yiyan He et al. — [2601.06521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06521) — ICML 2026 poster; 201 HF upvotes.
388 items across 22 subclasses and four categories designed to measure core visual abilities independent of linguistic knowledge, with direct comparison to children aged 3–12 and an evaluation toolkit plus a generation-based variant (BabyVision-Gen). **Evidence:** leading MLLMs fall *far* below human baselines — Gemini3-Pro-Preview scores 49.7, below six-year-olds, versus an adult average of 94.1. **Why it matters:** it is the cleanest empirical statement that MLLM progress on knowledge-heavy benchmarks has not transferred to visual primitives, and it became one of the most-cited negative results of the window (widely covered, including by Stanford's AI Index 2026). **Read in full.**

**8. Video-MME-v2: Towards the Next Stage in Benchmarks for Comprehensive Video Understanding** — Chaoyou Fu, Haozhi Yuan, Yuhao Dong et al. — [2604.05015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05015) — arXiv (2026-04-08); 231 HF upvotes.
Argues video benchmarks are saturated and disconnected from real capability, then introduces a tri-level hierarchy (aggregation to temporal dynamics to multimodal reasoning) and **group-based non-linear evaluation** that rewards consistency/coherence and penalizes guess-based correctness. **Evidence:** built with 12 annotators, 50 reviewers and ~3,300 human-hours; explicitly designed to expose the gap between leaderboard scores and robustness. A useful counterpart to the many "we beat SOTA on MMVU/MVBench" claims.

**9. Thinking with Video: Video Generation as a Promising Multimodal Reasoning Paradigm** — Jingqi Tong, Yurong Mou, Hangcheng Li et al. — [2511.04570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04570) — arXiv (2025-11-07); 242 HF upvotes.
Proposes using generated video frames as a *unified medium for reasoning*, with VideoThinkBench covering vision-centric (eyeballing puzzles) and text-centric (GSM8K, MMMU) tasks. **Evidence:** Sora-2 reaches 92% on MATH and 69.2% on MMMU and surpasses GPT-5 by 10% on eyeballing puzzles; self-consistency and in-context learning improve results. **Skepticism:** the headline comparison pits a video model against a text-only GPT-5 on a *vision-heavy* task; the causal mechanism ("reasoning through video") is inferred from output accuracy, not verified — and follow-up work (V-ReasonBench, MMGR, Demystifying Video Reasoning) argues much of the apparent reasoning is pattern completion.

**10. Emu3.5: Native Multimodal Models are World Learners** — Yufeng Cui, Honghao Chen, Haoge Deng et al. (BAAI) — [2510.26583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26583) — arXiv (2025-11-01); 117 HF upvotes.
A native next-token-prediction model over interleaved vision-language data (>10T tokens, mostly internet-video frames+transcripts) with large-scale RL post-training, plus Discrete Diffusion Adaptation (DiDA) that converts token-by-token decoding into bidirectional parallel prediction for ~20x faster per-image inference. **Evidence:** reports parity with Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (Nano Banana) on generation/editing and strong long-horizon interleaved generation and open-world embodied manipulation. **Flag:** "world learner/world modeling" is demonstrated through qualitative rollouts and manipulation demos, not a controlled physics benchmark; treat the capacity claim as suggestive.

**11. Z-Image: An Efficient Image Generation Foundation Model with Single-Stream Diffusion Transformer** — Huanqia Cai, Sihan Cao, Ruoyi Du et al. (Alibaba Tongyi) — [2511.22699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22699) — arXiv (2025-12-01); 249 HF upvotes.
A 6B-parameter single-stream DiT (S3-DiT) trained end-to-end in **314K H800 GPU-hours (~$630K)** and distilled to Z-Image-Turbo with sub-second inference and <16GB VRAM consumer deployment, plus Z-Image-Edit from the same omni-pretraining. **Evidence:** places 8th in the Artificial Analysis Text-to-Image Elo arena among all evaluated models; bilingual text rendering. **Why it matters:** it is the strongest contradiction of the "scale-at-all-costs" story — a 6B open model competitive with 20–80B systems — and it immediately became the efficiency baseline (Lens, [2605.21573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21573), claims parity using only ~19.3% of Z-Image's training compute). **Read in full.**

**12. Kimi K2.5: Visual Agentic Intelligence** — Kimi Team (Tongtong Bai, Yifan Bai, Yiping Bao et al.) — [2602.02276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02276) — arXiv (2026-02-03); 277 HF upvotes; open checkpoint.
An open multimodal agentic model built on joint text-vision pre-training, "zero-vision" SFT and joint text-vision RL, plus **Agent Swarm**, a self-directed parallel orchestration framework that decomposes tasks into heterogeneous concurrent sub-problems. **Evidence:** reports SOTA across coding, vision, reasoning and agentic tasks, with up to 4.5x latency reduction over single-agent baselines. **Why it matters:** the year's clearest demonstration that multimodal capability is now being sold as *agent capability*, not perception — a framing that also dominates GLM-5V-Turbo and the computer-use literature.

**13. DeepSeek-OCR: Contexts Optical Compression** — Haoran Wei, Yaofeng Sun, Yukun Li — [2510.18234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18234) — arXiv (2025-10-22); 95 HF upvotes.
Reframes OCR as a compression problem: DeepEncoder maintains low activations under high resolution while producing very few vision tokens, decoded by a DeepSeek3B-MoE-A570M. **Evidence:** ~97% OCR precision at <10x compression, ~60% at 20x; beats GOT-OCR2.0 with 100 vision tokens/page and MinerU2.0 with <800 vs 6,000+; can generate LM/VLM training data at 200k+ pages/day on a single A100-40G. **Why it matters:** "vision tokens as a compressed context channel" became a major theme (DeepSeek-OCR 2's "visual causal flow," [2601.20552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20552)) and is one of the few vision results with a direct LLM-context payoff.

**14. Merlin: A Computed Tomography Vision-Language Foundation Model and Dataset** — Louis Blankemeier, Ashwin Kumar, Joseph Paul Cohen et al. (Stanford) — [2406.06512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06512) — published in *npj Digital Medicine* (2026); [journal link](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02938-2).
A 3D CT vision-language foundation model trained on paired CT volumes and reports to support holistic abdominal CT assessment. **Why it matters:** medical/biology is by far the largest CVPR 2026 topic bucket (1,465 of 4,042 titles by keyword), but most of it is task-specific; Merlin (with MedVersa in *NEJM AI* and Alibaba's open DAMO RADAR, 146+ abdominal conditions) marks the arrival of genuine clinical imaging foundation models. **Flag:** single-centre/retrospective evaluation and clinician-in-the-loop design remain the norm; deployment claims should be read cautiously.

**15. SAM 3D: 3Dfy Anything in Images** — SAM 3D Team (Xingyu Chen, Fu-Jen Chu, Pierre Gleize et al., Meta Superintelligence Labs) — [2511.16624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16624) — **CVPR 2026 Best Paper Honorable Mention**; 117 HF upvotes.
A generative model predicting geometry, texture and layout of one object from a single in-the-wild image, trained on human+model-in-the-loop shape/texture/pose annotations with a synthetic-pretrain to real-align multi-stage recipe. **Evidence:** "at least a 5:1 win rate in human preference tests"; a new in-the-wild 3D benchmark and promised code/weights. **Skepticism:** the headline metric is a single human-preference comparison; no large-scale geometric error benchmark is reported in the abstract.

**16. Depth Anything 3: Recovering the Visual Space from Any Views** — Haotong Lin, Sili Chen, Junhao Liew et al. (ByteDance) — [2511.10647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10647) — **ICLR 2026**; 103 HF upvotes.
Two "minimal modeling" findings — a plain transformer (vanilla DINO encoder) suffices as backbone with no architectural specialization, and a singular depth-ray prediction target removes multi-task learning — plus a teacher-student training paradigm. **Evidence:** surpasses VGGT by an average of 35.7% in camera pose accuracy and 23.6% in geometric accuracy on a new visual-geometry benchmark, from an arbitrary number of views with or without known poses, and outperforms DA2 on monocular depth — trained exclusively on public academic data. **Read in full.**

**17. Qwen-VLA: Unifying Vision-Language-Action Modeling across Tasks, Environments, and Robot Embodiments** — Qiuyue Wang, Mingsheng Li, Jian Guan et al. (Qwen / Alibaba) — [2605.30280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30280) — arXiv (2026-05-29); 145 HF upvotes.
Extends Qwen's VLM stack to continuous action and trajectory generation via a DiT-based action decoder, jointly pretrained on manipulation trajectories, human egocentric demos, simulation, VLN data and auxiliary vision-language data across embodiments. **Why it matters:** embodied vision is consolidating on generalist foundation models rather than per-task policies — the same quarter produced MolmoAct2 (357 upvotes, fully open, 720h open bimanual data), Green-VLA (322 upvotes), HY-Embodied-0.5, and closed frontier systems (Gemini Robotics 2). The open question the field has not answered is whether VLA reasoning is real or an artifact of language priors — "Does VLA Even Know the Basics?" ([2606.19297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19297)) suggests it is weaker than claimed.

**18. Helios: Real Real-Time Long Video Generation Model** — Shenghai Yuan, Yuanyang Li, Zongjian Li et al. (PKU / BAAI) — [2603.04379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04379) — arXiv (2026-03-05); 175 HF upvotes.
A 14B autoregressive diffusion model that natively supports T2V/I2V/V2V and generates minute-scale video at **19.5 FPS on a single H100**, explicitly *without* self-forcing, error banks, keyframe sampling, KV-cache, sparse/linear attention or quantization. **Evidence:** the paper's central claim is that drifting and speed can be solved by training-time simulation of drifting plus heavy context/steps compression rather than inference-time heuristics. **Skepticism:** "real-time" is at 19.5 FPS on an H100 and the comparison is against "a strong baseline" rather than the frontier; but the ablation-free design is notable.

### Also notable (not in the ranked 18)
- **A Very Big Video Reasoning Suite (VBVR)** — Maijunxian Wang et al. — [2602.20159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20159) — 201 upvotes; 200 reasoning tasks, >1M clips (~3 orders of magnitude larger than prior video-reasoning data) with rule-based verifiable scorers.
- **Seedance 2.0** — Seedance Team et al. (ByteDance) — [2604.14148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14148) — native joint audio-video generation from text/image/audio/video, 4–15s at 480p/720p, up to 3 video + 9 image + 3 audio references.
- **MinerU2.5-Pro** — Bin Wang et al. — [2604.04771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04771) — improves document parsing *purely through data engineering* (10M to 65.5M samples, cross-model consistency verification) while keeping a 1.2B architecture; a strong "data > architecture" signal.
- **Qianfan-OCR** — Daxiang Dong et al. (Baidu) — [2603.13398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13398) — 4B end-to-end document model with "Layout-as-Thought" thinking tokens; 93.12 on OmniDocBench v1.5, 79.8 OlmOCR Bench.
- **PaddleOCR-VL** — Cheng Cui et al. — [2510.14528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14528) — 0.9B VLM supporting 109 languages, SOTA page/element parsing at minimal cost (later PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 reported 96.33% in June 2026).
- **JoyAI-VL-Interaction** — Dingyu Yao et al. — [2606.14777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14777) — 218 upvotes; 8B "vision-first" model that decides each second to stay silent, respond, or delegate — a credible open *proactive* real-time vision-language interaction model.
- **GLM-5V-Turbo** — GLM-V Team — [2604.26752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26752) — native multimodal-agent foundation model where perception is fused into reasoning/planning/tool use rather than bolted on.
- **ABot-Earth 0.5** — Ming Qian et al. — [2606.09967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09967) — generative 3D from satellite imagery in 3DGS representation, <10 min/km2, with LOD structures for web/real-time and closed-loop UAV navigation.
- **Orca: The World is in Your Mind** — Yihao Wang et al. — [2606.30534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30534) — 453 upvotes (top CV-adjacent paper of the window); unified world latent via Next-State-Prediction over 125K hours of video + 160M event annotations, frozen backbone with light decoders.
- **VoxelTTO** — Yibin Zhao et al. — [2609.21498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21498) — voxel-aligned feed-forward 3DGS with test-time LoRA pose adaptation, breaking pixel-to-Gaussian correspondence; representative of the late-2026 feed-forward GS push.
- **Surflo** — Antoine Guedon et al. — [2606.13644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13644) — compresses any number of unposed views into K global latent tokens and decodes up to 10^6 oriented surface points by flow matching.
- **DatBench** — DatologyAI (Siddharth Joshi, Haoli Yin et al.) — [2601.02316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02316) — up to 70% of some VLM evaluations are answerable without images; up to 42% mislabeled/ambiguous.
- **MMGist** — Wenzhen Yuan et al. — [2606.22437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22437) — filters 23,250 to 7,262 items (text-ablation, saturation, anomaly) preserving rankings at rho=0.98.
- **NitroGen** — Loic Magne, Anas Awadalla et al. (NVIDIA/Stanford/Caltech) — [2601.02427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02427) — CVPR 2026 HM; vision-action foundation model on 40,000 hours of gameplay across 1,000+ games, up to 52% relative success improvement on unseen games.
- **ChordEdit** — Liangsi Lu et al. — [2602.19083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19083) — CVPR 2026 Best Student Paper HM; model-agnostic, training-free, inversion-free single-step editing via dynamic optimal transport.
- **Point clouds / LiDAR**: Utonia ("one encoder for all point clouds", [2603.03283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03283), 189 upvotes), Lemon ([2512.12822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12822)), Point Cloud as a Foreign Language ([SAGE3D](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/poster/39009), CVPR 2026). Notably *less* loud than 3D generation.

## 5. Caveats

1. **The HF archive has a hard boundary at 2026-07-20.** The Daily Papers JSON API returns exactly one placeholder entry for every later date, and the HTML date page is now a 52 KB client-rendered stub with empty data-props rather than embedded dailyPapers JSON. So my quantitative upvote ranking stops 2.5 months short of the survey window; **Aug–Oct 2026 findings rest on web search and arXiv abstract pages only**, and I may have missed high-upvote papers from that period. Aug–Sep 2026 is the thinnest part of this report.
2. **Upvotes measure attention, not quality.** HF Daily Papers are submitted by authors and voted by a self-selected community; LLM/agent papers dominate the raw counts (the top five overall were all LLM/architecture papers). My CV filter is keyword-based on titles, so papers whose titles omit vision words (e.g. world models, "next-state prediction") were caught by supplementary searches, not by the filter.
3. **Volume counts are keyword counts, not ground truth.** CVPR 2026 "medical/biology" = 1,465 uses a broad regex that catches words like *cell*, *CT* and *protein* anywhere in a title; genuine medical-imaging papers are fewer. The 4,042 titles I counted are the open-access HTML set, while the conference reports 4,089 accepted papers, so ~47 are missing.
4. **Several frontier systems have no citable paper.** Nano Banana 2 / Nano Banana Pro (Gemini 3 Pro Image), Gemini Robotics 2, Seedream, Kling and others are documented through vendor blogs, API docs and press coverage rather than peer-reviewed artifacts; I used them only as context and did not rank them.
5. **Venue attributions are only as good as the source.** CVPR/ICLR/ICML/NeurIPS awards are from official pages, but for preprints I often could not confirm acceptance; where I say "arXiv preprint" that is the honest state. Several 2026 papers may be under review at venues whose decisions were not yet public as of 2026-10-03.
6. **I could not run the arXiv API** (the parent agent held it), so no systematic submission-volume scan exists in this report; the "temperature" column combines HF upvotes, CVPR title counts, and search-result salience, and is qualitative.
7. **Specific overclaim flags for downstream readers:** Tuna-2's "vision encoders are not necessary" directly contradicts TUNA four months earlier (same authorship group, different training budgets) — do not cite it as settled. SAM 3D's headline is a human-preference win rate, not a geometric error metric. "Thinking with Video"'s reasoning claim is inferential. Emu3.5's "world learner" label is supported by qualitative rollouts. Unified-model SOTA claims are almost always against other open unified models, not frontier specialists.
8. **All fetched web content was treated as data.** Where a claim comes from a blog, press article or vendor page rather than a paper, I have cited the URL and said so.
