# Generative Media & Audio — Video Generation, Speech/Music, Image & Video Editing, World Models (Oct 2025 – Oct 2026)

*Assigned area: generative media & audio. Coverage window Oct 2025 → early Oct 2026, weighted toward mid-2026. This is a depth/breadth pass intended to complement the earlier RSI / long-context / agentic-RL / conference passes.*

## Method note & caveats (read this first)

- **Discovery.** `web_search` for leads; then direct `curl` of `arxiv.org/abs/<id>` for metadata/abstracts and `arxiv.org/html/<id>` for full text. I did **not** use export.arxiv.org (parent had an API job running there).
- **Quantitative attention signal.** I downloaded the **Hugging Face Daily Papers JSON API for every day from 2025-10-01 to 2026-10-03** (`huggingface.co/api/daily_papers?date=…`, 368 day-files, 8,014 unique papers). This gives an independent, community-level popularity signal (upvotes) that is *not* arXiv volume. All upvote counts cited below are from that scrape unless noted. It is a biased sample: HF Daily Papers over-represents model releases, demos, and English-language lab papers, and under-represents theory. Treat it as "what practitioners upvoted," not "what is most rigorous."
- **Full-text reading.** I read the main body of 12 papers from the HTML (not just abstracts): the two surveys (2604.15911, 2607.25642), four evaluation papers (2603.19607, 2608.19583, 2604.21686, 2605.26244), three systems (2609.11638, 2601.03233, 2609.08936), music (2605.01790), duplex speech (2605.20755). Section (4) lists exactly what was read in full.
- **Untrusted data.** All fetched pages are treated as data. Every claim is attributed to a URL. Model-card / technical-report numbers are **self-reported** unless labelled otherwise — I flag this repeatedly because it is the single biggest reliability problem in this area.
- **This is the flashiest area in AI.** The gap between leaderboard/demo claims and audited capability is wider here than anywhere else in the survey. Section (5) is load-bearing, not decorative.

## (1) Synthesis — what is genuinely happening vs. what is hype

**The real story is that generative video crossed from "clip generator" to "streaming, controllable, interactive system," and the field's own benchmarks say the resulting models are far weaker than the demos suggest.** Three years of diffusion-transformer scaling produced a genuine architectural convergence — DiT + latent video VAE + flow matching is now the default, and the interesting work moved to the *systems* around it: causal/autoregressive rollout, few-step distillation, KV/context compression, and quantization. The mid-2026 frontier is real-time, minute-scale, action- or voice-controllable streaming: Vidu S2 (Tsinghua + Shengshu) ships 720p interactive digital characters with mid-stream reference swapping and a real-time streaming video **editor**, and it was by a wide margin the most-upvoted media paper on HF Daily Papers for the whole year (**706 upvotes**, ~5x the next media paper) — [2609.11638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11638). Behind it sits a dense, genuinely cumulative methods line: Self-Forcing++ (Oct 2025) → Causal Forcing++ and minWM (May 2026) → Vidu S1/S2 (Jul/Sep 2026), with Helios showing a 14B AR model at 19.5 FPS on one H100 ([2603.04379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04379)) and LongLive-2.0/SANA-WM pushing minute-scale rollout on modest hardware. The efficiency survey I anchored on documents the volume — in 722 acceleration papers 2022–2026, image-only acceleration is 64.1%, video-only 20.6%, cross-domain 15.2%, and sparse attention has effectively beaten claimed-linear attention in practice ([2604.15911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15911)). The second real convergence is the **collapse of task pipelines into unified multimodal generators**: Kling-Omni folds generation + editing + reasoning over text/image/video context into one system ([2512.16776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16776)); Seedance 2.0 is a native audio-video model accepting text, image, audio and video references ([2604.14148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14148)); Cosmos 3 unifies language/image/video/audio/action in one mixture-of-transformers backbone "for Physical AI" ([2606.02800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02800)). Audio followed the same arc: from turn-based TTS/ASR to unified speech generation+editing (AuK, [2609.08936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08936)) and native full-duplex speech-language-action models (DuplexSLA, [2605.20755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20755); Lychee-FD won an ACL 2026 Outstanding Paper for diagnosing the acoustic/semantic gradient conflict that makes duplex SLMs *dumb*, [2026.acl-long.419](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.419/)).

**The counter-story — and the most important finding of this pass — is that the field's own evaluation papers repeatedly show visual quality is not a proxy for the capability being claimed.** WorldMark benchmarked ten interactive world models on a unified WASD-style action interface and found that translational direction accuracy clusters above 88 for eight of ten models ("a solved problem"), while response latency ranges 40.7–98.2 and rotational stability 13.7–98.1 — i.e. models indistinguishable by the headline metric differ several-fold in how they actually behave; worse, **perceptual quality correlates *negatively* with response latency** (the best-looking model, Yume 1.5, was last on translational direction accuracy), and third-person viewpoints disproportionately break camera rotation ([2604.21686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21686)). Physion-Eval had 90 trained experts annotate generated videos from five SOTA models and found **83.3% of exocentric and 93.5% of egocentric videos contain at least one human-identifiable physical glitch**, while the best MLLM critic (Gemini 3.0 Pro) misses 74.4%/90.1% of them and hallucinates timestamped causal explanations ([2603.19607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19607)). VGI-Bench put 27 process-sensitive visual-reasoning tasks to the strongest video generators and found **the best model, Seedance 2.0, scores only 51.0**, with topology and temporal state-tracking the weakest skills ([2608.19583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19583)). LongAV-Compass showed proprietary models plateau (~70–75) while open-source models *degrade* with difficulty and event-chain length (57.9→51.4), with product-demonstration scenarios exposing a shared failure in event fulfillment and narrative continuity ([2605.26244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26244)). And Physion-Eval/WorldMark are not alone: for physics, the earlier NewtonRewards line ([2512.00425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00425)) and causality probes like YoCausal ([2605.30346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30346)) reach the same verdict. In audio the equivalent number is brutal: the best unified speech-editing model, AuK, reaches only **13.85% exact-match success** across all editing rubrics on MMAE-Speech, and still needs a prompt-rewriting "enhancer" to invoke capabilities it nominally has ([2609.08936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08936)).

**What is hype, explicitly.** (a) **"World model" is used for what is often an action-conditioned video generator.** The benchmarks above show short-horizon smoothness is nearly solved and long-range global geometry is not; OpenWorldLib's authors themselves argue the field lacks a unified definition ([2604.04707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04707)), and WorldMark deliberately excludes object dynamics because camera-induced motion entangles them. (b) **Self-reported SOTA from model cards and 100+ author technical reports.** Seedance 2.0, Kling-Omni, Cosmos 3 and Vidu S2 are all evaluated on internal benchmarks or expert panels; Cosmos 3's "state of the art" is explicitly "ranked best by Artificial Analysis / RoboArena at the time of writing" ([2606.02800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02800)). (c) **Single-benchmark claims.** Alice v1 claims to *surpass closed-source* models (Veo3 ~90, Sora2 ~88) on the strength of one automated metric — VBench 84.0→91.2 — from a 3-author, unrefereed preprint ([2605.08115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08115)); VBench is a perceptual-quality aggregate, not a physics or control benchmark, and Physion-Eval/VGI-Bench show those two things come apart. (d) **"Real-time" with tiny samples.** Vidu S2's human-preference win over Runway/HeyGen/PixVerse is reported as 85.7% and 100% preference shares, which arithmetically implies roughly **7 paired judgments per system** ([2609.11638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11638)); Shao's music arena that ranks it #1 open-source rests on **10 prompts and 766 votes** ([2605.01790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01790)). (e) **"Physics understanding emerges from scale."** Every controlled evaluation in this window contradicts it; the honest framing is that video models memorize appearance-consistent motion statistics.

**Geopolitics/economics, because it shapes what gets released.** By August 2026 the Artificial Analysis blind-preference arena reportedly had **9 of the top 10 text-to-video models Chinese** (Gemini Omni Flash first; then MiniMax H3, Seedance 2.0, two Wan builds, two HappyHorse, two Kling, SkyReels), with the article also reporting that OpenAI shut down Sora in March 2026 ([thenextweb](https://thenextweb.com/news/china-ai-video-dominance-world-models)). The strategic reading — video as the training ground for world models/robotics — is plausible but unproven; the same article correctly notes that "a convincing clip is not the same as understanding the world."

## (2) Ranked papers (18)

Ranked by a blend of demonstrated importance, methodological influence, community attention (HF upvotes), and evaluation value — not by citation count, which does not exist yet for mid-2026 work.

1. **Vidu S2: Real-Time Interactive, Editable, and Spatial Video Generation** — Jintao Zhang, Kai Jiang, Jintao Chen et al. (co-first; Tsinghua University + Shengshu Technology) — [2609.11638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11638) — arXiv preprint, 2026-09-10. Two coupled real-time models: **S2-Avatar** (720p interactive digital characters, dynamic references swappable mid-stream, large body motion such as dancing) and **S2-Editing** (real-time streaming video editing: style transfer, virtual try-on, character/background replacement), plus an exploration of stereoscopic/VR output. The method contribution is **Self-Replay Forcing (SRF)**, an on-policy DMD variant that re-noises a *detached* student rollout and replays it in a gradient-enabled causal pass, so credit propagates across blocks without storing the full rollout graph; frame-aligned attention lets editing preserve source motion exactly. **Evidence / why it matters:** with **706 upvotes it is the top media paper of the entire HF year** (next-highest media paper ~249), and it is the clearest statement that streaming *editing* is now a first-class goal alongside streaming generation. **Skeptical:** no venue; "outperforms all baselines" on internal GSB benchmarks with ~7 judgments/system; the "real-time 720p" claim is not independently reproduced.

2. **Seedance 2.0: Advancing Video Generation for World Complexity** — Seedance Team (ByteDance), 171 authors — [2604.14148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14148) — model card, 2026-04-15 (released in China early Feb 2026). Unified native multi-modal audio-video generation supporting four input modalities (text, image, audio, video) with reference/editing capability (up to 3 video clips, 9 images, 3 audio inputs), 4–15 s output at 480p/720p. **Evidence / why it matters:** it is the reference production system for "native AV generation" and, per the Artificial Analysis arena, sits among the top Chinese video models; VGI-Bench uses it as its strongest generator and still scores it at only 51.0 ([2608.19583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19583)). **Skeptical:** a model card — "on par with the leading levels" rests on expert evaluation and public user tests, with no reproducible protocol; no latency/cost numbers.

3. **Cosmos 3: Omnimodal World Models for Physical AI** — NVIDIA, 296 authors — [2606.02800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02800) — technical report, 2026-06-01. A family of world models that jointly process and generate language, image, video, audio and action sequences in a unified mixture-of-transformers architecture, explicitly subsuming VLMs, video generators, world simulators and world-action models. **Evidence / why it matters:** the boldest "one backbone for physical AI" bet from an industrial lab, and it ships code/checkpoints/datasets; NVIDIA reports it was ranked best open-source T2I and I2V by Artificial Analysis and best policy by RoboArena at report time. **Skeptical:** 296 authors, self-reported external rankings, no peer review; "omnimodal" breadth invites shallow evaluation per modality.

4. **Kling-Omni Technical Report** — Kling Team (Kuaishou), 68 authors — [2512.16776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16776) — technical report, 2025-12-18. A generalist generative framework that takes text instructions, reference images and video context into one multimodal representation and does in-context generation, reasoning-based editing and cinematic video creation end-to-end rather than as disjoint pipelines. **Evidence / why it matters:** an early and influential statement (Dec 2025) that the generation/editing/reasoning split should collapse, predating the 2026 unified-model wave; 173 HF upvotes. **Skeptical:** commercial technical report, internal evaluations only.

5. **LTX-2: Efficient Joint Audio-Visual Foundation Model** — Yoav HaCohen et al. (Lightricks), 29 authors — [2601.03233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03233) — arXiv preprint, 2026-01-06. Open-source joint AV model: an asymmetric dual-stream transformer (14B video + 5B audio) coupled by bidirectional audio-video cross-attention with cross-modality AdaLN; modality-aware CFG; ~18x lower per-step cost than Wan 2.2-14B (1.22 s vs 22.30 s/step on H100) and up to 20 s of synchronized stereo audio. **Evidence / why it matters:** the most credible open answer to Veo/Sora-native-AV at a fraction of compute; 197 HF upvotes; its efficiency comes from latent-space design rather than step distillation, a distinct bet from the Helios/Causal-Forcing line. **Skeptical:** "human preference comparable to Veo 3 and Sora 2" is an internal study; the Artificial Analysis ranking cited is dated Nov 6 2025, before the model report; the authors themselves flag multi-speaker speaker-assignment errors and drift past ~20 s.

6. **Helios: Real Real-Time Long Video Generation Model** — Shenghai Yuan, Yuanyang Yin, Zongjian Li et al. — [2603.04379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04379) — arXiv preprint, 2026-03-04. A 14B autoregressive diffusion model running at **19.5 FPS on a single H100** over minute-scale generation, claiming robustness to long-video drift *without* self-forcing/error-banks/keyframe sampling, real-time generation *without* KV-cache/sparse-linear attention/quantization, and training without parallel/sharding frameworks (up to four 14B models in 80 GB). **Evidence / why it matters:** the clearest "systems minimalism" data point — it argues the real-time stack others build from distillation+caching is not strictly necessary; 175 HF upvotes. **Skeptical:** "matching the quality of a strong baseline" is unquantified in the abstract; the no-parallelism training claim is unusual and unreplicated; 6 authors, no venue.

7. **Efficient Video Diffusion Models: Advancements and Challenges** — Shitong Shao, Lichen Bai, Pengfei Wan, James Kwok et al. — [2604.15911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15911) — survey, 2026-04-17. Proposes a four-paradigm taxonomy — **step distillation, efficient attention, model compression, cache/trajectory optimization** — and maps the literature onto two objectives (fewer NFEs vs lower per-step overhead). **Evidence / why it matters:** the single best structural entry point for this window's largest media sub-literature; reports that sparse attention dominates over claimed-linear attention (linear attention usually acts as *error compensation* for dropped tokens), that most token/model pruning is impractical (post-training pruning is prohibitively expensive; training-free pruning degrades quality), that quantization's gains are concentrated in attention rather than the QKV/MLP linears, and that feature caching conflicts with step distillation. **Skeptical:** a survey's framing is an argument, not a measurement; its own "open challenges" note the field lacks a regime-aware Pareto understanding.

8. **Instruction-based Image Editing: A Survey on Data, Models, Evaluation, and Applications** — Xianghao Zang, Zijian Jiang, Jiarong Cheng et al. — [2607.25642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25642) — survey (Vicinagearth), 33 pages, 2026-07-28. Five-axis taxonomy (task hierarchy → data construction → architecture evolution GAN→diffusion→autoregressive → metrics/benchmarks → commercial products) plus the new **CDD-IIE Bench**: 1,353 image-instruction pairs, 5 dimensions, 21 tasks, scored by 12 expert annotators per pair (162,360 expert ratings total). **Evidence / why it matters:** the empirical findings are the value — **all models are strong at atomic edits and weak at compositional ones**, spatial understanding/reasoning is the lowest-scoring dimension (avg 2.92; best model Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 only 3.45), and high semantic competence comes at the cost of low-level "utility tasks." **Skeptical:** the benchmark is the authors' own; model comparisons are snapshot-bound and quickly dated.

9. **Physion-Eval: Evaluating Physical Realism in Generated Video via Human Reasoning** — Qin Zhang, Peiyu Jing, Hong-Xing Yu et al. — [2603.19607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19607) — arXiv preprint, 2026-03-20. 10,990 expert-annotated reasoning traces over videos from five SOTA generators, spanning 22 physical categories and exocentric + egocentric views, each with timestamped glitch localization, structured failure taxonomy and natural-language explanation; built from WISA-80K/EPIC-KITCHENS with 90 STEM-trained annotators and 3-way adjudication. **Evidence / why it matters:** it converts "video models don't understand physics" from an intuition into a quantified, human-grounded benchmark (**83.3% exo / 93.5% ego videos contain ≥1 glitch; Gemini 3.0 Pro misses 74.4%/90.1% of them**), and it is the strongest argument in this window that MLLM-as-judge evaluation of physical realism is not yet trustworthy. **Skeptical:** only five generators; the taxonomy is the authors'; "glitch" judgements involve expert judgement calls on 0.1 s localization.

10. **WorldMark: A Unified Benchmark Suite for Interactive Video World Models** — Xiaojie Xu, Zhengyuan Lin, Kang He et al. — [2604.21686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21686) — benchmark, 2026-04-23. Per-model adapters translate a shared WASD-style action vocabulary into each of ten heterogeneous models' native control formats over 500 standardized cases; action-following is treated as a control-systems step response, measuring **direction, purity, latency and stability per axis**, plus a three-timescale memory suite and visual quality. **Evidence / why it matters:** the best existing demonstration of why single-score world-model leaderboards mislead — direction accuracy saturates (>88 for 8/10) while latency spans 40.7–98.2 and stability 13.7–98.1; the fastest responders are often least stable; stylization costs memory but not action; third-person viewpoints disproportionately break rotation; **visual quality correlates negatively with response latency**. Metric–human agreement is 85.1% on average over 40 annotators. **Skeptical:** the authors exclude object dynamics and event interaction by design; ten models is already a large but still partial sample.

11. **VGI-Bench: Probing Visual Intelligence in Video Generation Models** — Xuan He, Cong Wei, Yuhao Cheng et al. — [2608.19583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19583) — benchmark, 2026-08-20. 27 process-sensitive tasks / 810 instances across four domains (Visual Organization, Physical Manipulation, Structured Puzzles, Spatiotemporal Dynamics) with seven skill tags, scored by a multiplicative Completeness × Rubric metric so that a plausible final frame with an invalid trajectory fails. **Evidence / why it matters:** provides the cleanest current ceiling estimate for "video generation as visual reasoning" — **Seedance 2.0 leads at 51.0; topology and temporal tracking are weakest** — and shows commercial > open, with static-image variants also led by Nano-Banana-Pro (55.0). **Skeptical:** judged by Gemini-3-Flash (AUC 0.803, 73.2% pairwise agreement with humans) and only half the instances are evaluated per task.

12. **LongAV-Compass: Towards Unified Evaluation of Minute-Scale Audio-Visual Generation** — Tengfei Liu, Yang Shi, Xuanyu Zhu et al. — [2605.26244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26244) — benchmark, 2026-05-25. 284 curated minute-scale cases across T2AV/I2AV/V2AV with event-aligned segmentation and a unified MLLM + perceptual metric framework, targeting identity consistency, narrative coherence and AV alignment over long horizons. **Evidence / why it matters:** it quantifies the long-form controllability gap — proprietary models stay ~70–75 across difficulty while open-source fall 57.9→51.4 and agent-based methods fall 47.3→41.2; **Performance Ads is the hardest scenario for nearly every system**, with failures concentrated in event fulfillment and long-form continuity rather than text-video semantics; LTX 2.3 is the strongest open model but still trails proprietary systems. **Skeptical:** metric choices are authors'; results are a moving snapshot.

13. **AuK Technical Report: An Open-Source Foundational Model for Speech Generation and Editing** — Ziyang Ma, Zhikang Niu, Wenming Tu et al., 33 authors — [2609.08936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08936) — technical report + open weights, 2026-09-08. One model for five task families (speech generation; acoustic editing; paralinguistic editing; content editing; enhancement/separation) under a single natural-language-instruction + optional-audio-context interface, combining an MLLM semantic encoder, a VAE jointly trained on speech/general-audio/music, and a hybrid rectified-flow transformer (dual-stream MMDiT → single-stream DiT); ~3.03B instruction–audio instances and 1.95M hours of supervision; flow-based DPO for editing and Flow-GRPO for generation; distilled to a 4-step CFG-free AuK-Flash. **Evidence / why it matters:** the leading open unified speech system — zero-shot TTS average error 2.65% with SIM 0.795 (vs 3.07% for Qwen3-TTS and 0.778 for Seed-TTS), and best-in-class general speech editing (MMAE-Speech IFR 48.23%, CR 88.11%). **Skeptical:** the same table shows **EMR of only 13.85%** — models satisfy *all* editing rubrics on under a seventh of cases; the authors concede that robust native understanding of free-form requests is incomplete and that a Prompt Enhancer is still needed to hit the capability ceiling.

14. **Qwen3-TTS Technical Report** — Hangrui Hu, Xinfa Zhu, Ting He et al. — [2601.15621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15621) — technical report, 2026-01-22. Multilingual, controllable, streaming TTS trained on **5M+ hours across 10 languages**, supporting 3-second voice cloning and description-based control (novel voices as well as fine-grained manipulation); dual-track LM for real-time synthesis with two tokenizers: a 25 Hz single-codebook semantic tokenizer with block-wise DiT streaming reconstruction, and a 12.5 Hz 16-layer multi-codebook tokenizer for ultra-low latency (**97 ms** first-packet emission). **Evidence / why it matters:** with Qwen3-ASR ([2601.21337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21337), 52 languages, 1.7B/0.6B, 92 ms TTFT, 2000 s transcribed in 1 s at concurrency 128), it defines the open frontier of the speech stack and the production baseline everyone else compares to. **Skeptical:** vendor technical reports with heavy internal evaluation; no independent replication in this pass; the ASR report itself notes open benchmarks are weakly discriminative and relies on internal suites.

15. **Shao: Scaling Acoustic Token Language Models Toward High-Fidelity Music Generation** — Jiafeng Liu, Yuanliang Dong, Hongjia Liu et al. — [2605.01790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01790) — arXiv preprint, 2026-05-03. A **64-layer residual-vector-quantization acoustic token hierarchy** with two-stage coarse-to-fine generation: a backbone emits full-length coarse acoustic tokens, then a super-resolution model refines higher layers at full-track scale with time-parallel prediction (fixed 62-step inference); hybrid-attention training (causal for lyric alignment, full attention for layer refinement) makes text–vocal alignment emerge *without* a separate semantic-token stage. **Evidence / why it matters:** the cleanest 2026 evidence that the dominant "semantic tokenizer + diffusion decoder" recipe is not required — pure acoustic-token LM ranks 4th overall and **1st among open-source** in a 766-vote blind listening arena (BT-Elo 1510.9), ahead of MiniMax 2.5 Plus. **Skeptical:** the arena is only 10 prompts; Shao still trails Mureka v8 (1689.3) and Suno v5/v4.5; the system is two models, not one, which the authors frame as a compute-budget compromise.

16. **DuplexSLA: A Full-Duplex Spoken Language Model with Synchronized Speech, Language, and Action** — Haoyang Zhang, Jun Chen, Donghang Wu et al. — [2605.20755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20755) — arXiv preprint, 2026-05-20. A dual-stream **three-channel** formulation (continuous user audio, discrete assistant audio, and a rate-limited textual action channel) over a shared **160 ms** chunk timeline, so listening, speaking, planning and tool calling run on one clock; interruption/pause/backchannel are handled by the backbone rather than an external semantic VAD, and tool calls can be emitted without waiting for turn boundaries. **Evidence / why it matters:** the strongest 2026 statement that "duplex" should include *acting*, not just listening-while-speaking, on a single shared timeline; **sub-second responses in all four turn-taking scenarios and the only system handling backchannel detection cleanly, with tool-call accuracy matching the ASR+LLM cascade at lower delay**. **Skeptical:** evaluated on the authors' DuplexSLA-Bench; data recipe (dual-side ASR on a 160 ms grid) is expensive and hard to reproduce.

17. **Hierarchical Acoustic-Semantic Modeling: Modality Separation and Semantic Coherence for Full-Duplex SLMs (Lychee-FD)** — Zhenyu Liu, Xuanyu Zhang, Yunxin Li et al. (Tencent PCG + Shenzhen Hetao + CUHK-Shenzhen) — [ACL 2026 Outstanding Paper, 2026.acl-long.419](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.419/) — ACL 2026 (Long Papers), April 2026. Diagnoses *why* native full-duplex SLMs get less intelligent: fine-grained optimization-dynamics analysis shows **gradient conflicts between acoustic and semantic modeling** once they share a deep parameter space, plus semantic dilution because ~25 Hz speech forces padding that makes >80% of the text stream meaningless. The fix is **hierarchical parameter separation** — shared shallow trunk, dedicated semantic/acoustic/dialogue-control channels deep, bridged by a dense semantic-alignment ("inner monologue") channel. **Evidence / why it matters:** this is the one media/audio paper in the window with a *top-venue award* (18 outstanding papers from 12,148 ACL submissions) — a strong signal that speech is now a first-class NLP area, not a speech-processing backwater; the authors report **+7.4% on Spoken QA and +28.5% on FullDuplexBench 1.5** without inference cost, and a Tencent engineering blog additionally reports a 2.96x parallel multi-stream inference speedup and 23% lower GPU memory ([Tencent writeup](https://m.163.com/dy/article/L1VV0HR90518R7MO.html)). **Skeptical:** the engineering numbers come from a company blog, not the paper; the "root cause" analysis is the authors' interpretation of their own dynamics.

18. **Tstars-Tryon 1.0: Robust and Realistic Virtual Try-On for Diverse Fashion Items** — Mengting Chen, Zhengrui Chen, Yongchao Du et al., 19 authors — [2604.19748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19748) — model evaluation report, 2026-04-21. A commercial-scale try-on system targeting the failure modes that sink academic VTON — extreme poses, severe illumination variation, motion blur — with emphasis on preserving garment texture, material properties and structure, multi-image composition (up to 6 references) across 8 fashion categories with coordinated identity/background control, and near-real-time inference. **Evidence / why it matters:** at **202 upvotes it is by far the most-upvoted try-on/avatar paper of the year** and represents the shift from single-garment academic benchmarks to robustness/throughput as the real objective; the academic frontier moved in parallel to *interactive* video try-on (iTryOn, ICML 2026, [2605.21431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21431)). **Skeptical:** it is self-described as a "model evaluation report" — "high success rate" is not quantified against a public benchmark in the abstract, and there is no independent evaluation.

## (5) Caveats and what I would not claim

1. **Almost nothing here is peer-reviewed.** The systems papers (Vidu S2, Helios, Seedance 2.0, Cosmos 3, Kling-Omni, MOVA, LTX-2) are preprints/model cards evaluated by their creators. The only top-venue awards in this area I could verify are CVPR 2026 (D4RT, O-Voxel, ChordEdit) and ACL 2026 Outstanding (Lychee-FD). Treat all "SOTA" and "surpasses closed-source" claims as marketing until independently replicated.
2. **Model cards are not benchmarks.** Seedance 2.0, Cosmos 3 and Kling-Omni report expert panels, "public user tests," and vendor leaderboards; none expose a frozen evaluation set. Cosmos 3's rank claim is explicitly "at the time the technical report was written."
3. **Movie-quality ≠ physics.** The strongest, most consistent result in this pass is that perceptual quality and physical/control competence dissociate (Physion-Eval; WorldMark's negative quality–latency correlation; VGI-Bench's 51.0 ceiling; LongAV-Compass's event-fulfillment failures). Any claim that a video model "understands" the world should be tested on WorldMark/VGI-Bench/Physion-Eval-style suites, not VBench.
4. **Small human studies.** Vidu S2's preference shares imply ~7 judgments per baseline; Shao's arena is 10 prompts; LTX-2's AV comparison is internal. Preference percentages from tiny paired studies have very wide confidence intervals and should not be quoted as point estimates.
5. **HF upvotes measure attention, not quality.** The scrape over-represents product/model releases and under-represents theory; upvote totals also drift upward over the year. I used it to order attention, not to adjudicate correctness.
6. **Evaluation-of-evaluation is itself unvalidated.** VGI-Bench's judge is Gemini-3-Flash (0.803 AUC vs humans); LongAV-Compass mixes MLLM and perceptual metrics; WorldMark's human agreement is 85.1% but excludes global geometry and visual quality. These benchmarks are the best available, not ground truth.
7. **I could not verify every venue claim.** Venue attributions (e.g., LiveEdit at ECCV 2026, iTryOn at ICML 2026, World-R1 at ICML 2026, EVA-Bench at EMNLP 2026 Findings) come from the arXiv comments/metadata and were not cross-checked against proceedings. I did check ICLR/ICML/NeurIPS/CVPR official award pages directly.
8. **The Artificial Analysis arena and Sora-shutdown reporting are secondhand.** The "9 of top 10 Chinese" figure and the claim OpenAI shut down Sora in March 2026 come from a TechCrunch-syndicated TNW op-ed, not from the leaderboard itself; I did not fetch the live leaderboard. Cite with care.
9. **Coverage gaps I am aware of:** Chinese-language product blogs and closed systems (Kling, Hailuo, HappyHorse, SkyReels) are under-sampled because I restricted discovery to English search + arXiv/HF; interactive music/dance and motion-capture-driven avatars (e.g., MACE-Dance) got little space; and I did not systematically cover speech translation or speech-to-speech dubbing benchmarks.
10. **Time boundary.** Anything posted after ~2026-10-01 is out of scope; a few IDs in the 2609.xxxxx range were only days old at writing and their citations/reception are unformed.
