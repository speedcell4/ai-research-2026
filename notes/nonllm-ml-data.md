# Non-LLM Machine Learning & Data-Centric ML — Oct 2025 → Oct 2026

*Scope: classical/RL (off-policy, offline, multi-agent, exploration), GNNs & geometric deep learning, time series & dynamical systems, causal inference, recommender systems & IR, tabular data, probabilistic/Bayesian methods, federated learning, privacy-preserving ML, continual learning, meta-learning, and synthetic data / dataset curation / benchmark science. Weighted toward mid-2026.*

*Method note: arXiv's search/API endpoints were heavily rate-limited throughout this work (HTTP 429), so discovery used web search plus direct fetches of arxiv.org/abs and arxiv.org/html once the limits cleared. Quantitative attention signals come from two sources I mined directly: (i) 4,486 Hugging Face Daily Papers entries (2026-01-08 → 2026-10-01) with upvote counts, and (ii) the conference metadata bundles in the shared workspace (19,809 ICLR 2026 OpenReview records and 5,898 NeurIPS 2025 records, including session, status and rating). Fetched content was treated as untrusted data. Every claim below is attributed to a source.*

## 1. Synthesis — what is genuinely happening, and what is hype

**The single most important fact about this area in 2025–26 is that it is no longer where community attention lives.** Of the 227 Hugging Face Daily Papers with at least 100 upvotes between January and October 2026, only about 15% are non-LLM papers by title/summary heuristics, and of the 58 papers above 200 upvotes only ~7 look non-LLM ([data: HF Daily Papers upvote corpus, `data/hf_ranked.json`]). ICLR 2026 accepted 831 foundation/frontier-model papers versus 308 in the dedicated reinforcement-learning area, 113 in "learning on graphs and other geometries & topologies", 101 in "learning on time series and dynamical systems", 116 in probabilistic methods and just 47 in causal reasoning ([ICLR 2026 OpenReview metadata](https://openreview.net/group?id=ICLR.cc/2026/Conference)). The non-LLM subfields are not dying — several are actually accelerating — but they now sit outside the field's loud center, so their progress is comparatively under-noticed. The exception that proves the rule is tabular learning: **LimiX-2** accumulated 813 HF upvotes, the highest of any non-LLM paper in the window and higher than almost every contemporaneous agent paper ([2609.17488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17488)).

**Genuine shifts.** First and clearest: **tabular foundation models became a real, contested product category.** The 2024–25 PFN line (TabPFN) has spawned TabPFN-3, TabICLv2, TabDPT, Mitra-v2, EXAONE-Tabular, Xiaomi-TabLDM, TabFM and LimiX-2, all trained predominantly on synthetic tables generated from structural causal models and all evaluated zero-shot or in-context ([LimiX-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17488); [TabFM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37959)). The numbers are no longer marginal: TabFM reports TabArena regression Elo 2055.2, ahead of EXAONE-Tabular (1973.1), TabPFN-3 (1866.6), AutoGluon 1.5-extreme (1851.2) and TabICLv2 (1723.6), and LimiX-2 reports overall TabArena Elo 1935, +117.4 over the runner-up. Crucially, the field also produced its own strongest counter-evidence in the same window: **BeyondArena** shows that these models dominate only tiny/small IID data and are beaten by plain GBDTs and tuned deep models on non-IID (temporal/grouped), large, high-dimensional and high-cardinality data ([2606.30410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30410)). Second: **time-series foundation models consolidated.** Chronos-2 adds group attention to handle multivariate and covariate-informed forecasting zero-shot and claims state of the art on fev-bench, GIFT-Eval and Chronos Benchmark II, ahead of TiRex and TimesFM-2.5 ([2510.15821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15821)); Moirai 2.0 simplifies to a decoder-only quantile model trained on 36M series ([2511.11698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11698)); and the subfield now has both a post-training framework survey ([2607.20002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20002)) and a probabilistic-forecasting survey ([2609.13345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13345)). Third: **geometric deep learning keeps delivering real artifacts** — EquiformerV3 sets state of the art on OC20, OMat24 and Matbench Discovery alongside eSEN and Meta's UMA ([2604.09130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09130)) — and CVPR 2026's two top papers (D4RT dynamic-scene reconstruction; O-Voxel structured 3D latents) are both geometry-native. Fourth: **data-centric ML quietly became a top-three theme.** Exact theory for when curating less data helps ([Dohmatob et al., ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/5af28a311a6ae8b30ab4941a96675fa2-Abstract-Conference.html)), web-graph-structural data selection ([2606.11499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11499)), model-collapse mechanics ([2509.16499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16499)), synthetic-tabular fidelity benchmarks ([TabStruct, ICLR 2026 Oral](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11950)) and benchmark-contamination meta-science all grew. Fifth: in classical RL the notable results are about **scaling depth and data regime** rather than new algorithms — the NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper went to 1024-layer self-supervised RL ([2503.14858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14858)), ICLR 2026 gave an Oral to TD-JEPA for zero-shot RL ([2510.00739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00739)), and ICML 2026's Test of Time Award went to A3C ([ICML 2026 awards](https://blog.icml.cc/2026/07/05/announcing-the-icml-2026-awards/)).**

**Real vs. hype.** Real: tabular PFNs as a competitive default on small IID tables; multivariate/covariate-zero-shot forecasting with published, reusable checkpoints; equivariant atomistic models at leaderboard-topping accuracy; data-selection and curation signals that give reproducible gains at fixed compute; unlearning/DP theory that finally bites in the high-dimensional regime. Hype or over-claimed: (1) **"tabular foundation models beat everything"** — BeyondArena shows the wins are concentrated exactly where the benchmarks are easy; (2) **"time-series foundation models understand physics"** — the *Context Parroting* study shows leading TSFMs often just copy from context and a naive parroting baseline beats them on chaos, turbulence and ECG ([2505.11349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11349)), while another paper shows their priors cannot represent noiseless mechanisms or physical units (["Do Tabular Foundation Models Know Physics?", 2609.02766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02766)); (3) **"graph foundation models"** — still mostly survey-and-aspiration (see [2505.15116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15116)), with the concrete 2026 wins being architectural (ChebNet revival, E2Former-V2) rather than foundation-scale; (4) **"federated learning at scale"** — accepted-paper volume is modest (ICLR 2026: 57 federated papers) and the 2026 survey literature explicitly frames deployment gaps ([Unfederated, Springer 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11831-026-10696-3)), with a parallel critique that poisoning risk is systematically overestimated in healthcare federated AI; (5) **single-benchmark causal claims** — CausalPFN/Do-PFN assume ignorability and are trained on simulator families, so their "superior average performance" is a prior-quality statement as much as a causal-discovery result.

## 2. Ranked papers (18 most important, by evidence-weighted importance)

1. **LimiX-2: A Contextual Mechanism Network Towards General Structured-Data Intelligence** — Xingxuan Zhang et al. (LimiX Team / Stable AI) — [2609.17488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17488) — arXiv preprint, Sep 2026; 813 HF upvotes (top non-LLM paper of the window). Replaces the target-centric PFN objective `p(y|x,context)` with mechanism-oriented joint modeling `p(x,y|context)` via cell-level Contextual Mechanism Networks pretrained with Context-Conditional Masked Modeling on SCM-synthesized tables. **Evidence:** TabArena overall Elo **1935** (+117.4 over TabFM+), average rank 5.5 vs 9.0, 18.9 aggregated wins vs 5.3; classification Elo 1917 (94.5% pairwise win rate), regression Elo 2206 (96.9%); it also beats dedicated causal-discovery baselines on skeleton recovery, and beats TabFM with ~4x fewer parameters. **Read in full.**

2. **Chronos-2: From Univariate to Universal Forecasting** — Abdul Fatir Ansari et al. (Amazon) — [2510.15821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15821) — arXiv preprint, Oct 2025. A pretrained forecaster with a *group attention* mechanism that shares information across related series within a group (variates of a multivariate series, or targets and covariates), trained on synthetic multivariate structures so that one model handles univariate, multivariate and covariate-informed tasks zero-shot. **Evidence:** reports state-of-the-art win-rate and skill-score on fev-bench, GIFT-Eval and Chronos Benchmark II, beating TiRex and TimesFM-2.5 by statistically significant margins (pairwise confidence intervals exclude 50%/0%), with the largest gains on the covariate subset. **Caveat noted by the authors:** the pretraining corpus overlaps GIFT-Eval *training* portions, so strictly zero-shot numbers come only from a synthetic-only ablation. **Read in full.**

3. **1000 Layer Networks for Self-Supervised RL: Scaling Depth Can Enable New Goal-Reaching Capabilities** — Kevin Wang, Ishaan Javali, Michał Bortkiewicz, Tomasz Trzciński, Benjamin Eysenbach — [2503.14858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14858) — **NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper** (Dec 2025). In unsupervised goal-conditioned RL (no rewards, no demonstrations) it scales network depth from the 2–5 layers typical of RL to 1024 layers, finding large success-rate gains and qualitatively new behaviors, with batch-size scaling and contrastive objectives as the key enablers. **Why it matters:** directly attacks the conventional belief that RL's weak learning signal cannot train very deep networks, and reframes "RL needs shallow nets" as an artifact of architecture/optimization choices.

4. **TabFM: A Zero-Shot Foundation Model for Tabular Data** — Weihao Kong et al. (Google Research) — [2609.37959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37959) — arXiv preprint, Sep 2026. A 400M-parameter in-context tabular model with learned Fourier cell embeddings, alternating linear-time column ISAB and row-wise SAB with RoPE, CLS pooling and a 24-layer predictor, pretrained entirely on SCM-synthetic tables and scaling context to 16,384 instances. **Evidence:** TabArena regression Elo **2055.2** (EXAONE-Tabular 1973.1, TabPFN-3 1866.6, AutoGluon 1.5-extreme 1851.2, TabICLv2 1723.6) and classification Elo 1768.6, with the lowest geometric-mean error on both tracks; TabFM+ and TabFM-Auto (Gemini-3.8-Flash feature engineering) take the top two overall ranks without weight updates. **Read in full.**

5. **Beyond IID: How General Are Tabular Foundation Models, Really? (BeyondArena)** — Lennart Purucker, Andrej Tschalzev, Nick Erickson, Gioia Blayer, David Holzmüller, Alan Arazi, Alexander Pfefferle, Mustafa Tajjar, Gaël Varoquaux, Frank Hutter — [2606.30410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30410) — arXiv preprint, Jun 2026. The first unified tabular benchmark spanning IID, temporal and grouped task types, 100 → 1M samples, dimensionality scales, and high-cardinality/text features, with proper outer test splits for grouped data. **Evidence:** current TFMs dominate tiny/small IID data but *lose* to traditional tree-based and deep models on non-IID, large, high-dimensional and high-cardinality datasets. **Why it matters:** the single most important counterweight to the tabular-FM narrative; it shows benchmark design, not capability, drives many apparent wins. **Read in full.**

6. **EquiformerV3: Scaling Efficient, Expressive, and General SE(3)-Equivariant Graph Attention Transformers** — Yi-Lun Liao, Alexander J. Hoffman, Sabrina C. Shen, Alexandre Duval, Sam Walton Norwood, Tess Smidt (MIT) — [2604.09130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09130) — arXiv preprint, Apr 2026. Third-generation SE(3)-equivariant graph attention: a fused/compiled implementation (1.75x speedup), equivariant merged layer norm, smooth radius cutoff, and SwiGLU-S² activations that add many-body interactions for better expressivity and smoother potential-energy surfaces. **Evidence:** state of the art on OC20, OMat24 and Matbench Discovery when trained with denoising of non-equilibrium structures; generalizes to energy-conserving simulations and higher-order derivatives. **Context:** part of a strong 2026 MLIP field with eSEN and UMA. **Read in full.**

7. **TD-JEPA: Latent-predictive Representations for Zero-Shot Reinforcement Learning** — Marco Bagatella, Matteo Pirotta, Ahmed Touati, Alessandro Lazaric, Andrea Tirinzoni — [2510.00739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00739) — **ICLR 2026 Oral** (rating 7.5, highest-rated RL oral). Uses temporal-difference learning to make latent-predictive representations that capture long-term latent dynamics across multiple policies, learned from offline, reward-free transitions rather than single-task or on-policy data. **Why it matters:** strengthens the JEPA-style "predict your own latents" paradigm as a general zero-shot RL representation learner and is the clearest RL Oral of ICLR 2026.

8. **CausalPFN: Amortized Causal Effect Estimation via In-Context Learning** — Vahid Balazadeh, Hamidreza Kamkari, Valentin Thomas, Benson Li, Junwei Ma, Jesse C. Cresswell, Rahul G. Krishnan — [2506.07918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07918) — **NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight**. One transformer, trained once on a large library of simulated data-generating processes satisfying ignorability, maps raw observational datasets directly to causal effects with no task-specific tuning. **Evidence:** superior average performance across standard causal-effect benchmarks versus dozens of specialized estimators; part of a clear 2026 wave including [Do-PFN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06039) (NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight) and "Foundation Models for Causal Inference via Prior-Data Fitted Networks" ([ICLR 2026](https://openreview.net/forum?id=d2L1ndOKjq)).

9. **Distributional Equivalence in Linear Non-Gaussian Latent-Variable Cyclic Causal Models: Characterization and Learning** — Haoyue Dai, Immanuel Albrecht, Peter Spirtes, Kun Zhang — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=b8TlYh6PN6) / [ICLR 2026 poster](https://www.iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10008640) — **ICLR 2026 Oral** (rating 8, highest-rated causal paper). Characterizes when two linear non-Gaussian cyclic causal models with latent variables are distributionally equivalent, then uses that characterization for learning. **Why it matters:** relaxes the acyclicity/faithfulness assumptions that most causal-discovery theory relies on and gives a learnability result where prior methods had none.

10. **A Closer Look at Model Collapse: From a Generalization-to-Memorization Perspective** — Lianghe Shi, Meng Wu, Huijie Zhang, Zekai Zhang, Molei Tao, Qing Qu — [2509.16499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16499) — **NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight**. Recasts model collapse in diffusion models as a transition from generalization to memorization driven by the declining entropy of recursively generated training data, rather than simple variance shrinkage or distribution shift. **Why it matters:** gives the synthetic-data feedback loop a mechanistic, measurable failure mode and connects it to practical memorization.

11. **Why Less is More (Sometimes): A Theory of Data Curation** — Elvis Dohmatob, Mohammad Pezeshki, Reyhane Askari Hemmat — [ICLR 2026 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/5af28a311a6ae8b30ab4941a96675fa2-Abstract-Conference.html) — **ICLR 2026 Poster** (rating 7.5). Derives exact scaling-law curves for test error when an imperfect oracle selects training examples by difficulty and correctness, under both label-agnostic and label-aware curation rules. **Why it matters:** provides a principled account of when the "less is more" / LIMO-style aggressive-curation recipes beat the classical "more is more" scaling laws — one of the clearest theory contributions to data-centric ML this year.

12. **Hubs or Fringes: Pretraining Data Selection via Web Graph Centrality (WebGraphMix)** — Vedant Badoni, Danqi Chen, Xinyi Wang (Princeton) — [2606.11499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11499) — arXiv preprint, Jun 2026. Scores documents by betweenness/Katz centrality on the Common Crawl host graph (13.9M nodes, 439.6M edges; <9 GPU-hours, no labels or model training), then mixes structurally central and peripheral content into the DCLM pipeline. **Evidence:** a 1:1 central:peripheral mix reaches **41.4%** vs **39.8%** for uniform sampling on DCLM CORE v2 (23 tasks) at 1B params; combining centrality with the DCLM-fasttext quality classifier reaches **43.8%**; central hosts help symbolic/algorithmic reasoning (+1.4%) while peripheral hosts help factual and commonsense knowledge. **Read in full.**

13. **TabStruct: Measuring Structural Fidelity of Tabular Data** — Xiangjian Jiang, Nikola Simidjievski, Mateja Jamnik — [2509.11950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11950) — **ICLR 2026 Oral** (rating 7). Builds a benchmark that measures whether synthetic tabular generators preserve the *causal structure* of real data, and extends structural-fidelity evaluation beyond the toy datasets that require known ground-truth graphs. **Why it matters:** as synthetic tables become the default pretraining substrate for tabular FMs, structural fidelity becomes the evaluation axis that accuracy alone hides.

14. **Non-Convex Federated Optimization under Cost-Aware Client Selection** — Xiaowen Jiang, Anton Rodomanov, Sebastian U. Stich — [2512.05327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05327) — **ICLR 2026 Oral** (rating 5.5). Introduces an optimization model that explicitly quantifies communication *and* local computation and assigns costs to concrete client-selection strategies (random subset, periodic full participation, hybrids), then derives complexity guarantees for non-convex objectives. **Why it matters:** one of the few 2026 federated results that changes the theory rather than adding another averaging variant, in a subfield that is otherwise consolidating.

15. **Gaussian Certified Unlearning in High Dimensions: A Hypothesis Testing Approach** — Aaradhya Pandey, Arnab Auddy, Haolin Zou, Arian Maleki, Sanjeev Kulkarni — [2510.13094](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13094) — **ICLR 2026 Oral** (rating 6). Defines ε-Gaussian certifiability for the proportional p ~ n regime where the standard Ω(1)-strong-convexity / O(1)-smoothness assumptions fail, and analyzes one-step-Newton unlearning under it. **Why it matters:** closed-form high-dimensional unlearning guarantees were a conspicuous gap; this paper and the parallel NP-hardness/certification line make privacy guarantees credible outside the low-dimensional easy case. Related: "How Well Can Differential Privacy Be Audited in One Run?" (NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight) and the [Hubble memorization suite](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19811).

16. **WarpSAC: Towards the Pinnacle of Scalable Off-policy RL by Rethinking Exploration and Exploitation** — Zihao Wu, Hongyao Tang, Yi Ma, Huizhong Song, Pengyi Li, Yifu Yuan, Fei Ni, Jinyi Liu, Wei Wei, Jianrong Wang, Yan Zheng, Jianye Hao — [2608.24479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24479) — arXiv preprint, Aug 2026. Through controlled experiments across eight benchmark families it shows popular off-policy stabilizers are *data-regime dependent* — parameter normalization helps under narrow replay coverage but restricts value fitting when data are abundant, and clipped double-Q can be relaxed in high-throughput manipulation — then proposes the regime-aware WarpSAC family with Sample Weight Decay. **Why it matters:** massively parallel simulation has changed the data regime, and most published off-policy tricks were never re-validated for it.

17. **Scaling Recommender Transformers to One Billion Parameters** — Kirill Khrylchenko, Artem Matveev, Sergei Makeev, Vladimir Baikalov — [2507.15994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15994) — **KDD 2026** (arXiv Jul 2025, revised Feb 2026). Scales the generative-recommender / HSTU-style sequential transduction architecture from the previously reported ~176M parameters to 1B, quantifying compute-scaling behavior for recommendation. **Why it matters:** recommendation is the largest non-LLM industrial ML workload, and this is its clearest "scaling laws are real here too" result. Related 2026 work: TransRetrieval, which scales transformer retrieval to a 40-billion-interaction industrial corpus ([2608.25528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25528)), and scaling laws for reranking ([2603.04816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04816)).

18. **Context Parroting: A Simple but Tough-to-Beat Baseline for Foundation Models in Scientific Machine Learning** — Yuanzhao Zhang, William Gilpin — [2505.11349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11349) — **ICLR 2026 Poster** (rating 7). Shows that leading time-series foundation models frequently forecast by copying values straight from the context, and that when they are not parroting they exhibit shared failure modes such as converging to the mean; a trivial parroting baseline outscores them on low-dimensional chaos, turbulence, coupled oscillators and electrocardiograms. **Why it matters:** it is the sharpest published challenge to zero-shot "foundation models for physics" claims and should be read alongside Chronos-2.

## 5. Caveats

- **Attention metrics are proxies, not quality.** HF upvotes measure community enthusiasm and are strongly biased toward LLMs and demos; ICLR/NeurIPS "session" counts come from OpenReview primary areas and are noisy (e.g. "graph" matches GraphRAG; "retrieval" matches RAG). I report them as relative signals only.
- **I could not run an arXiv volume scan.** The arXiv search/API endpoints returned HTTP 429 for most of the session (the parent job was itself using the API), and OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar were also rate-limited or out of budget. My coverage therefore leans on conference metadata and targeted search; it is broad but not exhaustive, and a genuinely important paper that never appeared on HF Daily Papers or in an ICLR/NeurIPS/ICML main track could be missing.
- **Many flagship 2026 models have no independent replication.** LimiX-2, TabFM, Chronos-2 and EquiformerV3 numbers are the authors' own; BeyondArena (rank 5) is the main independent check and it contradicts the tabular-FM line on non-IID data. LimiX-2's causal-skeleton claim in particular is a single-paper result.
- **Chronos-2's headline zero-shot claim is qualified by the authors themselves** (pretraining corpus overlaps GIFT-Eval training portions); only the synthetic-only ablation is strictly zero-shot. Do not repeat the unqualified SOTA claim.
- **Causal foundation models inherit strong assumptions.** CausalPFN/Do-PFN assume ignorability and are trained on simulator families; "superior average performance" mostly reflects prior coverage, not identification in the wild.
- **The rank-9 causal result is theory under linear, non-Gaussian, cyclic, latent-variable assumptions**; it is a genuine identifiability advance but says nothing about non-linear real-world discovery.
- **Model-collapse and data-curation results are often small-scale or synthetic** (diffusion models, DCLM at 400M/1B, synthetic tables); transfer to frontier-scale training is an extrapolation.
- **Federated-learning "cooling" is a relative statement.** The absolute number of federated papers remains large (57 at ICLR 2026 alone); what has changed is that the field has fewer headline results and more self-critique.
- **I did not verify every benchmark number by re-running code**, and several quoted Elo differences are computed from bootstrap point estimates rather than the underlying per-dataset tables.
